## ★★ PACE — and why the old numbers are half the new ones

☠ **The `TQ` pace table below was written for a man pulling a hand cart. There is a donkey team now, and it changes every travel estimate on this map.**

| Mode | Sustained | Day |
|---|---|---|
| **Porter / hand column** *(53–81 kg)* | **2.5–4.2 km/h** by `TQ` | ~25–30 km |
| ★★★ **Donkey team, wagon, mature road** | **~6 km/h** | ★★★ **~65 km** on a long summer day |
| **Fast string, listening only** | — | ~92 km on mature `L0`–`L9` · **~40 km** through the Issus squeeze |

> ★★ **PLAYER STANDARD: Belen in ONE day, Erzin or Dörtyol in TWO.** *That is ~65 km/day, which is a long day and an honest one for a team on `TQ ~3` road in summer.*

⚠ **It does not hold in winter, on a frontier leg, or with trail work in the plan.** **A build lap is not a travel lap** — a day that stops to grade, blast or listen covers half the ground or less.

**Hop time** between nodes ≈ `km ÷ pace` **+ crossing minutes** (ford ~15 min · bridge ~25 min).

## ★★★ LONGEVITY — how fast a leg falls back, and when to look at it

> ☠ ★★★ **THE SLOPE IS THE CLOCK.** *A flat crowned plain leg barely moves in five years. A pass leg with cross-drains loses its surface every wet winter — and the drains are the cause, not a separate item: they silt, the water goes onto the running surface instead of across it, and it takes the fines with it.*

| Leg character | Loses | ⧗ **Re-read every** | What fails first |
|---|---|---|---|
| ★ **Flat plain, crowned, gravelled** | ~0.05 `TQ`/yr | **5 yr** | Brush on the fringe. Cosmetic |
| **Rolling, gravelled** | ~0.1 `TQ`/yr | **3 yr** | Rut at the wheel line |
| ⚠ **Terrace and side-slope** | ~0.2 `TQ`/yr | **2 yr** | Edge creep · slump narrowing the running width |
| ☠ **Pass, steep, cross-drained** | ~0.3 `TQ`/yr | ★★★ **1 yr** | **Silted cross-drains → fines washed out of the steep sections** |

### Crossings last far longer than legs

| Tier | Longevity | Cycle |
|---|---|---|
| ★ **Concrete abutment + pozzolan wing** | **Decade+** | **Listen-only** most years · scour cheek when the flood says so |
| **Reinforced wood, `BQ ~3.5`** | **Decade class** abutments | Redeck or touch on the deck cycle |
| **Live wood, early** | **~5–8 yr to redeck** | **Touch the aprons** every lap |
| ☠ **Ford, `FQ GO`** | **Seasonal** | ★★ **Re-read every spring.** A ford is a promise the river renews or withdraws each year |
| ☠ **Any pier in a live channel** | ⚠ **The scour clock runs whatever the timber says** | **Read the BED, not the deck** — see the `CAMPUS-BRIDGE` block |

> ☠ ★★★ **A CROSSING AND ITS LEG DECAY ON DIFFERENT CLOCKS AND ARE INSPECTED ON THE SAME VISIT, WHICH IS WHY THE LEG GETS MISSED.** *The bridge is the object you came to see; the seven kilometres you walked to reach it are not.*

### ☠ ★★★ ARREST and RECOVER are two jobs — never file them as one *(d3293)*

| | ★ **ARREST** | ☠ **RECOVER** |
|---|---|---|
| **Work** | Clear silted drains · re-cut crown · clear fringe | Replace lost surface · rebuild washed-out outlets |
| **Cargo** | ✓ **Tools only** | **Gravel, stone, bar train** |
| **Cost** | ★ **A day, one person, any time** | **A loaded BUILD lap, planned** |
| **Buys** | ★★ **Stops the NEXT interval of loss** | **Returns the grade already lost** |

> ☠ ★★★ **`L6` SAT AT `TQ ~2.25` FOR TWO YEARS BECAUSE "THE LEG NEEDS WORK" WAS ONE LINE ON THE SLATE.** *It was always two items with different costs and different cargo.* ★★★ **Welding a cheap job to an expensive one means the CHEAP HALF NEVER GETS DONE EITHER — because the slate only ever shows you the price of the pair.**

★ **Silted and washed-out drains look identical from ten metres and are not the same problem.** **A silted drain is full — labour. A washed-out drain is gone — material.**

⚑ **`READ-LAP-FIRST`:** any leg past its interval gets a **tools-only** lap before material is loaded. *A heavy wagon buys a guess, executed.*

### ★★★ `DRAIN-READ` — a second axis, not part of `TQ` *(d3292)*

> ★★★ **`TQ` GRADES THE RUNNING SURFACE. RAIN GRADES THE WATER PATH. A leg can be rough and dry or smooth and drowning, and one number cannot say which.** ☠ *`L4` carries the second-worst number on the west road and sheds better than legs graded above it.*

| `DRAIN-READ` | Means |
|---|---|
| ✓ **SHED** | Water leaves the running surface. Ditches carrying |
| ⚠ **POOL** | Standing water, no channel cut yet. **Cheap to fix** |
| ☠ **CHANNEL** | ★★★ **Water is running ALONG the road, in the ruts.** *The road has become the drain* |
| **—** | Never read wet |

> ★★ **GO IN THE RAIN ON PURPOSE.** *A dry ditch tells you nothing; a wet one shows you exactly where the water has decided to go instead.* ★★★ **Water is a free surveyor and it grades every metre you walk past, not only the leg you came for.**

**Upgrade ladder:** `LISTEN` → `TOUCH` → `REINFORCE` → `DEPLOY` → `REBUILD`. Escalate one tier only when the tier below fails audit. Full doctrine: [trail-longevity.md](../../government/procedures/trail-longevity.md).

**How `TQ` climbs:** each maintenance block files **+1 work point**, and **+4 WP = +0.5 `TQ`**, capped at 5. ★ **Gains are slow by design** — which is why mature legs sit near 3 and not near 5, and why a leg allowed to decay is expensive to recover.

### ☠ ★★★ `TQ` IS PROVISIONAL — IT WAS NEVER A MEASUREMENT *(d3307)*

> ☠ ★★★ **`TQ` HAS ALWAYS BEEN MY OPINION OF MY OWN WORK, WRITTEN DOWN AS A NUMBER SO THAT IT WOULD LOOK LIKE A MEASUREMENT.** *I grade the road. I also built the road. I grade it immediately after building it, while pleased with myself.* ★★ **The d3279 datum rule applies exactly: a measurement inherits every fault of whatever it was compared against** — ☠ **and `TQ` was compared against nothing at all.**

★★★ **`ROLL-TEST` replaces the judgement with an observable.** *A road's quality is how hard it is to pull a load along it — that is the entire content of the word.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Method** | **Release a loaded cart from rest off a fixed ramp; count paces to a stop on a marked strip** |
| **Ramp** | ✓ **Short timber ramp carried on the cart. Same ramp, same height, always** — *so launch energy is identical and cancels* |
| **Load** | ✓ **Measured stone ballast. The same stones** |
| ☠ **Rule** | ⚠ **Same cart, same wheels, same greasing, same day where possible** — ★★ *the point is comparing legs to each other, not measuring an absolute* |
| **Strips** | ✓ **40 paces, cairned both ends, marked on `L0`–`L4` at d3307** |

☠ ★★ **Every `TQ` in this register is PROVISIONAL until the first full `ROLL-TEST` set.** ⚠ *The deficit ranking below is still believed, but it was drawn in units that were never checked.*

### ☠ ★★★ THE FAILURES ARE AT THE JOINTS, AND NO ROW OWNS A JOINT *(d3306)*

> ☠ ★★★ **EVERY LEG ON THIS ROAD READS `SHED` AND THE ROAD IS STILL FAILING, BECAUSE A LEG CAN ONLY EVER SHED ONTO ANOTHER LEG.** *Every soft spot found on `L3` was at a boundary — cut to fill, road to abutment, track junction, one season's work meeting the next.*

> ★★ **I split the road into legs because legs are how you walk it and how you schedule it.** ☠ **Water does not know where my legs end.**

## `BRIDGE-Q` — crossing friction

`BQ` grades **loaded crossing friction** — deck stiffness, scour, approach apron, listen in millimetres. ★ **It is not march speed**, and it does not belong on a leg.

| `BQ` | Label | Loaded listen | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Scout** | Not crossed loaded | Ghost site · reserve queued |
| **1** | **Ford GO** | N/A · boot or knee wade | Summer and low water · **re-read every spring** |
| **2** | **Live wood** | ~4–5 mm | Early oak deck · deployed, not reinforced |
| **3** | **Mature wood** | ~3 mm | Repeat lap · apron dressed |
| **3.5** | **Pass** | ~2–3 mm | Reinforced, or concrete abutment |
| **4** | **Haul** | ≤~2 mm | Post-reinforce |
| **5** | **Road** | ≤~1 mm | Multi-reinforce · ideal apron. Rare, and a target rather than a hero |

**Fords have no `BQ`.** They carry **`FQ`** — **GO · AMBER · RED**, nothing finer. Where a ford has concrete shore work, the shore takes a `BQ` and the ford lip still takes an `FQ`.
