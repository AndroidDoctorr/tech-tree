## Rules that apply to every harvest

- **Elite never mixes.** Separate jar, separate tag, `Yn` suffix. Thresh elite last, on clean cloth. Roughly 10% of a crop as the elite draw.
- **Save from the best plants, never the field average**, and never from the tail of a harvest unless tagged TRIAL.
- **Rogue before you cut, not after** — lodging, mould, early bolt, shatter, bitterness, weak stand, at the green line.
- **One observation row per crop per season**, even when the note is "no change". [crop-selection-improvement-code-1.md](../regulations/crop-selection-improvement-code-1.md)
- **A wild stand is selected for margin hardiness**, not bed uniformity. Do not apply field standards to P-series patches.
- **Flag ripe perennials inline on the farm pass** — `PERENNIAL-EYES` in [periodic.md](../../checklists/periodic.md).

## Grain

### `GRAIN-GATHER-WILD`

Two-stage, and the first stage is six months before the harvest.

- **Mark the stands in March, while they are green.** `P-06-A/B/C` barley, `P-05-A/B` einkorn, P-04 wild emmer, ~200 m on the plain. In June the stand is indistinguishable from everything around it.
- Wild heads **shatter as they ripen**. Cut **early and repeatedly** across the band; a stand visited once on what looks like the right day is a stand mostly on the ground.
- Select plump heads, six-row fat barley. Cull musty.
- Winnow in the breeze at the plain stake, chaff downwind.
- Wild jars vault **separately** from elite field seed: `GRAIN-WILD-A1` emmer, `GRAIN-WILD-A3` barley.
- P-06 barley bank stands at zero — treat the first lap as seed, not food.

### `EMMER-HARVEST` · `PULSE-HARVEST`

Emmer day one, pulse day two. Do not combine — threshing floor and drying racks collide.

- **Gate:** `EMMER-YELLOW-GO-READ` — heads hard-dry, **snap ×3 PASS**. Pulse gate is dry straw, pod rattle, hard kernel.
- Stubble ~8–10 cm. `IRON-SICKLE-1` hero-sharp, `COLLECT-BAG-7`, `BASKET-HARVEST`, team and `WHEEL-CART-1`.
- Sheaf-bind with M-08 → `SHEAF-RACK-1`.
- Thresh and winnow leeward at `THRESH-ZONE-1` / `WINNOW-SHEET-1`, chaff off the kitchen path.
- Elite threshed last: `EMMER-ELITE-Yn`, `P-17-ELITE-Yn`.

## Fibre

### `FLAX-PULL-WILD` · `FLAX-PULL-FIELD`

**Pull it. Do not cut it.** Flax is pulled in handfuls, roots and all, and the roots are shaken free of soil before binding.

Two reasons, and both are about the fibre rather than the plant. The fibre bundles run the **whole length of the stem, below ground level included**, so cutting throws away several centimetres of the longest line there is. And a cut butt is ragged: it shreds at the heckle and turns line into tow. A pulled stem has a clean tapered end.

*Earlier laps were cut at the base. That is why line yields have run a little short of what the green weight suggested — the loss was at the butt, not in the retting.*

- **Wild gate:** snap ×4 — stalks snap before they fully bend. **Field gate:** snap ×3 — stalks tan, shoulder yellow, lower straw dry.
- Shake seed off before retting: ~10 g from the wild patch back to the patch ghost and the vault; ~11 g elite class from the best field stalks.
- Bind with M-08 root-end aligned, so the butts stay together through retting and breaking.
- Straight to `FLAX-RETT` on the same pass if the pool is clear; ~10–14 day rett clock starts at pool load.
- Dense stand gives long fibre, thin stand gives seed. Where both are wanted, split the bed rather than compromise.
- The June wild lap (ditch W, T-1 lip, `FLAX-PATCH-1`) is a **second** annual fibre source, separate from the Bed B field pull after grain.

### `HEMP-CUT` — fibre

**Hemp is cut, flax is pulled.** Not an inconsistency: hemp is tall and woody at the base, the bottom of the stem is coarse fibre nobody wants, and a hemp root will not come out of the ground by hand anyway.

- Wild band: cut **at flowering**, and take the males immediately after they shed pollen — that is the finest fibre in the stand and they die back regardless, so taking them first costs nothing.
- Bed A north: cut before the seed goes hard. Gate is dry leaf and a brittle stalk snap.
- CU-KNIFE at the base, M-08 sheaf bind, hang at the **W-1 north rafter**.
- Shake seed off the cut → `HEMP-SEL-Yn` vault tag.
- Local and campus-margin stands only. Fibre is bulk; it is not worth road miles.

### `HEMP-SEED-STRIP` — Ghab run

A **genetics** run, not a fibre run. Seed is light, dense, and the only part that cannot be grown at home.

- Females hold seed ~5–6 weeks after pollination.
- Dead heads first: strip crowns, knife-split, knock to tarp. Then a **×4 palm sweep grid** of the ground — sweeping, not digging.
- Seed ID: grey-brown mottled, round-oval, hard. Not acorn, not dock.
- Winnow by lip blow; cull anything soft or mouldy.
- Strip seed first, then cut female stalks for coarse fibre on the same pass — but do not plan the trip around the fibre.
- Bed A north has been closed since Y6, from one 45 g drill and four grow-outs, and hemp is an obligate outcrosser, so the imported line is worth protecting.
- **Isolate by TIME, not by distance.** Hemp is wind-pollinated and the pollen carries for kilometres — there is no corner of the campus far enough away, and a plot "over the hill" is a plot that crossed. Stagger the sowings so the two stands **flower weeks apart**, or accept that they merge.
- Band closes against the 27 Sep expedition gate. Go early in the band or do not go.

## Orchard and vine

### `GRAPE-SHOOT-THIN` — budbreak

Doubles, blind shoots, suckers, interior growth. **Snap them green with a thumb** — secateurs are for dormant wood.

**Keep the renewal shoot on any winter-killed cordon.** Tow-tie it; do not thin it off. That shoot is the replacement arm.

This summer's canopy shade sets **next** spring's fruitful buds, which is why the job cannot slide into the fruit year. Roughly 12–15 leaves ripens one cluster.

### `GRAPE-CLUSTER-THIN` — fruit set

- Drop ~20–30% of green bunches once berries have **set**, before they swell.
- Best at veraison, when the keepers show colour and the choice is obvious.
- Do not overthin. This sun burns exposed fruit and the canopy is the sunscreen.

### `GRAPE-PICK`

Partial strips are fine across the whole band — south and mid cordon first, upper green left for a later lap. Stem-pick before crushing. Split at the crush into eat-now, must, and hold. `P-03-SEL-Yn` from the sweetest clusters on the tagged vine only.

### `FIG-PICK`

Bed D `FIG-C1`–`C4` is primary and **not missable**. Hand-pick ripe only; green holds fine on the tree, so lap it. `C2` has led on sweetness since Y8 — propagate from the winner, not the nearest branch. Wild P-12 at ~650 m is an optional extra lap and is skipped when Bed D covers the need.

### `OLIVE-PICK`

- Hand strip a blush-black mix into `COLLECT-BAG-1` and basket, ladder for the height. About four picks per season, target ~95% stripped.
- **Table and press are different grades.** Tail and high-twig stragglers go to the brine crock, not to press staging.
- The December close is a stray lap only: singles and holdouts, hand, no ladder hero.
- `P-02-SEL-n` elite fruit drawn before anything reaches the press.

### `PISTACHIO-PICK`

- **Primary gate: hull split at ~80%.** Below that, come back.
- Pole tap, hand pick, sweep the fallen. **No green take.**
- Reject worm and mould, ~4% class.
- Dry **hull-on** ~10–14 days at the porch mat before shelling. Shell gate is a firm hull snap.
- Largest nuts to the seed bank before the rest is hulled.
- The ~20 Nov – 1 Dec band is **ground recovery after a missed primary**, not a second harvest — expect ~40% discard.

## Herbs

### `HERB-SEED-DESIGNATE` — Mar–Apr

This band designates plants. It does not cut seed; ripe seed is May–June.

- **Leaf or seed, never both.** Stake the chosen plants and take them off the kitchen rotation the day you choose them.
- Select for **vigour and late bolting**. Never for size, and **never save from the first bolter**.
- Allium: leave ×4 scapes to run. Snapping scapes is a kitchen job and it stops bulb growth — do not do it to a designated plant.
- Parsley is biennial: leave ×3 second-year plants standing.
- Bigger beds are the real lever. A bed of six plants is a closed population of six, and selecting out of the minimum that satisfies the kitchen is how a line drifts.

### `HERB-SEED-CUT` — June

Coriander **shatters**. Cut whole stalks while brown and dry them head-down in a bag; the bag is the harvest. Allium, thyme and parsley are gentler but follow the same pattern.

### `HERB-LAYER` · `MINT-DIVIDE`

- Rosemary and thyme from seed are slow, poor and do not come true. **Layer them:** nick the underside of a branch, pin it down, cover.
- Mint is **divided** at the root — take the vigorous outer pieces. Keep it in `MINT-CROCK-1` or it takes the bed.
- Neither belongs in the seed programme.

### `HERB-LEAF-PICK`

Kitchen picks, from non-designated plants only. Mint: cut the **upper third**, never scalp it. Coriander: leaf plus a modest first seed-head pick. Allium at the ditch: bulbs and greens, modest. Thyme and marjoram at `P-16a` in the olive shade: modest.

### `THISTLE-GATHER`

This is rennet, and rennet gates every aged cheese.

- Gather heads **in full flower**, dry them hard, store dry.
- Own isolated plot, never in or beside a bed. Seed is wind-borne, so distance is not the control — **cutting every head at full flower is**, and that is the rennet stage anyway. Never let one head ripen.
- Prefer a cardoon type: big heads, one clump, does not run at the root.

## Materials

### `ACORN-GATHER`

A calendar hero, not an opportunistic pick. This band collides with grain and pulse harvest every year, and that collision is how Y9 was lost.

- Windfall sweep the P-09 terrace tread and the `OAK-ORCHARD-RESERVE` margin, then **pole-knock and shake** the low branches.
- Cull in the field: cap stain, worm, split. **Float-cull at the ditch** — floaters are hollow.
- **Do not shell on the gather day.** Stow shell-on at the v1 mat, `WOOD-CRATE-4`, `BARREL-4` for overflow. Shelling is a separate batch job.
- Repeated laps beat one big day; mast falls over weeks.
- The ~1 Dec lap is windfall only, then hands off until next fall.

Typical: ~6.7–7.5 kg bulk per season, ~3.9–4.1 kg net.

### `WOAD-PULL`

Outer rosette leaves only, CU-KNIFE, hands light, **crown intact** — the plant has to carry on producing. Several pulls through the window, ~180 g fresh each. Bed D year-two-plus rosettes are the working stand; wild P-18 at ~2.8 km NW is for breeding stock.

A last pull can be traded for letting the crown stand to seed. That is a choice between this year's dye and next year's line — make it deliberately.

### `MADDER-DIG`

**South pair only** of `MADDER-BED-RESERVE ×4`; the north pair regrows for next time. Grain lanes untouched. The wild lip `P-19` is dug on the same pass. Dig, shake at the thresh lip, dry stock to the chem shelf. ~240–280 g dry per season.

Select for root crimson yield and crown vigour; against crown rot. Dye lines override grain-drill grammar — do not rogue or thin them to field standards.

### `NITRE-LEACH`

Harvest band only. The bed itself is a year-round process — charge, feed, and the ~18–21 day turn — held in `NITRE-TURN` on [periodic.md](../../checklists/periodic.md), and the leach-to-crystal sequence is `NITRE-BOIL` in [processing.md](processing.md).

**Recharge the bed on the same pass.** An unrecharged bed loses a year, and the year is the process.

### `SALT-HAUL`

Brackish from the S-03 marsh at ~700 m, or the PM-R1 north flat at the tagged pour point. ~8.4 L per amphora, finger read ~1.022 class, amphora lashed to the cart. Evaporation is `SALT-EVAP` in [processing.md](processing.md).

### `ICE-HAUL`

- `ICE-ROUTE-1` to `LENS-1` at ~915 m.
- **Gate is a hard frost.** Mild or wet means prep only — a thaw nibbles the lens face and the cut is unsafe.
- `ICE-TONGS-1`, blocks to crate, **straw between courses**, chips filling the gaps.
- Expect ~4 blocks to shatter on the thin edge, plus transit melt. Budget it rather than being surprised.
- Stow to the horreum ice vault, hard blocks to the west niche, cull edge slush before the peak lap.
- **The constraint is vault capacity, not the lens.** Size the arc to the vault.

~25 kg net per lap, ~130 kg per season.

### `STONE-HAUL` · `ORE-HAUL` · `CLAY-HAUL`

- **Clay** M-01 at T-1 90 m, ~2 m cut face, ~31 kg net per lap to pile 1. Ranked D #1, C #2, B filler, A reject.
- **Sand** M-09 river at T-1 200 m, for grog and temper.
- **Limestone** M-06 to pile 7, for lime and mortar.
- **Reference stone** is a separate standard. Grain size is set by cooling rate, so take chilled **dyke margins and pillow rims**, never gabbro. **Ring the block** — sound stone rings and holds the note, vesicular or cracked stone thuds. Cut from a **fresh break**, because the outer few fingers of any exposed rock is an altered weathering rind. Cut generously oversize, then season on three points.
- **Gypsum** M-21: pale band, thumbnail-soft, **no vinegar fizz**. That last test is what separates it from carbonate.
- **Nickel** on serpentinite: the ore is the **red dirt on top of the rock**, and sparse stunted flora is the indicator.

### `WOOD-HAUL` · `RESIN-COLLECT`

Four-lap cart runs off the T-1 margin and T-2, ~25–29 kg net per event, stacked green at pile 5 under a bark mat. Select **straight** stock for wagon and rail work. Retort feed comes off pile 5; see `CHARCOAL-BURN` in [processing.md](processing.md).

Pine resin at `M-08`: ×8 trees scored and cupped, collected on the pass rather than as a trip.

## Animals

### `WEIR-FISH` · `SNAIL-GATHER`

The two standing calorie sources that need no window.

- Weir at W ~200 m, checked on the farm fork, 4–8 small per day class. Reset stakes as needed. Surplus goes straight to `JERKY-TOPUP`, not eaten down.
- Snails at A-02, creek and river rocks under the reed lip, ×2 passes, ~400–500 g a lap. **Purge in a vented tray for 24 hours minimum** before cooking or brining — slime clear, smell PASS. Winter snails run smaller.

### Hunting

- **Calories are fat; meat is protein. They are not the same bank.** A spring animal can be the largest carcass of the year and carry almost none — the d3269 stag was ~95 kg and gave ~1.4 kg tallow.
- Autumn deer are the fat deer. Plan `PEMMICAN-MAKE` around autumn, not around whatever was shot.
- A pack is measured in fat, protein and starch, never in kilograms.
- Good tracking weather: cool, overcast, soft ground, scent held low.
- Hide: cut the butt along the backbone for belting — densest, least stretch.
- Cast antler is hard and worth collecting free in spring at the T-2 oaks; velvet antler is soft and worthless.

### `DONKEY-HUNT`

Wild recruit at D-27, narrow autumn band, entirely separate from holding care. Not a food hunt.

### `BROWSE-TRIM`

Runs **inline** at the fig and grape heroes — fig tips and Bed D fence growth to the goat manger. Never a trip of its own.
