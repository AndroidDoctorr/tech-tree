## CU-SMELT-O1-Y10 · D1

### 1 · Roast

| | |
|---|---|
| **Charge** | **~17 kg** dressed ore, crushed to nut size |
| **Why roast first** | **This ore is a carbonate — it is carrying gas and water it does not need to take into the furnace.** Roasting drives both off on **cheap open-hearth heat** instead of spending **expensive blast heat** on decomposition. The charge goes in **lighter, blacker, and hotter** |
| **Read** | **Green → **black oxide**. Clean, no smell of sulfur — **this is a carbonate ore, not a sulfide, which is why I can go straight to reduction with no second roast |

### 2 · The iron is an asset here

> **Yesterday the iron in this ore fouled my liquor. **In the furnace it is exactly what I want.

| | |
|---|---|
| **The problem a furnace has** | **The gangue is silica**, and silica on its own is stiff and glassy at smelting heat. **A stiff slag traps the metal droplets and they never find each other** |
| **The fix** | **Iron oxide fluxes silica into a thin, runny slag.** The ore brings its own flux — **I topped it up with a little of the iron-stained rock I set aside rather than adding anything new** |
| **Result** | **Slag ran **thin and dark, exactly as it should |

### 3 · Reduce

| | |
|---|---|
| **Blast** | **WW-2 off the belt tree** · steady, no arm on a bellows all day. **This is the difference a wheel makes and I should remember it while I am building the generator** |
| **Charge** | **Alternating charcoal and roasted ore · **deep charcoal bed |
| **The one thing that matters** | **Keep the charge in the REDUCING zone.** Too much air and the copper simply **burns back to oxide** and goes out in the slag. **A copper furnace is won or lost on atmosphere, not on temperature** |
| **Prills** | **Metal came down as scattered droplets through the slag, settled, **coalesced into a button and scatter |

### 5 · Consolidate · **and POLE it**

| Step | Notes |
|------|-------|
| **Re-melt** | **Prills into the crucible under a **charcoal cover |
| **POLING** | **Thrust a green wood pole into the melt.** It spits and boils as the sap gasifies, and **those gases scrub the oxygen back out of the metal** |
| **Why this is not optional for WIRE** | **Copper melted in air drinks oxygen and comes out gassy and brittle.** Brittle copper is fine for a plate you hammer flat. **It is useless in a drawplate — it cracks and snaps partway down a run** · **wire demands ductile metal, and poling is how you get it** |
| **Tell** | **Poured a test dip and **bent it double, cold. It folded and did not crack. That is the bar |
| **Cast** | **×1 wire bar** poured into a sand-and-plaster-faced mould — **first job for the new plaster: a clean flat mould face** |

## Discoveries

| ID | Notes |
|----|-------|
| **IRON-IS-A-FLUX-3230** | **The same iron that fouls a leach liquor is what makes a smelt work.** Silica gangue alone gives a stiff slag that **traps the metal droplets**; **iron oxide thins it so the prills can settle and merge.** The ore carries its own flux — **a contaminant in one process is a reagent in another** |
| **WIRE-NEEDS-POLING-3230** | **Poling is not a finishing nicety when the metal is destined for a drawplate.** Gassy copper is fine hammered flat and **snaps halfway down a draw**. **Bend a test dip double cold — if it folds without cracking, it will draw** |
| **PLASTER-FIRST-JOB-3230** | **A plaster-faced mould gives a flat clean cast face** — first real use of the new material, and it will only get more useful |
