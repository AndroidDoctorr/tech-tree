## COMMUTATOR-2 · design read

**Mocked the whole thing up in soft wire and wood before committing any good copper — **COMMUTATOR-MOCKUP-READ HIT**.**

### What is right, and it is most of it

| Feature | Read |
|---------|------|
| **Wire-formed segments instead of a split tube** | **This is the best idea in the drawing.** A proper commutator is a copper tube **sawn into segments with insulation packed between** — fiddly, precise, and easy to ruin. **Bending wire into arcs is something I can do all day**, and I now have wire coming out of my ears |
| **Spring-caliper brushes** | **Correct.** A contact needs **constant gentle self-adjusting pressure**, and work-hardened copper wire is springy. The caliper gives that for free, and **it presses from both sides so the force balances and does not push the shaft sideways** |
| **Curving the contact tips** | **Right, and for the right reason.** Two crossed round wires touch at a **point**; curve the brush to the segment's radius and it becomes a **line**. **Lower resistance, less heat, less burning** |
| **Ramping the segment out to full radius** | **Very good instinct.** A brush hitting a **step** bounces, and a bouncing brush **arcs**. A ramp eases it on |
| **Petals for more segments** | **Right direction.** Two segments gives output that **dies to nothing twice a revolution**. More segments = **smoother output and less to burn off at each transition** |

### What I would change

| # | Change | Why |
|---|--------|-----|
| **1** | **THE GAP IS THE WHOLE DESIGN.** Make the brush tip **slightly WIDER than the gap** between segments** | **Two ways to get this wrong. **Gap wider than the brush** → the circuit **opens** at every transition, the coil kicks, and it **arcs and burns copper**. **Brush much wider than the gap*** → it shorts two segments together for too long. **Slightly wider is right**: it bridges briefly, and bridging briefly is **good** |
| **2** | **Put the gaps where the coil makes NOTHING**, and make the brush position **adjustable** | **The brief bridge only costs nothing if it happens while that coil is **between the poles and generating no voltage**. **I cannot calculate where that is — I have to find it.** So the brush holder must **rotate and clamp**: run it, watch for sparking, **rock the brushes until the sparking stops, lock it there |
| **3** | **Make the brushes literal BRUSHES — a bundle of fine wires, not one wire** | **One wire = one contact point carrying all the current = **a hot spot that pits and welds**. **A bundle of many fine wires gives many contact points, conforms to the surface, and no single point carries it all. I have fine wire and this costs me nothing. *(This is presumably why they are called brushes.)* |
| **4** | **Ramp only at the ENDS — constant radius in the middle** | **If the segment ramps continuously from small to full radius, the caliper is **flexed wide open and closed again every single pass**. That is **thousands of bend cycles an hour**, and copper **work-hardens and then snaps**. **Short lead-in and lead-out ramps, flat constant radius between — eased on, steady while riding |
| **5** | **Odd number of segments** | **With an even count, **opposite segments hit their gaps at the same instant**. An odd count staggers them so **there is always a good contact somewhere |
| **6** | **Capture the segments mechanically — do not rely on glue** | **They are spinning and **want to fly outward**. Set them into **grooves in a hardwood hub**, pegged, then **soak the hub in wax-rosin** — insulating **and it locks everything |
| **7** | **Plaster makes the hub PATTERN, not the hub** | **Plaster is brittle and this part is under centrifugal load. **Use plaster to cast an accurate pattern/jig so every segment sits at the same radius — then make the real hub in hardwood |

### The one measurement that matters

> **All the segments must sit at the SAME radius.** One proud segment and the brush is lifted clear of all the others each revolution. **That is why the jig matters more than the skill** — mark the radius once and set every arc to it.

### Verdict

> **The design is sound and I am building it.** It is **better suited to what I can actually make** than a sawn-tube commutator would be. The changes are all **refinements, not corrections** — the gap rule, adjustable brushes, wire-bundle contacts, end-only ramps, and a jig so every segment sits true.

## Discoveries

| ID | Notes |
|----|-------|
| **COMMUTATION-IS-ABOUT-THE-GAP-3233** | **Brush slightly wider than the gap.** Narrower → the circuit opens and **arcs**; much wider → it shorts segments. **Slightly wider bridges briefly, which is correct** — provided the gap sits where the coil makes no voltage |
| **FIND-THE-NEUTRAL-BY-EAR-3233** | **The brush position cannot be calculated, only found.** Build the holder to **rotate and clamp**, then **rock it until the sparking stops**. Design for adjustment, not for correctness |
| **WHY-THEY-ARE-CALLED-BRUSHES-3233** | **A bundle of fine wires beats one thick contact** — many contact points, conforms to the surface, no single point carries all the current and welds itself to the segment |
| **FLEX-FATIGUE-3233** | **A contact spring flexed through its full range every revolution is being work-hardened thousands of times an hour, and copper that is work-hardened SNAPS.** Ramp at the ends only; ride flat in between |
| **JIG-BEFORE-SKILL-3233** | **Every segment at the same radius matters more than any segment being beautiful.** One proud segment lifts the brush off all the others. **Cast the jig, then work to it** |
