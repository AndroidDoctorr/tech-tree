## THE READ · **acid curd is a dead end and I have been in it for years**

**Sat with the press and finally asked the right question: *why can I never age any of this?***

| | Acid-set *(what I make)* | Rennet-set *(what I do not)* |
|---|---|---|
| **How it works** | **Acid drops the casein out all at once.** The protein network is **shattered as it forms** | **An enzyme clips one specific bond** and the casein **knits into a continuous elastic gel** |
| **Texture** | **Crumbly. Short. Falls apart.** | **Elastic. Stretches. Holds together** |
| **Can it be cooked / cut / stirred?** | **No — it disintegrates** | **Yes — cut it, cook it, stir it, and it EXPELS whey and tightens** |
| **Can it be pressed hard and aged?** | **NO. This is the ceiling.** Fresh only, eat it in days** | **Yes. This is how a cheese becomes a KEEPING FOOD |
| **Whey left behind** | **Acid whey — already stripped | **Sweet whey — better for ricotta, better for feeding |

> **I have three goats and a thin grain year, and I have been converting milk into something that must be eaten within the week.** **Rennet is not a refinement of what I do. It is the thing that turns dairy into STORAGE.**

### Where rennet comes from *(without killing a kid)*

**The classic source is the lining of an unweaned ruminant's fourth stomach. **I am not killing kid #3 for it — KEEP doctrine stands.** There are plant proteases that do the same job:**

| Source | Read |
|--------|------|
| **Thistle / cardoon flowers** | **The traditional one** — dried flower heads steeped in warm water. **Storable, gatherable, repeatable.** **This is the right answer** |
| **Fig latex** | **The white sap. **Very strong** — a few drops. Works, but **easy to overshoot |
| **Nettle** | **Boiled in salt water. **Weak** — large volumes for a small set** |

**Caveat worth writing down now:** **plant rennets over-work the protein if you use too much or age too long, and the cheese goes BITTER.** **Use the least that sets it.**

## HERB-SEED-SAVE-Y10 D1 · **designation, not collection**

**Walked the campus herb beds. **Good overwintered stand.

### The timing read

> **Almost nothing is ripe yet.** Coriander is throwing flower, the alliums are just lifting scapes, the thyme is in bud. **Ripe seed is weeks away — May and June.**
>
> **What this window is actually for is DESIGNATING the plants and then leaving them alone.**

### The rule I had not stated

> **You cannot take LEAF and SEED from the same plant.** Every cut sends it back to vegetative growth and **delays or destroys the seed head**. **A seed plant must be taken out of the kitchen rotation on the day you choose it, not the day it flowers.**

### Designated + staked

| Crop | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Coriander** | **×6 strongest plants staked · OFF the cutting rotation.** Seed ~June · **cut whole stalks when brown and dry them head-down in a bag** — ripe coriander shatters |
| **Allium** | **×4 scapes left to run.** The rest get their scapes **snapped off** — a scaping allium **stops growing its bulb** |
| **Thyme** | **×3 plants left unclipped** |
| **Rosemary heads** | **Marked — **but see below |
| **Parsley** | **Second-year plants — they are biennial, this is their seed year.** ×3 left standing |
| **Selection** | **Chose for VIGOUR and LATE BOLTING, not for size.** An early bolter makes seed that bolts early — **that is breeding a worse herb every year without noticing** |

### MINT-DIVIDE-Y10 · **done today · ✓**

**Mint is on the calendar as *root divide*, not seed, and that is correct: **mint does not come true from seed** — the seedlings are a lottery and often nothing like the parent.** **Lifted the crock clump, tore it into ×4, replanted the outer vigorous pieces and discarded the woody centre.** **Kept in the crock deliberately — mint in open ground takes the bed.**

### ROSEMARY + THYME · **seed is the wrong method**

> **Rosemary from seed germinates badly and slowly and half of it comes out unlike the parent.** **Layering is far better and it is nearly free:** bend a low branch to the ground, **nick the underside**, pin it down, cover it. **It roots on its own while still attached to the mother**, and I cut it free next season with a full root system and **an exact copy of the plant I chose.**

**×4 rosemary branches pinned. ×3 thyme.** **Cost: ten minutes and four pegs.**
