## THE PRINCIPLE · **the two meters want opposite things**

> **A voltmeter must be nearly an OPEN circuit. An ammeter must be nearly a SHORT.**


|                        | Voltmeter                                                                  | Ammeter                                                        |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Goes**               | **Across** the thing                                                       | **In line with** the thing                                     |
| **Must**               | **Draw almost no current**                                                 | **Drop almost no voltage**                                     |
| **Or else**            | **It **loads** the circuit and **changes the very number it is reporting** | **It **chokes** the circuit and does the same                  |
| **So the movement is** | **MANY turns of FINE wire** *(sensitive — deflects on a trickle)*          | **FEW turns of FAT wire** *(carries the load without heating)* |
| **Plus**               | **A large resistance in SERIES**                                           | **A small resistance in PARALLEL** *(a shunt)*                 |


**Which means two instruments, not one with a switch. **Built both.

## THE OBSTACLE · **I need a BAD conductor and I have spent years learning to make good ones**

**Ran the arithmetic on R-STD-1 and got an unpleasant surprise.**


|                                       |                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **R-STD-1**                           | **10 m of 0.9 mm copper**                                                    |
| **Its actual resistance**             | **Tiny.** Copper that thick is **almost a piece of wire-shaped nothing**     |
| **For a voltmeter I need**            | **Hundreds of times that**                                                   |
| **In copper at 0.9 mm that would be** | **Kilometres.** Absurd — and I would be measuring the meter, not the circuit |




### Two ways out, and I need both


| Lever                 | Effect                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Go THINNER**        | **Resistance rises with the SQUARE of the thinning.** Halve the diameter and you **quadruple** the resistance per metre                           |
| **Use a WORSE METAL** | **Iron is several times worse a conductor than copper** — and worse is exactly what I want here. **For once, the cheap metal is the right metal** |


> **Resistance turns out to be a RESOURCE, and I have been unknowingly destroying it for years by making everything out of good copper.**

## IRON-FINE-DRAW-1 · **harder than copper, and I found out why**


|               |                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Target**    | As fine as I could take it                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Reality**   | **Stopped at ~0.3 mm.** Iron **work-hardens far faster than copper** — where copper tolerates several passes between anneals, **iron stiffens after one or two and then snaps in the die** |
| **Also**      | **It **scores the plate** more than copper does. **Wax generously and anneal sooner than feels necessary                                                                                 |
| **Result**    | **~106 m of ~0.3 mm iron wire**                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Mass cost** | **~59 g.** Sixty grams of iron                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Time cost** | **Most of the day**                                                                                                                                                                        |


> **Fine wire is cheap in METAL and expensive in HOURS.** That is the opposite of almost everything else I make, and it is worth remembering before I ever plan around drawing a lot of it.

**Wound non-inductively onto a former, ends to posts. **R-BIG-1.

### Known flaw, written down so I do not forget it

> **Iron's resistance changes noticeably with temperature.** **Do not hold the resistor in my hand, do not sit it near the forge, and take repeat readings at the same bench temperature.** *(A metal that does not do this would be better. I do not know how to make one yet — **filed.**)*
