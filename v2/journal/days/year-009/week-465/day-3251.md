### ★★ **Why HIVE-6 has five combs and HIVE-5 has two**

> **A SWARM LEAVES LOADED. They gorge on honey before they go — the swarm CARRIES ITS OWN WAX BUDGET.**
>
> **A DRIVEN COLONY DOES NOT.** I drummed them out of a basket with whatever happened to be in their stomachs. **At eight honey to one wax, that is the entire difference** between five combs and two.
>
> ***So the drive is not a worse method — it is a method with a known cost, and the cost is comb. Feed harder and earlier next time. That is the correction for HIVE-2.***

### ★★ **And why HIVE-6 has no eggs — a COMPLETELY different reason**

> **This was a CAST, not a prime swarm. A cast leaves with a VIRGIN queen.**
>
> **A virgin has to **fly out and mate** before she can lay a single egg, and **a mating flight wants a warm, calm afternoon — of which we have had almost none.
>
> **So I have two hives with no eggs, from two entirely unrelated causes:**
>
> | | Has | Lacks |
> |---|---|---|
> | **HIVE-5** | **A mated, laying queen | **COMB to lay in |
> | **HIVE-6** | **Plenty of comb | **A MATED queen |
>
> **And the cool snap is delaying BOTH — by two different mechanisms.** *One colony is too cold to build; the other's queen is too cold to fly.*

### ⚠ **The shared danger, and a gap in my setup it exposes**

> **Neither of these colonies has any brood. Which means neither can raise a replacement queen if it loses the one it has** — and HIVE-6's is out flying over a valley full of birds.
>
> **The standard insurance is to give a queenless colony a comb of EGGS from a strong one. They raise their own queen from it.**
>
> **I cannot do that. HIVE-2 is a skep and HIVE-3 is a fixed-comb box — I cannot lift a comb out of either.**
>
> ★★ **This reframes the whole top-bar project. I have been treating movable comb as a HARVEST improvement. It is also INSURANCE: movable comb is what lets me move BROOD between colonies, and moving brood is how you rescue a queenless hive.**
>
> **HIVE-3's conversion just got more urgent, and it is the easy one — it is already a box.**

## CONSULT · **WAGON-WHEEL COOLING**

**User:** *An attachment that uses the wagon wheel to drive a fan or compressor for active food cooling while rolling.*

### ⚠ **First, the correction that saves the project: **A FAN ON DRY FOOD MAKES IT WARMER

> **Moving air does not cool anything by itself. **It only equalises things faster.** On a hot day, blowing 35° air over meat brings the meat to 35° **sooner.
>
> **A fan cools ONLY when there is something WET for it to evaporate from.** So the fan is not the machine — **the wet surface is the machine, and the fan is an accessory to it.**

### **Ranked by return on effort**

| # | Approach | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **INSULATION + ICE FROM THE VAULT** | **Buildable TODAY, biggest effect, no moving parts, cannot poison anybody.** I have an **ice vault** — almost nobody alive does. **A well-insulated chest with a block of ice holds for days, and insulation is the entire game** |
| **2** | **EVAPORATIVE JACKET** | **Wet sand or wet cloth in an outer skin, **dry chamber inside** *(the food must stay dry — only the JACKET is wet)* · **buys maybe 8–12° in dry air, which is the difference between meat lasting a day and lasting three** · **and while the wagon rolls, the airflow is FREE — no fan needed at all |
| **3** | **Vapour compression *(a real refrigerator)* | **Multi-year, and gated on things I do not have:** pressure-tight vessels at several atmospheres, a **sliding seal on a reciprocating compressor that must not leak for days**, valves, and a refrigerant. **SO₂ is the plausible one and I will be making it at Dörtyol anyway** — but **a refrigerant leak inside a food wagon is a poisoning, not an inconvenience. Not soon |

### ⚠ **The flaw in wheel-driven cooling, specifically**

> **It stops when the wagon stops — and the wagon stops to rest in the heat of the day, which is exactly when the load is worst.**
>
> **A cooling system that works only while moving is **backwards**. **Ice does not care whether I am moving.

### ★ **But there IS a genuinely good job for the wheel, and it is not the fan**

> **The real failure of an evaporative jacket on a wagon is not airflow — **airflow is free while rolling.** It is that **THE JACKET DRIES OUT AND NOBODY NOTICES.
>
> **So: drive a small pump off the wheel and feed a DRIP HEADER over the jacket from a reservoir.**
>
> **Power the WETTING, not the airflow.** The wheel turns exactly when the wind is drying the jacket fastest, so **the supply matches the demand automatically** — and when I halt, the jacket is already soaked.
>
> ***Which is the same lesson as the whole of this week: find the actual bottleneck before building the impressive part.***

**Filed: WAGON-COOL-1 — (a) insulated chest + ice, (b) evaporative jacket, (c) wheel-driven drip. Compression refrigeration filed behind Dörtyol and pressure vessels.**
