## Basalt — the seasoning is a PROCESS, not a wait

**Went to turn the blocks and realised I had been thinking of "let them season" as doing nothing for five months. It is not.**

> ★★ **STONE RELAXES TOWARD WHATEVER IT IS LYING ON.** *A block left five months on an uneven bed does not merely fail to improve — **it creeps into the shape of the bed.*** **Three-point support, every block, and the three points properly solid.**

★ **And turn them.** *Gravity's own sag is small but it is always in the same direction, and five months is long enough for always to matter.* **Turned all three, marked the faces so the rotation is a cycle and not a muddle.**

### ★★ But the real fix is that "season until autumn" is the wrong KIND of instruction

> ★★ **"WAIT FIVE MONTHS" IS A CALENDAR TRIGGER FOR SOMETHING I CAN ACTUALLY MEASURE.** *Per [routines.md](../../../../checklists/index.md): prefer a state trigger wherever the state is readable.*
>
> ★★ **THE SEASONING IS DONE WHEN THE BLOCKS STOP MOVING — and I can watch them stop.** **Rough a reference face on each, set a datum, and check them against each other every few weeks. When two successive checks agree, the movement has stopped and the stone is ready** — **whether that is August or next spring.**

★ **It also tells me something I would otherwise never learn: HOW LONG this rock actually takes.** *Which I will want to know every time I quarry again.*

**Roughed a reference face on each block. First datum recorded. Added to routines: basalt datum check, every ~3 weeks.**

## ★★★ ARMATURE-3 — and the load line stopped me building it blind

**I sat down to start the coarse armature and instead did the arithmetic, because yesterday taught me what arithmetic is worth.**

### ☠ The deadlock I was walking into

> ☠ **ARMATURE-3 needs ~500 g of copper. The copper comes from CU-CELL-1. CU-CELL-1 runs slowly BECAUSE ARMATURE-2 is the wrong shape for it.** ★★ **I need the armature to make the copper to make the armature.**

### ★★ The way out is to reshape the LOAD instead of the machine

> ★★★ **I CANNOT REWIND THE GENERATOR AGAIN WITHOUT UNDOING YESTERDAY. BUT I CAN REWIND THE LOAD** — **series for a high-voltage source, parallel for a high-current one.** ★★ **The load line cuts both ways, and shaping the load is an afternoon where shaping the machine is a week.**
>
> ★★★ **AND SERIES DOES SOMETHING BETTER THAN DIVIDE THE CURRENT — IT REUSES IT.** *Cells in parallel split the current between them and each gets a share. **Cells in series all carry the SAME current, and every one of them plates copper with it.*** **The same coulomb does the job twice.**

### ⚠ So I worked out how many, before building any of them

**Each cell costs voltage — its decomposition voltage plus its own resistance — so every cell added buys a plating station and spends headroom. There is an optimum, not a ladder.**

| Cells in series | Current | **Copper rate** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.32 I | 0.32 |
| ★★ **2** | 0.20 I | ★★ **0.40 — best** |
| 3 | 0.11 I | 0.33 |
| 4 | 0.04 I | 0.17 |

**Built the pair. Measured. It sits where the arithmetic said — about a quarter more copper per day than one cell, from no extra power at all.**

> ⚠ ★★ **AND THE ARITHMETIC ALSO SAID WHY IT IS ONLY A QUARTER: THE GENERATOR'S OWN 8.6 R IS THE BIGGEST RESISTANCE IN THE WHOLE CIRCUIT.** ★★★ **You can shape the load, but you cannot shape away the source's internal resistance.** *When the source dominates, matching is all that is on offer — and matching is just the load line again.*

★ **So ARMATURE-3 is still wanted. It is no longer URGENT, and it is no longer a deadlock.**

⚠ **I also checked whether the water cell could ride in the same series string. It can — but it costs about two-thirds of the copper rate, because its ~2 GB comes straight out of the headroom.** ★ **So they ALTERNATE. One machine, two products, not at once.**

## ☠ ★★ AND THE THING THAT WOULD HAVE QUIETLY RUINED IT

**I was about to plumb the two cells to a shared leach tank — the circulation fix from d3261, which is entirely correct on its own.**

> ☠ ★★ **A CONTINUOUS COLUMN OF LIQUOR BETWEEN TWO CELLS IN SERIES IS A WIRE.** *The electrolyte conducts. Join two series cells with a pipe of it and I have handed the current a path that bypasses one of them — **it plates nothing, it just leaks, and worse, it corrodes whatever it enters and leaves by.***
>
> ★★★ **THE LIQUID PATH IS A WIRE, AND I HAVE TO THINK ABOUT PLUMBING THE WAY I THINK ABOUT WIRING.**

★★ **Two good ideas that fight: SERIES wants the cells electrically separate, CIRCULATION wants them liquidly joined.** ★★★ **Resolved by breaking the stream: cascade the liquor through AIR. A falling gap of liquid is beads, and beads do not conduct.** *Launder and drip, never a full pipe.*

⚠ **Rebuilt the feed as an air-gap cascade before running it.**
