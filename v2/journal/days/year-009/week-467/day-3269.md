## ☠ ★★★ AND THEN I WEIGHED THE FAT, AND THE WHOLE POINT OF THE HUNT FELL APART

**I went out to bank CALORIES for the May trips. I came back with a mountain of meat and almost no calories.**

> ☠ ★★★ **HE IS IN VELVET AND HE IS EMPTY. A stag in late April has spent the winter on stored fat and is now spending what is left on growing antler.** ★★ **There is barely any tallow on him — a fraction of what the autumn deer gave.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **THE SPRING HUNGER GAP IS NOT JUST MY LARDER. IT IS EVERY ANIMAL IN THE VALLEY, AT THE SAME TIME, FOR THE SAME REASON.** *I have been thinking of the lean season as a fact about my stores. **It is a fact about the whole system, and the game is in it with me.***

### ★★★ Which exposes something I had simply never separated

> ★★★ **CALORIES ARE FAT. MEAT IS PROTEIN. THEY ARE NOT THE SAME BANK AND I HAVE BEEN COUNTING THEM AS ONE.** *Fat carries more than twice the energy of the same weight of anything else — **so "95 kg of meat" told me nothing at all about whether I can walk to Dörtyol on it.***

### ☠ And the part that is genuinely dangerous

> ☠ ★★★ **YOU CANNOT LIVE ON LEAN MEAT ALONE. It is not merely thin rations — it makes you ILL.** *The body can only process so much protein, and past that point it fails whether or not you are eating your fill.* ☠ **You can starve with a full belly, and eating MORE lean meat makes it worse rather than better.**
>
> ☠ ★★ **A pack of pure jerky is the exact shape of that mistake, and I was three weeks from carrying one over a mountain.**

⚠ **This is the one that would have got me, and it would not have looked like a hunting error or a packing error. It would have looked like getting sick on the trail for no reason.**

### ★★★ THE FIX — and jerky was always only half of it

> ★★★ **PEMMICAN. Dry the lean meat hard, pound it to shreds, and work rendered fat back INTO it.** ★★ **Jerky's problem was never preservation — lean is exactly right for keeping, because fat is what goes rancid. Jerky's problem is that it is HALF A RATION, and the other half is fat added back at the END, after the drying, where it cannot spoil the drying.**
>
> ★★ **It keeps for years, it is denser than either part alone, and I have both halves already.**

★★ **And it re-reads the whole food doctrine.** *I have been banking jerky AND cheese and thinking of them as two foods.* ★★★ **They are not two foods. Cheese is the FAT and jerky is the PROTEIN, and neither is an expedition ration without the other.** ★ **Acorn, honey and grain are the third leg.**

⚠ **Revised trip rule: a pack is not measured in kilograms. It is measured in FAT, PROTEIN and STARCH, separately.**

★ **Practical consequence: the autumn deer are the FAT deer, and that is when the pemmican gets made. Spring kills go to jerky and the fat gets added later from tallow and olive oil.**

## ★ Free material I have been walking past

**Stags cast their antlers in spring. The ground under the T-2 oaks has them lying on it, and I have never once picked one up.**

★★ **Cast antler is HARD; velvet antler is soft and useless.** *So the sheds on the ground are the good material and the ones on the animal I just shot are not.* **Antler beats bone for anything that takes a blow or a pressure load — pressure flakers, toggles, tool handles, wedges.**

**Collected six sheds on the way back. Free, renewable, annual, and lying in the open.**

## Trip prep — the bridge/oil lap

### ★★ Bridges do not fail where you look at them

> ★★★ **A TIMBER BRIDGE ROTS AT THE BURIED ENDS, NOT IN THE MIDDLE OF THE SPAN.** *The beam ends sit in the bank — dark, damp, no airflow — and that is exactly where rot lives.* ★★ **The deck looks perfect right up until the abutment lets go.**
>
> ★★ **AND YOU CANNOT SEE IT. YOU PROBE IT.** *Sound timber resists a spike; rotten timber takes the point and keeps it.* **Probe every beam end. Ignore the deck.**

> ☠ ★★ **AND THE COMMONEST FAILURE OF A SMALL BRIDGE IS NOT THE BRIDGE AT ALL — IT IS THE STREAMBED WASHING OUT FROM UNDER THE ABUTMENT.** *High water digs, the footing loses its ground, and a perfectly sound span drops.* **Read the BED, not just the structure** — ★ **and spring, right after the high water, is when the scour is freshest and most legible.**

**Kit: spike probe · cord and plumb for scour depth · a straightedge to read sag · notebook.**

### ★★★ And the oil trip is not a side quest — it is the gate on the August plaster

> ★★★ **BITUMEN IS THE DAMP-PROOF COURSE.** *BC-2 says the DPC is a HARD GATE before any interior plaster, because gypsum is slightly soluble and will dissolve out of a wicking wall.* ★★ **I had the gate filed and I had the material filed and I had not connected them.**
>
> ★★ **So: no bitumen, no DPC. No DPC, no plaster. The August plaster block depends on a trip in May.**

★ **Also: waterproofing, boat and vessel caulking, hafting mastic, roof seal** — **and eventually a still, because bitumen is the crude end of the same tree as the turpentine I already distil.**

**Kit: lidded crocks · sand dust to stop lumps welding to each other · and take it SOLID, not warm.**
