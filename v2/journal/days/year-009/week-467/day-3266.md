## ☠ HIVE-6 — the pattern is SCATTERED, and I cannot yet say why

**Went in early, before the cool set in. Eggs across three combs — and gaps. Not a slab. Cells missed all through the patch.**

**My first instinct was to call the queen poor. That instinct is wrong, and it is wrong in an interesting way.**

### Three causes, and they look the SAME today

| Cause | Why it would scatter |
|---|---|
| ★ **She is simply NEW** | A queen three weeks into laying **has not settled.** Early combs are ragged and tighten as she gets into her stride |
| ★ **The comb was not READY** | She will not lay in a cell the workers have not cleaned and polished. **On new comb in a small colony they cannot keep ahead of her** — the gaps are the workers' backlog, not her failure |
| ☠ **DIPLOID DRONE BROOD** | See below. **The queen is fine and the eggs are fine and they still die** |

> ☠ ★★ **THE ONE I DID NOT KNOW ABOUT. A honeybee's sex is set at a single place in its inheritance. If a queen mates with a drone carrying the SAME version she already has, then half of her fertilised eggs come out as MALES THAT SHOULD HAVE BEEN FEMALE** — and **the workers detect them within hours of hatching and eat them.**
>
> ★★ **The queen lays a perfect slab and half of it vanishes.** *Nothing is diseased, nothing is failing, and the colony builds at half speed forever.*
>
> ☠ ★★ **AND THE CAUSE IS A SMALL MATING POPULATION — WHICH IS EXACTLY WHAT I HAVE BUILT.**

### ★★ The test: ONE COMB, TWO TIMES

**I cannot separate these by staring harder today. But they separate perfectly over the next nine days, because they differ in WHAT HAPPENS TO THE EGGS — not in where they are.**

> ★★ **MARK ONE COMB. MAP IT TODAY. RE-MAP THE SAME COMB AT SEALING.** ★★ **The signal is the DIFFERENCE between what was laid and what survived** — *which is the same controlled experiment I run on everything else. One comb, two readings, one variable.*

| What the same comb does by ~d3275 | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Gaps FILL IN, sealed slab comes up solid** | ★ **Workers' backlog. She was always fine** |
| **Later combs TIGHTEN** | ★ **She was just new. Fine** |
| ☠ **Laid solid, seals with holes — a REGULAR, EVEN scatter of missing cells** | ☠ **DIPLOID DRONE. The mating was too close** |

★ **Today's scatter is at the EGG stage, and that argues AGAINST the bad case** — *diploid drone brood is laid solid and disappears later. So the likely answer is a new queen on unready comb, which is nothing at all.* **But now I have the test, and I will never have to guess about a queen again.**

**Marked the comb. Kept feeding. Re-map ~d3275.**

## ★★ GLASS-COVER MELT TRIAL — tax 4% → ~1.3%

**The problem from d3262: electrowon copper is the purest metal I have ever made, and MELTING IT to draw wire gives 4% of its conductivity back. Molten copper drinks oxygen out of the air.** Charcoal has been the blanket. **Charcoal is porous — it makes the space above the metal reducing, but it does not SEAL it.**

**Trial: a layer of my palest glass, floated directly on the melt.**

### Result

**Melted, held, poled, cast, drawn, measured on the four-terminal jig against R-STD-1.** ★★ **Tax down from ~4% to about 1.3%.**

### ★★ And the thing that nearly made it worse instead of better

> ☠ ★★ **A GLASS THAT CONTAINS OXYGEN CAN GIVE OXYGEN.** *A silicate melt is not inert — it is a solvent full of metal oxides, and if any of them is happier as metal than copper is as oxide, **the cover feeds the melt the exact thing it is meant to keep out.*** ⚠ **Iron from the sand is the culprit, and iron-stained glass is my COMMONEST glass.**
>
> ★★ **So the cover must be my cleanest, palest, most iron-free glass — the expensive stuff — and this is the first job where glass QUALITY buys something other than clarity.**

### ★★ The layered answer

> ★★ **GLASS ON THE METAL. CHARCOAL ON TOP OF THE GLASS.** *Both float, and they stack in that order.* **The glass SEALS the surface the charcoal never could; the charcoal keeps the space above reducing so the glass itself is not fed oxygen from the air.** ★★ **Each one covers the other's failure, and neither alone does the job** — *charcoal cannot seal, and glass cannot reduce.*

### ⚠ Two costs, both real

- ⚠ **Glass WETS the crucible and sticks.** *On cooling it shrinks at a different rate than the clay and cracks it.* **Pour the crucible out hot and empty, or count crucibles as consumables.**
- ⚠ **Pouring drags glass into the mould**, and glass in a casting is a flaw. **Skim first, pour slow, and STOP EARLY — leave the last of the metal behind with the glass rather than chase it.**
