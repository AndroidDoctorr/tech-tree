## QUALITY CONTROL · **the first time I have been able to CHECK rather than believe**

**Every previous coat run ended with "it isolates." **That is a yes-or-no answer and it hides everything interesting.

### Test 1 · **the wire's own resistance is a GAUGE CHECK**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Method** | **Measured the **whole 104 m run against R-STD-1 on the bridge |
| **Logic** | **I know the length and I know what the resistance SHOULD be for 0.9 mm.** **If the wire is thin anywhere, the total comes out HIGH.** Resistance integrates every defect along the whole length into one number |
| **Result** | **WIRE-GAUGE-CONSISTENCY HIT — came in close to predicted.** The draw is even end to end |
| **Why this is good** | **A thin spot in an armature is a hot spot, and I would never find it by eye on a hundred metres of wire.** **One measurement checks the lot** |

### Test 2 · **leakage, not just isolation**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | ***"Does it conduct? No. Good."* — **a film one molecule from failing passes that test |
| **Now** | **Put several cells across a test wrap and MEASURE the leakage.** A marginal film **leaks a little**; a good one leaks nothing I can read |
| **And** | **Test at the voltage it will actually see**, not at one cell. A film that holds at one may not at four |
| **Result** | **No readable leakage, **compressed or slack**. **The wax-rosin holds |

### The honest calibration · **what will actually kill this coating**

> **It will not be voltage.** At the few volts this machine will make, **electrical breakdown is not a real risk** and I should not pretend it is.
>
> **The enemies are ABRASION and HEAT** — the film **scraped through during winding**, and **softening in service.** So the meaningful test is the one I already run: **wind it, then test it under compression.**

### One thing filed for later, deliberately not acted on

> **In a layer-wound coil, **turn-to-turn** voltage is trivial but **the first layer and the last layer can sit at nearly the whole coil's voltage while touching each other.** **Layer-to-layer is the failure point, not turn-to-turn — which is exactly why the paper between layers matters and the thread does not.
>
> **At my voltages this does not matter yet. Writing it down for when it does.**

What made today worth writing down is not the coating, which is now dull, but that for the first time I could check it instead of believing it. I put the whole run on the bridge, and because I know how long it is and what it ought to measure, the number tells me whether the wire is thin anywhere — one reading integrating every defect along a hundred metres, and a thin spot in a winding is a hot spot I would never find by eye. It came in where it should. Then I stopped asking whether the coating isolates, which is a question with only two answers and hides everything, and measured how much it leaks instead. Nothing I can read, slack or squeezed. I also made myself write down the honest version: at the handful of volts this machine will produce, the insulation is never going to fail electrically. It will fail by being scraped off during winding or going soft when the coil warms. Which means the only test that means anything is to wind it and then squeeze it, and I have been doing that since the start without quite knowing why.*
