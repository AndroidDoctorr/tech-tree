## Close

**THE WEEK THE MACHINE GOT A NUMBER.**

**Seven days, and the arc closed exactly where it was aimed: **GEN-WW-1 is built, running, and measured.** Three weeks of drawing, coating and metrology went in, and what came out is not *"a generator"* but **~1.15 GB loaded, ~0.45 I, ~2.4 R internal — the first machine on this campus that can be compared to itself next month.

**The strongest thing that happened all week was a disappointment.** The output came in well under my arithmetic, and because I had a meter the shortfall **broke apart into a ranked work list** instead of a shrug: the wheel droops from 12 rpm to 8 the moment it is loaded *(and I had built every calculation on the free-running figure)*, and the air gap is too generous because I did not trust my own concentricity. **A year ago this week would have ended in "it works!" and taught me nothing.**

**Mechanically, the belt taught more than the generator. **Grip is WRAP, not tension** — the small pulley always slips and always matters; **a flat belt climbs to the highest point of a pulley, so crowning self-centres it and a flange only chews its edge off.** And when I asked whether to cut the belts out for gears, the answer was **no, on three counts**: the wooden gears I could cut this year are *worse* than the belt, **the belt is a mechanical fuse** between a water wheel's torque and a brittle tooth, and above all — **do not rebuild the part you have not measured.** **GEAR-SPRINT-1 filed under metrology**, where it belongs, because what gears actually buy is **phase *(leadscrew lathe, clock train, dividing head)*.

**On bars: **four colonies, two of them on movable comb, and a swarm that took the bait box on its own.** The egg check found no eggs — **and the date was wrong, not the colony.** A driven colony arrives with nowhere to lay; **the clock starts at the first finished comb.** Out of which came the number that explains three years of thin harvests: **wax costs honey about eight to one. It is the most expensive thing in the hive.

**Also filed: **BC-2 confined masonry** *(build for the earthquake, because I will be there for it — and **retrofit, don't rebuild**)*, and **FOOD-DOCTRINE-Y10 *(thin grain is answered by changing the fraction — jerky and aged cheese — and this is food security the expedition rides on, not the reverse)*.
