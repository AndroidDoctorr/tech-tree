# Day 5

## Events

Charcoal day. You said it before dawn: **dig, haul, build, burn if you can.**

### Dawn — small batch + weir

First: open the **Day 3 burial**. You scrape earth with the digging stick, crack the clay cap, and smell charcoal before you see it. Not perfect — some **half-burnt centres** — but **2.8 kg** of usable black lumps go on the dry shelf. The method works in this valley. You grin like an idiot.

Weir check (fast): **five fish**. Two to pile 6; three for lunch later. Heron absent.

### Design — how big is "big"?

You and the assistant argue over the cleared pad (80 × 60 cm). You wanted **big**. The assistant counts clay:

| Component | Clay needed (wet) | You had (start of day) |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Pit lining (1 m × 45 cm, 4 cm wall) | ~35 kg | 22 kg |
| Seal cap for first burn | ~10 kg | — |
| **Total for one firing** | **~45 kg** | **short** |

Verdict: build a **1 m diameter** pit (big for week one, not circus-scale). Haul clay first, or build skeleton and gather — you choose **gather → build → fire**.

### Clay haul (morning)

**Five trips** to clay bank and red seep (90–110 m, loaded 10–12 min each):

| Trip | Haul | Running total |
|------|------|---------------|
| 1 | 6 kg grey | 28 kg |
| 2 | 7 kg grey | 35 kg |
| 3 | 5 kg red + grey | 40 kg |
| 4 | 6 kg grey | 46 kg |
| 5 | 4 kg grey | **50 kg** |

Reed basket + bark cup + arms. Trail T-1 feels like a road now.

### Wood haul (midday)

Charcoal wants **dense, dry oak** — not willow trash. You hit terrace deadfall and oak slope above camp:

| Source | Collected | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Dead oak (split with hammerstone + wedge flake) | **38 kg** | Arm-thick to wrist; 30–60 cm lengths |
| Prior staged chunks (pile 5) | **12 kg** | From Day 4 |
| **Charcoal load staged** | **50 kg** | Dedicated stack beside pit |

General firewood pile drops; shelter poles **untouched** (11 still).

### Building the charcoal pit (afternoon)

You expand the pad into a proper **pit kiln**:

| Spec | Measurement |
|------|-------------|
| Outer rim diameter | **100 cm** |
| Depth | **45 cm** |
| Clay wall thickness | **3–5 cm** (grey clay inside; red at base patch — experiment) |
| Air inlet | 8 cm slot at south edge; plug ready (stone + clay) |
| Liner stones | 4 flat slabs at base for drainage gap |
| Collar | Raised rim 10 cm — **for seal cap** |

Hands shape walls for two hours. Clay goes fast — **38 kg into structure**. **~12 kg wet reserve** left when stacking starts.

Wood loaded **vertical-ish** around a central airway — tight but not stupid; assistant warns air-starved corners make unburnt cores. You pack **~46 kg** of the 50 kg stack; 4 kg kept as emergency fuel.

**Not built yet:** the "pretty big" **phase 2** ring you sketched on the map stone — outer 1.4 m collar for double batches. Not enough clay. Marked for Day 6+.

### First large firing (evening)

You transfer coals from the cooking fire with bark tongs (split stick). Add tinder, then feed. The pit roars — orange on clay walls you built today. You sit on the bed pit lip and watch wood collapse for **three hours**, feeding the inlet until the stack sinks and glows steady.

At dusk: plug inlet with stone + clay. **Cap** with remaining clay mixed with earth — hand-wide seal, then earth mound **80 cm × 30 cm** over the top. Hiss. Steam. Then quiet.

**Batch #1 (large) is running.** Open tomorrow.

You eat three fish and pistachios by the cooking fire (separate — smart). Sleep in bed pit but check the mound once at midnight — warm, intact.
