## Events

Same goals. You say them out loud like a prayer: **trap, food, fire, storage, bed.** Fire you already have — today is keeping it, using it, and building the boring scaffold everything else hangs on.

### Dawn — fish trap

You check the weir before the sun hits the water. The funnel **worked**, barely: **four minnow-sized fish** and a frog in the cage end you lashed shut yesterday with willow bark. Not a feast — you cook them whole on a flat stone at the pit edge and eat them with your fingers, bones and all. Protein is protein; your lazy character accepts the terms.

You spend an hour **widening the funnel stakes** (spacing closer — your fist test was too generous) and adding a second row on the downstream side so nothing learns the trick. The trap is now something you'd call *a trap*, not *an intention*.

### Trails (slow, deliberate)

You are not building roads. You are **marking decisions** — where your feet should go until buildings exist.

| Trail | Route | Markers | Purpose |
|-------|-------|---------|---------|
| **T-1 River** | Camp → clay bank → fish weir | Stacked cairns every 20 steps | Water, clay, protein daily |
| **T-2 Plain** | Camp → emmer patch → olive slope | Broken branch cuts on oak | Farm loop, P-02, seed plot |
| **T-3 Flat** | Camp → downstream onto river flats (northwest bearing) | Reed bundles tied to stakes | Survey, future building zone |

You clear brush on T-1 only — knee-high nettles and deadfall — enough that you won't trip carrying clay. T-2 and T-3 get markers first; clearing waits. The assistant records bearings from the map stone.

### Survey — where things go (including the circus)

You climb to the best view point on the terrace lip and spend the afternoon **drawing territory**, not building it.

**Camp (now):** Correct. Terrace above flood line, rock back wall, fire pit, piles — keep compact.

**Future buildings:** You pick a flatter shelf **downslope 80 m** — closer to river chores but still above flood stain. Assistant notes Romans will pack the low city between mountain and river; your shelf is a reasonable pre-industrial workshop zone before you deserve a whole house.

**Farm / fence:** Plain edge where you found emmer — **~200 m west**, patch roughly 15 × 8 paces cleared today (digging stick, miserable). Stake row started; no cord left for lattice yet. Birds are the enemy. You plant nothing yet — clearing and fence first.

**Trails meet farm at T-2 junction** — you scratch a **J** on the map stone.

**The Roman circus (guess):** You ask because you're lazy and want one fixed landmark in the mental movie. The assistant walks you through it:

- Future Antioch sits where the Orontes **leaves the gorge** and can be crossed and channelled on the plain.
- The famous hippodrome — ~**513 m long**, seats for tens of thousands — goes on the **Insula / "Island"** district: river flats **northwest of the city core**, where the Orontes splits around alluvial land. Long axis roughly **north–south**; chariots want flat ground and a water channel (*euripus*) down the spine.
- From your camp: follow T-3 onto the flats, northwest ~**400–600 m** to where the plain opens widest toward the haze — low ground, soggy in places, long straight reach parallel to the river. You drive a **marker stake** there: *circus?*

You know it's absurd at 10,000 BC. The plain is marshier than Rome's engineers would tolerate. You don't care. You have a stake named **Circus** and a trail to it. When you eventually build something stupid and monumental, you'll know where.

### Storage

Pile 8 was getting raided by moisture and ants. You fix **rudimentary storage**:

- **Dry shelf:** Flat stones stacked under the rock back wall overhang — clay lumps, seed jar, chert A-grade, green rock.
- **Snail / shell bin:** Woven **reed basket** (C-1 cordage, embarrassing geometry but holds). Snail meat smoked briefly on rack of sticks above coals — experiment; most eaten fresh today anyway.
- **Buried cool:** Small clay-lined pit for reed bundles and willow bark — covered with flat stone; marked on map as **B-1**.

Not a pantry. Not a root cellar. **Stuff has addresses now.**

### Bed (shame reduction project)

Last night was tolerable. Today you upgrade:

- More grass packed firm in corners
- Reed mat doubled

Verdict: you could show it to a Natufian and receive a shrug, not laughter. **Mission partially accomplished.**

## Assistant

**You:** How confident are you on the circus stake?

**Assistant:** **Directionally** confident, not GPS-confident. Insula hippodrome was ~513 × 113 m, north of the city core on river island/flats — modern Antakya's Dalyan area. At 10,000 BC the hydrology differs; marshes shift. Your stake marks **intent**: flat alluvial reach, northwest of gorge mouth, long axis plausible. Romans will move earth for centuries; you're picking a field they would have wanted leveled. Good enough for a lazy immortal's city plan.

**You:** Am I spreading too thin?

**Assistant:** You're surveying wide and building narrow — that's correct. Trails and map cost almost nothing. Don't plant emmer until fence holds. Don't build a house until storage and food aren't daily crises. The circus stake is psychological infrastructure. Allowed.
