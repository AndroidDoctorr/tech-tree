# Day 2

## Events

You wake with a list. The assistant gets it in one breath: *base, piles, tools, catalog, eat.*

### Choosing the base

You walk the terrace edge at first light. Day 1's overhang was fine for one night; today you pick **home**:

- Above flood stain (checked again after last season's high-water marks on willows)
- Flat enough for sleep pit + fire pit + sorting yards
- River in view, approach visible, clay bank reachable downhill in ten minutes
- Partial rock back wall — rain shadow for your piles

You drag a boundary line in the gravel: **camp**. Same respawn rules; you just upgraded the anchor. You scratch a numbered map on the back-wall stone — pile numbers, river bearing, gorge east — so future-you doesn't forget your own system.

### The sorting yards

You spend most of the morning hauling and **sorting everything** into labeled piles:

| # | Pile | Contents |
|---|------|----------|
| 1 | **Clay** | Grey-brown sticky bank clay from riverside cut; rolls into worms; another redder clay from a seep — two types, unknown firing behavior |
| 2 | **Cordage stock** | Reed stems, willow bark strips soaking in eddy, oak inner bark, grass clumps, dead nettle-ish stalks (handled carefully) |
| 3 | **Flint/chert** | Knappable cobbles graded A/B/C by glassy interior exposure |

| 6 | **Food** | Pistachios, grass seeds, berries (tentative), snail shells emptied, nothing cooked yet |

| 8 | **Edible unknowns** | Things you won't commit to stomach yet |

Anything ambiguous goes to pile 8 until the catalog clears it.

**Sheep / goats:** On the far slope toward the gorge you glass (with hand shade) **two horned silhouettes** — assistant says likely **wild goat** (*Capra aegagrus*), not domestic. They see you and vanish. No flocks; no shepherds; no tame animals. Protein here is fish, snails, traps, and eventually a very stupid goat — not today.

**Olives:** Yes, nearby — see P-02. Not grocery-store olives; scrub trees, small fruit, months from anything pleasant.

**Grapes:** Yes — P-03; sour and green.

You scratch **P-01 through P-11** on the camp map stone and start a **seed selection jar** (clay-lined hollow in rock, covered with flat stone): largest emmer, einkorn, barley, and flax seeds set aside for a deliberate plot once you have fire and a fence strategy.

### Camp infrastructure

- **Fire pit:** Dug 40 cm circle, knee-deep, lined with flat stones from river; draft channel upslope. Empty until evening.
- **Bed pit:** Shallow depression (hip depth), cleared of stones. You lined the floor with layered **reeds**, then **willow switches** crosshatched, then armfuls of **dried grasses**. Still crude; first night you'll know. You consider grape leaves and oak moss — assistant says moss holds moisture (bad); stick with grass/reed.
- **Pitch/tar:** You climb toward pines on the north slope and collect **Aleppo pine** (*Pinus halepensis*) resin blobs in a bark cup. Not true "pitch boiling" yet — raw resin. Sticky, smells like civilization trying. Goes to pile 5 sub-basket for adhesive experiments.

### Fire

Last light. You have C-1 on the bow, tinder bundle of dry grass + shredded bark, hearth notch positioned over bark tray.

First run: smoke, brown powder, no coal.  
Second: **coal** — you tip it into nest, blow until your head spins — **flame**.

You nurse it into the stone-lined pit. Nothing to cook but you feed it reeds and feel absurdly wealthy. The digging stick tip finally chars properly in coals. You sit on the bed pit edge and eat **pistachios** roasted on a flat stone at the margin. They taste like effort.

Fish trap untended overnight — you hope; no point without a weir complete.

## Assistant

**You:** Are those olives actually useful or am I fantasizing?

**Assistant:** Real trees, real species — wild *oleaster*. Raw fruit is bitter and low yield. Useful over **years**: oil if you learn processing, wood is fine, selection for larger fruit is exactly how domestic olive happened. You're not fantasizing; you're just on a multi-year branch of the tree.

**You:** Sheep?

**Assistant:** What you saw was almost certainly **wild goat**. Mouflon-style wild sheep exist in the region but prefer higher open country. No domestic flocks without humans. Trapping/goat is a long-term project — fish and snails are your near-term protein.

**You:** Rye?

**Assistant:** Unlikely here at 10,000 BC. Your "nice grass" on the plain is probably **barley or brome**. Emmer and einkorn are the jackpot for early agriculture in this valley.

**You:** That green rock?

**Assistant:** Keep it. Could be weathered **copper carbonate**. You can't smelt it yet; when you can, it's a hint that you're not far from ore in geological time — maybe days of walking into the mountains, not hours.
