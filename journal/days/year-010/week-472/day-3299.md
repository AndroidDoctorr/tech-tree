## ☠ ★★★ Then I lit a rag and the whole design argued with itself

**Smouldering rag at the hood lip, forge cold. The smoke went in, but slowly — it hung at the lip and thought about it before it committed.**

**Lit the forge and tried again.** ☠ **It curled out. Straight out over the lip and into the shelf, which is where I would have been standing.**

★★ **Sat down with it and the answer was in the design before I ever laid a brick.**

> ☠ ★★★ **A FLUE DRAWS BECAUSE THE GAS IN IT IS HOT AND WANTS TO RISE. THAT IS THE WHOLE ENGINE.** ★★★ **And I have deliberately built a flue whose entire purpose is to make the gas COLD.**

☠ **It is not one mistake. It is every feature at once:**

| What I built it for | ☠ **What it did to the draw** |
|---|---|
| **Fourteen metres of length, so the vapour cools and drops** | **Cool gas does not rise. I spent the engine to get the product** |
| **A widening, so the gas slows and the dust falls out** | **Slow gas does not pull. The chamber is a brake by design** |
| **A close low hood, so nothing escapes at the source** | **A tight hood needs MORE pull, not less** |

> ☠ ★★★ **EVERY SINGLE THING THAT MAKES THIS FLUE COLLECT MAKES IT DRAW WORSE, AND I OPTIMISED ONE SIDE OF A TRADE I DID NOT KNOW WAS A TRADE.**

## ★★★ The resolution — it has to do both jobs, so it must do them in different places

**The tempting fix is a fire at the base of the stack to get the gas moving.** ☠ *And my first thought was that this destroys the point, because heating the flue is exactly what stops it collecting.*

**Which is true of heating the flue. It is not true of heating the END of it.**

> ★★★ **THE VAPOUR ARRIVES LOADED AND LEAVES SPENT. Those are two different gases in two different parts of the same pipe** — *and I have been asking every yard of it to do both jobs at once.*
>
> ★★★ **COLD AT THE NEAR END, WHERE IT IS STILL CARRYING SOMETHING. HOT AT THE FAR END, WHERE ALL THAT IS LEFT TO DO IS PULL.**

✓ **So the flue is not wrong. It is unfinished, and it is unfinished at the wrong end from where I was looking:**

- ⧗ **Carry the run further up the slope and raise the final stack** — ★★★ **height is the only currency that buys draw without costing collection, because it does not care what temperature the gas is**
- ⧗ **A small draught fire at the stack base, past the settling chamber** — *it pulls the whole line and never touches the loaded end*
- ⧗ **Re-read with the rag, forge lit, before anything else happens here**

★ **A day's more work. No brick wasted.**

## ☠ ★★★ And I tested it against the competition, not on its own

**The cold-forge read was marginal and I would have accepted it. It was the second read that told the truth.**

> ★★★ **A HOOD THAT DRAWS BEAUTIFULLY ALONE AND SPILLS WHEN THE FORGE IS LIT HAS NOT BEEN TESTED.** *The forge is not an interference with the test. The forge is the condition.*

★★ **That is the d3282 reading turned into a procedure rather than a complaint** — ☠ *every stack here is an exit with no entrance, so any two draws compete* — ★★★ **which means the only honest way to read any of them is with everything else running.**

⚑ **Standing rule: draught reads happen under full campus load, or they do not count.**

## ★★★ Two days running I have tested a thing empty, and two days running it has paid

☠ **Yesterday the jars had no seed in them. Today the hearth had no lead in it.**

> ★★★ **IF I HAD LEARNED THIS FLUE'S DRAUGHT WITH A BATH OF MOLTEN LEAD ON THE BED, I WOULD HAVE LEARNED IT BY BREATHING IT.** *The smoke that curled over that lip would have been litharge fume, and it would have been the first run, and I would have been leaning over it watching the bath.*

★★ **I did not do this because I was being careful about lead. I did it because the rag was to hand and the hearth was not finished.** ☠ ★★★ **Which is worse, not better — it means the good outcome was luck wearing the clothes of a method.**

⚑ **So make it a method: `PROVE-IT-EMPTY`.** ★★★ **Any vessel, flue, seal or containment gets its first real test with nothing valuable and nothing dangerous inside it** — *and the test is not a formality, because it has now caught something on both of the two occasions I have run it.*

## Discoveries

1. ☠ ★★★ **EVERY FEATURE THAT MAKES A CONDENSING FLUE COLLECT MAKES IT DRAW WORSE.** *Length cools the gas and cool gas does not rise; a widening slows the gas and slow gas does not pull; a tight hood needs more pull, not less.* ★★★ **I optimised one side of a trade I did not know was a trade, and built the best collector and the worst chimney on the property.**
2. ★★★ **IT HAS TO DO TWO OPPOSITE THINGS, SO IT MUST DO THEM IN DIFFERENT PLACES.** *The vapour arrives loaded and leaves spent — two different gases in two parts of one pipe.* ★★★ **Cold at the near end where it still carries something; hot at the far end where the only job left is to pull.** ☠ **I was asking every yard of it to do both.**
3. ★★★ **HEIGHT IS THE ONLY CURRENCY THAT BUYS DRAW WITHOUT COSTING COLLECTION** — *because it does not care what temperature the gas is.* ★ **Everything else on the list trades one against the other.**
4. ☠ ★★★ **A HOOD THAT DRAWS ALONE AND SPILLS WHEN THE FORGE IS LIT HAS NOT BEEN TESTED.** *The forge is not interference with the reading — it is the condition.* ⚑ **Draught reads happen under full campus load or they do not count.**
5. ☠ ★★★ **`PROVE-IT-EMPTY` — and the good outcome today was luck wearing the clothes of a method.** *I used a rag because the hearth was unfinished, not because I had a rule.* ★★★ **Two for two on catching something: the jars yesterday, the flue today** — ★★ **so it becomes a rule now, before the third time is the one with lead in it.**
