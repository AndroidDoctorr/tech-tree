## ✓ Domus hearth — and there are two different things called an inlet

**Spot tile first, before touching anything.** ◐ **Grey, not black.** ✓ *Adequate, as read on d3302 — it wants the duct, it is not dangerous.*

**Then nearly cut the same hole I cut in the forge, and stopped.**

> ★★★ **IN A WORKSHOP THE DIFFERENCE DOES NOT MATTER AND IN A BEDROOM IT IS THE WHOLE THING.**
>
> ★★ **One kind of inlet ventilates the ROOM and lets the fire take what it needs out of the room's air.** ☠ *In the forge that is right, and I ducted it behind the anvil so it did not blow on the work.*
> ★★★ **The other kind feeds the FIRE and never enters the room at all.**

☠ **A low room inlet in the domus is a cold draught across the floor, all winter, every winter, in the place I sleep** — ★★ *and I would have blocked it by February and forgotten I had.*

> ☠ ★★★ **AN INLET I WILL STUFF WITH A RAG IN JANUARY IS NOT AN INLET. IT IS A HOLE WITH AN INTENTION.**

✓ **So: ducted through the wall and delivered under and beside the firebox itself, discharging into the hearth's own air path.** ✓ **Fired-clay grille outside, coarse aperture, laid to fall so water runs out** — ⚠ *an opening is a rodent door until it is grilled, and the domus is where the food is.* ✓ **Throttle inside, on the duct, not on the grille.**

**Relit and read: flame stands up, tile comes back near-clean, and the throat holds with the shutters closed.**

⚑ **BC-2 amended:** ★★ **workshop — a room inlet is acceptable · occupied space — DIRECT TO THE FIREBOX.**
