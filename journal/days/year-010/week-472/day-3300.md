## ✓ The height, and the fire at the far end

### ★★ A second fire that makes nothing

☠ **The draught fire burns the whole time the hearth runs and produces nothing whatever.**

> ★★★ **WHICH IS THE FIELD COIL AGAIN.** *The armature does the work and rests; the field does no work and never stops.* ★★★ **Both machines needed a part that produces nothing and cannot be removed** — ★★ **and I keep meeting that shape and keep being mildly offended by it.**

## ☠ ★★★ And I found most of the rest by putting my hand on the flue

**Walked the length of it afterwards while it was still warm, and the first four or five metres out of the hood are furred on the inside.** *I could feel the deadness of it through the brick, and a knuckle on the rodding plug at the joint came back grey.*

☠ **A fair part of what I did not catch is not gone. It is stuck to the inside of my flue, in the stretch where the gas cools hardest.**

> ☠ ★★★ **I BUILT FOURTEEN METRES OF PIPE WHOSE ENTIRE PURPOSE IS FOR THE PRODUCT TO STICK TO THE INSIDE OF IT, AND I DID NOT PUT A SINGLE DOOR IN IT.**

★★ **The chamber has a hatch. The chamber has a hatch because the chamber is where I expected the dust to end up.**

> ☠ ★★★ **BUT THE DUST IS WHERE THE COOLING IS, AND THE COOLING IS THE WHOLE LENGTH — THE CHAMBER IS ONLY THE LAST STAGE OF IT.** ★★★ **I put the door where I expected the product instead of where the process puts it** — *and the design had already told me, because the flue is long precisely because long is where it cools.*

★★ **The answer was inside the reason for the thing.**

⚑ **Rodding doors at every ~2 m of the cooling run, brick-stopped and lime-sealed between sweeps.** ⧗ **A half-day.** ★ **On the numbers, that moves recovery from ~42% to something near two-thirds without touching the draught at all.**

☠ ⚠ **And a flue sweep is a lead job, not a chimney job** — **wet, downwind, kit on, sweepings to the same pan as the chamber.**

## Discoveries

1. ☠ ★★★ **DRAW HAS A CEILING AS WELL AS A FLOOR, AND THE FAILURE AT THE TOP LOOKS LIKE SUCCESS.** *Strong pull, clean hood, nothing spilling — and the whole charge going out of the stack.* ★★★ **The target is a band, not a direction, and I had spent two days travelling in a direction.** ✓ **Damper and an adjustable draught fire — two controls.**
2. ☠ ★★★ **I PUT THE DOOR WHERE I EXPECTED THE PRODUCT INSTEAD OF WHERE THE PROCESS PUTS IT.** *The chamber got a hatch; the fourteen metres of deliberately-cooling flue got nothing.* ★★★ **The dust is where the cooling is, and the cooling is the whole length** — ★★ **and the design contained the answer, because the flue is long precisely because long is where it cools.**
3. ★★★ **I CANNOT SEE WHAT GOES UP THE STACK, SO WEIGH WHAT DOES NOT.** *Lead in, bead out, sweepings out, and the shortfall is the loss.* ★★ **Third measurement this month taken by weighing the thing's complement** — *the desiccant, the coulometer, now the flue.*
4. ★★ **A SECOND FIRE THAT MAKES NOTHING, AND IT CANNOT BE REMOVED.** *The same shape as the shunt field — the part that does no work and never rests.* ⚑ **Overhead is structural; a design with none is usually a design that is not finished.**
5. ★ **Put the stack on top of the rise rather than on top of the flue.** *The draw is the difference between the ends, and the hill was already there* — **free height for the price of nine metres of horizontal run.**
