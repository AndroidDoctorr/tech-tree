## ☠ ★★★ Morning — I was about to fit hinges

**Set out to cut rodding doors into the cooling run, every two metres, and had got as far as sizing the frames when I stopped.**

☠ **A door in a flue is a hole in a flue.**

**Worked through what a hole does there, and the first answer was reassuring.** ★ *With the draught fire lit the whole run upstream of it is under suction — so a leaking door draws air IN, which is harmless, and slightly helps the cooling.*

> ☠ ★★★ **AND THEN THE SECOND ANSWER: THAT IS ONLY TRUE WHILE A FIRE IS BURNING SOMEWHERE ELSE.** *Let the draught fire go out with a hot bath still on the bed — or simply light the hearth before the stack is established — and every one of those doors is an opening in a pipe full of lead fume, at my head height, along the path I walk.*

★★ **The obvious answer is an order of operations: draught fire first and hearth second, hearth out first and draught fire last.** ☠ ★★★ **But that is a rule I have to keep, every time, for years — and the thing it protects me from is silent and has no smell.**

> ★★★ **SO DO NOT BUILD A DOOR. BUILD A WALL THAT I HAVE AGREED TO BREAK.**
>
> ✓ ★★★ **Bricked and limed shut in normal running, broken open at every sweep, re-laid afterwards.** ★★ **It costs ten minutes of mortar per sweep, and it does not MANAGE the failure mode — it removes it, because while the hearth is lit those are not doors, they are flue.**

☠ **I was about to fit hinges because hinges are convenient, and convenience was going to buy me an eleventh way to poison myself quietly.**

✓ **Eleven rodding openings cut, bricked, limed.** ⧗ **Next sweep will tell me what the walls were holding.**

## ☠ ★★★ Afternoon — the inlet audit, and it says something about the last nine years

**Took the new number to the other two stacks** — *free inlet area of about half again the throat* — **and measured what they actually have.**

| Stack | Inlet as built | ✓ **Read** |
|---|---|---|
| **`KILN-D`** | **None designed — it stands in an open lane with a gappy shelter** | ✓ **Draws beautifully. Always has** |
| ☠ **Forge** | **None designed — and it now has proper walls, a fitted door and a glazed light** | ☠ **Sluggish. Rolls smoke back at the hood on a still day, and today was still** |

**Stood there with a smouldering rag at the forge door and opened it.** ★ **The smoke snapped straight up the stack.** ✓ **Shut it. The stack gave up again.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE FORGE DRAUGHT WAS NEVER DESIGNED. IT WAS A BY-PRODUCT OF HOW BADLY THE WALLS FITTED** — ★★★ **and I have spent nine years carefully fixing the walls.**

★★ **The crude one works and the good one does not, and I built them in that order, each time proud of the improvement.**

> ☠ ★★★ **EVERY TIGHTENING WAS AN IMPROVEMENT ON ITS OWN TERMS AND A WITHDRAWAL FROM AN ACCOUNT I DID NOT KNOW EXISTED.** *Nobody was ever wrong on the day. The door was a good door. The glazing was a good window.* ★★★ **The draught was spending a budget that nothing in the build was tracking.**

★ **And this corrects d3282 rather than confirming it.** ☠ *I wrote then that every vent here is an exit with no entrance, and asked why nothing had failed yet.* ★★★ **This is the answer: it had entrances. They were accidents, and I have been repairing them.**

✓ **Sized low inlet cut into the forge north wall, ducted low and behind the anvil line so it does not blow on the work** — ★ *cold air on a fire is good and cold air on a smith is not.* ✓ **Damper flap for winter.** ✓ **Re-read with the rag and the door shut: it draws.**

⚑ ★★★ **And this is a BC-2 clause, not a repair.** *Every enclosed space containing a fire gets a sized, dedicated, low combustion-air inlet — named in the build, not left to the joinery.*

## Discoveries

1. ☠ ★★★ **EVERY TIME I HAVE MADE A BUILDING TIGHTER I HAVE MADE ITS FIRE WORSE, AND I HAVE BEEN DOING IT FOR NINE YEARS WITHOUT NOTICING.** *The crude open kiln draws beautifully; the properly walled forge rolls smoke back.* ★★★ **The draught was never designed — it was a by-product of how badly the walls fitted, and I have been carefully fixing the walls.**
2. ★★★ **NOBODY WAS EVER WRONG ON THE DAY.** *The door was a good door. The glazing was a good window. Each tightening was an improvement on its own terms* — ☠ ★★★ **and a withdrawal from an account that nothing in the build was tracking.**
3. ★ **This corrects d3282 rather than confirming it.** *I asked why nothing had failed when every vent is an exit with no entrance.* ★★★ **They had entrances. The entrances were accidents, and I was repairing them.**
4. ☠ ★★★ **THE SAFE DOOR IS THE ONE THAT IS NOT A DOOR WHILE THE HEARTH IS LIT.** *Under draught the flue is under suction and a leak is harmless — but that is only true while a fire is burning somewhere else, and fires go out.* ★★★ **Bricked and limed shut, broken open each sweep: ten minutes of mortar removes the failure mode instead of managing it.**
5. ☠ ★★★ **I WAS ABOUT TO FIT HINGES BECAUSE HINGES ARE CONVENIENT.** ★★ **A procedural safety is one I must keep every time for years, against a thing with no smell** — ★★★ **prefer the version where forgetting is impossible over the version where forgetting is forbidden.**
6. ★ **Cold air on a fire is good and cold air on a smith is not** — *inlet ducted low and behind the anvil line, with a winter damper.*
