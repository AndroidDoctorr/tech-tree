## ☠ ★★★ The gypsum, and the window I never mapped

**Took the ~2.1 kg of spent calcined gypsum to the bread oven to re-burn it, being very careful about the narrow window** — *gentle, bread-oven heat, not kiln heat, because over-roast is dead.*

**Stood there watching it and something did not add up.**

☠ **"Dead" means it will not set. That is what over-roasting costs me: a plaster that will not go hard.**

> ☠ ★★★ **BUT I AM NOT MAKING PLASTER. I AM MAKING SOMETHING THAT WANTS WATER SO BADLY IT WILL PULL IT OUT OF SEALED AIR.** ★★★ **"It will not set" and "it is hungry for water" cannot both be failures. They are the same fact, and which one is the fault depends entirely on the job.**

★★★ **And then the real thing: there are not two states of this stone. There are three, and I have only ever deliberately made two of them.**

| Heat | State | ★ **Good for** |
|---|---|---|
| **Raw** | **The stone as dug** | Field dressing · nothing else |
| ★ **Gentle · bread-oven** | **Sets hard cold in minutes** | ★★ **PLASTER.** *This is the narrow window and I mapped it carefully* |
| ★★★ **Hotter, and past where I have always stopped** | ☠ **Will not set — and is far hungrier** | ★★★ **DESICCANT.** *The state I actually want, and I have never once made it on purpose* |
| ☠ **Kiln heat, far past** | **Inert. Will not set and will not take up water either** | **Nothing. This is the only real "dead"** |

> ☠ ★★★ **I HAVE BEEN STOPPING AT THE PLASTER WINDOW AND CALLING EVERYTHING BEYOND IT RUIN, WHEN THE NEXT ROOM ALONG IS THE ONE I NEEDED.** ★★ *The caution was correct. It was just borrowed from the other job.*

✓ **Ran three small trials past the plaster window at increasing heat, re-wet a pinch of each to confirm it would NOT set** — ★ *the failure to set is the proof of success here, which took some getting used to* — ✓ **then took the batch to the hottest of the three that still crumbled rather than sintered.**

⚑ ★★★ **Fire straight into the jars, hot, lids on as they come.** ☠ *Every minute in open air is capacity spent drying the atelier.*

## ★★★ The seal test — and the pass is a flatline, not a number

**Four jars. No seed in any of them.**

> ☠ ★★★ **I CANNOT TRUST A JAR I HAVE NEVER TESTED WITH THE ONLY COPY OF SOMETHING.** *And the charge is a hundred and eighty-eight days out at the November emmer cut, which is exactly long enough to find out.*

✓ **Each jar: a weighed open dish of fresh charge, ~120 g, sealed, and the whole jar weighed.** ✓ **A fifth dish, identically charged, left OPEN on the bench as the control.**

**Then I had to work out what a pass even looks like, and it is not what I assumed:**

| Reading | ★ **Means** |
|---|---|
| **Gains fast, then STOPS** | ✓ ★★★ **PASS.** *It dried the air that was sealed in with it, then had nothing left to find* |
| ☠ **Gains slowly and never stops** | ☠ ★★★ **LEAK.** *It is drying the room through a gap, one breath at a time* |
| **Gains at the control's rate** | ☠ **The lid is doing nothing at all** |
| **No gain from the start** | ⚠ **The charge is dead — re-burn went too far** |

> ★★★ **I AM NOT MEASURING A NUMBER. I AM MEASURING WHETHER IT HAS STOPPED CHANGING** — ★★★ **and that is the second test this month with the same shape, because `BASALT-DATUM` passes the same way: two successive readings that agree.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **NEITHER TEST CAN BE PASSED BY A SINGLE READING, HOWEVER GOOD IT LOOKS.** *A jar weighed once is a jar with an opinion about it.* ★★ **Weekly, and the flatline is the certificate.**

⧗ **Weigh every seven days. A jar that flatlines twice running is cleared for the ark.**
