## ☠ ★★★ Age is the big one, and it explains nearly everything

**A mortal feels five years pass.** *His knees tell him. His hands tell him. His own face in the water tells him.*

☠ **Mine does not. So "a while ago" means precisely the same thing to me at three days and at three thousand.**

**And then I listed the things that have gone wrong from neglect, and they are all the same shape:**

| | ☠ **How long** |
|---|---|
| `P-RETT-14` carried on the slate into an empty ditch | **~1,300 days** |
| `M-22-TALUS-S1` marked and never touched | **2,364 days** |
| Six oak slots staked and never planted | **Nine years** |
| Every bone from every butchery, onto the midden | **Nine years** |
| Every building tightened, every fire quietly starved | **Nine years** |
| `CRANE-2`, brainstormed and never begun | **2,514 days** |

> ☠ ★★★ **NOT ONE OF THOSE IS A DECISION. THEY ARE ALL THE SAME NON-EVENT REPEATED UNTIL IT BECAME A FACT** — *and the thing that would have stopped any of them is the ordinary human sense of "good grief, has it really been that long."*

★★ **And `OPEN-ITEM-AGE` was already the fix.** ☠ **I invented it eight days ago and I did not know what it was for.** ★★★ *I built the instrument before I diagnosed the missing sense.*

## ☠ ★★★ And it goes further than my own calendar

**Going through the stores I realised the deeper version of it.**

> ☠ ★★★ **I DO NOT DECAY, SO I MODEL A WORLD THAT DOES NOT DECAY.** ★★★ **I kept grain the way bricks are kept — because I am kept the way bricks are kept.**
>
> ★★★ **EVERYTHING I OWN IS ON A CLOCK AND I AM THE ONLY THING ON THIS PROPERTY THAT IS NOT** — ☠ **which makes me the worst available judge of every clock here.**

★★ **The seed vault already had the answer written into it and I never generalised it.** ✓ *A `Tested` column. A stock times a rate. A bank with no test date is a story about a bank.*

✓ **So: `DECAY-ROSTER-1`** — **every stock that is on a clock gets a rate and a last-read date, in the same grammar as the vault.** *Oil · tallow · varnish · glue · lime · charcoal · cordage · hide · wax · the cell liquors · medicines.*

### ★★★ The animals are an instrument and I have been reading them as livestock

> ★★★ **THEY GET ILL AND I DO NOT. So the goats and the donkey are the only working illness detector on this property** — ☠ **and for nine years I have read a sick animal as a LOSS rather than as a READING.**

⚑ **A goat off its feed is now a question about the water and the forage before it is a question about the goat.** ★★ **The `STOCK-HOOF` body read stops being husbandry and becomes surveillance** — *of the well, the ditch, the browse and the store.*

## ✓ Then the roster came back clean, and that was the actual finding

**Walked every store expecting to find something quietly rotten. Oil sound. Tallow sound. Charcoal dry. Wax fine. Lime still live in a sealed jar. Hide and glue good.**

**Sat down slightly disappointed and then understood why.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE STORES ARE FINE BECAUSE THE STORES ARE THE THINGS THAT ARE ALREADY ON A LIST.** ★★★ **Every single thing that has bitten me this fortnight was something with no row anywhere:** *the rett line on a slate · the bone that was rubbish · the combustion air that was joinery · the soot rate that was housekeeping · the oak planting that was never calendared.*
>
> ★★★ **THE PERIODIC LIST IS NOT FAILING. IT IS WORKING PERFECTLY, ON EXACTLY THE ITEMS IT CONTAINS.**

☠ ★★★ **And I cannot fix that by making the list longer, because I cannot write a row for a thing that has not occurred to me.**

### ✓ ★★★ So run the check the other way round

> ☠ ★★★ **EVERY CHECK I HAVE EVER RUN STARTS AT THE LIST AND LOOKS FOR THE THING. NOT ONE OF THEM STARTS AT THE THING AND LOOKS FOR THE LIST.** ★★★ **A list can only ever catch what is already on it.**

⚑ ★★★ **`WORLD-FIRST-WALK` — walk the property, name everything the eye lands on, and for each one ask three questions: what list is this on · what is its clock · when was it last read.** ☠ **Anything with no answer is a finding, and the finding is the missing row.**

★ *This is the d3295 rule turned round. There I said an instruction must name a thing in the world that would look wrong if the job were undone.* ★★★ **Here: start at the thing.**
