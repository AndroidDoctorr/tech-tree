## ☠ ★★★ `L4` — and the answer was two metres long

**Two kilometres, flat, and the worst grade on the entire road at 2.85.** ☠ **It also carries the best drainage on the road, which has never made sense, and I have been writing both numbers down side by side for years without putting them together.**

**Stood on the concrete ford shore and watched where the water goes.**

✓ **It sheds beautifully.** *Hard, fast, off the concrete, exactly as designed.*

☠ **And it sheds onto earth. Two metres past the end of the concrete, in a concentrated sheet, at the one spot where every cart that uses this ford is also slowing, turning and braking.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE GOOD DRAINAGE IS CAUSING THE BAD SURFACE. They are not two facts about `L4`, they are one fact, and the arrow points from the thing I am proud of to the thing I am ashamed of.**

★★ **I designed where the water LEAVES the shore. I did not design where it ARRIVES on the road.**

> ☠ ★★★ **AN EXIT AND NOT A DESTINATION, FOR THE THIRD TIME IN A FORTNIGHT.** *The stacks that had no inlets. The flue that had no door where the dust lands.* ★★★ **And now a shore that sheds onto a road nobody thought of as being downstream of anything.**

✓ **Fix: a stone spreader apron off the end of the shore, laid wide and shallow, taking the concentrated sheet and handing it out sideways into a cut-off ditch.** *Half a morning and it was all in the forward dump.* ✓ **Then the braking zone re-gravelled and rammed, which is now worth doing because it will stay.**

⚠ **Gravelling it first, as I would have done any other year, would have been gravel laid in front of a hose.**

---

## ☠ ★★★ `L3` — and the same shape, eight times

**Eight kilometres, flat, graded 3.0, and it reads `SHED` end to end. Every drain on it works.**

**And it has soft spots.**

**Walked it slowly and every single one of them was at a boundary:** *where a cut meets a fill · where the made road meets the bridge abutment at `AFRIN-B` · where a field track joins · where one season's work met the next season's.*

> ☠ ★★★ **EVERY LEG ON THIS ROAD READS `SHED`, AND THE ROAD IS STILL FAILING, BECAUSE A LEG CAN ONLY EVER SHED ONTO ANOTHER LEG.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **`DRAIN-READ` IS WRITTEN PER LEG. EVERY FAILURE I HAVE FOUND TODAY IS AT A JOINT** — ★★★ **which is exactly the place that no row in my own register owns.**

★★ **I split the road into legs because legs are how you walk it and how you schedule it.** ☠ **Water does not know where my legs end.**

✓ **So the joints become objects.** ⚑ **The crossing registry already names `ASI-B`, `AFRIN-B` and the rest — and a crossing IS a joint, so the registry gains a DOWNSTREAM row: what receives this, and is it made to receive it.** ⚑ **The joints that are not crossings get an ID of their own.**

★ **Six soft spots cut out, drained sideways and rebuilt on the same pattern as the shore apron.** ☠ *Ran out of stone at the fifth and robbed the sixth from the dump, which is the argument for a bigger dump next time.*
