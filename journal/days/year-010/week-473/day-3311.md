## ☠ ★★★ The fire itself — and thickness is not strength

**Long climb. ⚠ Hot still air and a lazy chimney, and it reached stoneware late and by a margin I did not enjoy.** ⚑ *Noted — see below.*

**Out at the end: ✓ `RETORT-A` sound. ✓ `RETORT-B` sound and ringing. ☠ `RETORT-C` cracked through. ☠ `RETORT-D` cracked through.**

✓ ★★★ **Which is the variant method doing exactly what it was built to do, and I want to be clear with myself that losing two is not the failure — not knowing which two would be.**

☠ **But look at WHICH two.** ★★★ **`A` and `B` are the thin-walled pair. `C` and `D` are the thick.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE THICK ONES DIED AND THE THIN ONES LIVED, WHICH IS THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT "STRONG" MEANS EVERYWHERE ELSE I WORK.**
>
> ★★★ **A thick wall in a fire is not one wall. It is a hot face and a cold face, tied together, pulling against each other** — *and the thicker it is, the further apart those two temperatures can get, and the harder they pull.*
>
> ★★★ **THICKNESS DOES NOT RESIST THERMAL SHOCK. THICKNESS CAUSES IT.**

★★ **Everywhere else on this campus, more material is more margin.** ☠ **In a fire it is more gradient, and the gradient is the load.**

⚑ **So the acid retort is to be as thin as I dare form it**, *and the instinct that has served me on every beam and every wall on this site is precisely the wrong instinct here.*
