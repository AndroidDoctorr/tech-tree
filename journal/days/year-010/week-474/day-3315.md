## ★★★ Rennet does not set milk the way acid sets milk

**Ten years of acid curd and I had assumed rennet was a better version of the same act. ☠ It is not the same act at all.**

> ★★★ **ACID DOES NOT SET MILK — IT WRECKS IT INTO A SOLID.** *The protein is driven out of suspension and lands where it lands, and what I get is a heap of curd that has no structure because it was never built, only precipitated.*
>
> ★★★ **RENNET CUTS ONE SPECIFIC THING AND THEN STANDS BACK.** *It snips whatever it is that keeps the protein apart, and the protein then knits ITSELF into a continuous fabric, with the fat and the water held inside the weave.*

☠ ★★★ **And that is the whole of why acid curd can never be aged, melted or stretched.** ★★★ **There is nothing in it to age. A heap has no grain. A fabric does.**

✓ **The break confirmed it — ★ a clean glassy split under the knife with sweet whey running off it, not the ragged crumbling I get from vinegar.** ★★ *I have made cheese for ten years and this is the first time the curd behaved like a material rather than a sediment.*

## ✓ Salt is not seasoning

★ **Salted to weight, carefully, from `SALT-CRYSTAL` stock, and I want to note why rather than how.**

> ★★★ **SALT IS THE CONTROL ROD.** ✓ *It pulls water out, it slows the bacteria down, and it sets the rind going.* ★★★ **The rate I salt at is the rate the cheese will age at** — ☠ **so salting is not a finishing step, it is me choosing a clock.**

☠ **Under-salt and it runs away from me and rots. Over-salt and it stops and never ripens at all.**

## ☠ ★★★ The cave — and the instrument I already owned

**`CAVE-3` read all day against the yard. ✓ Outside swung eleven degrees. ★ The shelf moved by something I could barely detect.**

✓ ★★★ **It is a better aging room than I had any right to expect, and the handrail I put in at d3304 means I can actually use it.**

☠ **But temperature is the easy half.** ★★★ **A cheese cave is really a humidity problem** — *too dry and the rind cracks and the whole wheel turns to a stone; too wet and it goes to slime and the wrong moulds take it.* ☠ **And I have never owned a way to measure the wet of air.**

## ✓ The rind, and the thing I nearly got wrong

⚑ **Wanted to wax the wheel immediately.** ☠ **The Assistant stopped it, and it was right.**

> ☠ ★★★ **A RIND IS NOT A COATING I APPLY. IT IS A SKIN THE CHEESE GROWS, BY DRYING ITS OWN OUTSIDE UNTIL THE OUTSIDE BECOMES THE THING THAT CONTROLS THE DRYING.**
>
> ☠ **Wax it now and I seal a wet wheel inside a jacket and come back in four months to something I will not want to open.**

✓ **Bare on the shelf until the rind forms. ⧗ Wax after — and the wax is beeswax, which I have because of the bees, which I have because of the seed plants.**

⚑ **Turning goes on the periodic checklist with a first action and a date, per d3313** — ★★ *else it is exactly the kind of rule that dies in the glow.* ☠ **An unturned wheel settles its moisture downward and ages wet at the bottom and dry at the top, and by the time that shows it is four months old and it is not fixable.**
