## ✓ ★★★ And the loose joint turns out to be a thermal break

**Set the retort and the receiver up dry to check the sand joint, and saw something I had not paid for.**

☠ **The receiver has to be COLD — that is its whole function, it is where the vapour gives up and becomes liquid.** ☠ **And it hangs off the neck of a vessel I am holding at red heat.**

> ★★★ **WHICH IS THE WORST THERMAL GRADIENT IN THE WHOLE RIG, AND IT IS EXACTLY THE THING THAT KILLED `RETORT-C` AND `D`** — *a hot face and a cold face tied together and pulling against each other.*
>
> ☠ ★★★ **A RIGID JOINT BETWEEN A HOT VESSEL AND A COLD ONE IS TWO TEMPERATURES BOLTED TOGETHER.** ✓ **A loose one packed with sand is two pieces free to be at two temperatures, and sand does not transmit a strain.**

★★★ **I chose the loose joint because a tight one would burst. It turns out a tight one would also have cracked.** ✓ **The sand seal is doing three jobs and I bought it for one:**

| | |
|---|---|
| ✓ **No organic lute in the path** | *Nothing for the fume to eat* |
| ✓ **Cannot hold pressure** | *Blows a channel before it bursts a vessel* |
| ✓ ★★★ **Thermal break** | ★★★ **The hot end and the cold end stop tearing at each other** |

> ★★★ **THE SAFEST ANSWER AND THE STRONGEST ANSWER TURNED OUT TO BE THE SAME ANSWER, AND I ONLY WENT LOOKING FOR ONE OF THEM.**

## ☠ ★★★ Asked what happens after it works, and found the real gap

⚑ **Put the question to the Assistant before firing anything: suppose the distillation goes perfectly. ☠ Then what am I holding, and what do I put it in?**

☠ **And there is no answer on this campus.** ★★ *Not a mistake — the acid has never existed, so the bottle has never been needed.* ☠ **But it is needed the same hour the acid does exist, and not an hour later.**

★ **Strong acid eats or is spoiled by every stopper I own: cork, wax, leather, wood, the clay-and-dung lute.** ✓ **The vessel itself is easy — glass, which I have.** ☠ **The CLOSURE is the problem, and it is the joint problem for the third time in three days.**

> ★★★ **THE ONLY THING THAT SEALS GLASS AGAINST THIS ACID IS GLASS.**

✓ ★★★ **And grinding two surfaces against each other with abrasive until they fit is the one thing I have spent this whole year learning to do.**

> ★★★ **I BUILT THE LAP FOR MACHINE TOOLS AND IT IS THE ANSWER TO A CHEMISTRY PROBLEM.**
>
> ★★ **And it is the same principle as everything else this fortnight** — ☠ *I do not grind the stopper to a measurement.* ★★★ **I grind it against ITS OWN BOTTLE until the two of them agree, and the fit is guaranteed by the method rather than checked afterwards.**

## ☠ ★★★ It fitted. Then it would not come out.

✓ **Ground a stopper into a bottle neck with fine grit and patience. ✓ The fit came up beautifully — even, silky, no rock in it.**

☠ **And then it seized, and I spent a quarter of an hour getting it back out of a bottle I did not want to break.**

**Sat with that, because it is not a bad fit. ☠ It is too good a one.**

> ☠ ★★★ **A PERFECT TAPER HAS NO AIR PATH, SO PULLING THE STOPPER MEANS PULLING A VACUUM BEHIND IT.**
>
> ★★★ **THE THING THAT MAKES IT SEAL IS THE SAME THING THAT MAKES IT STICK. THEY ARE NOT TWO PROPERTIES — THEY ARE ONE PROPERTY, READ FROM TWO ENDS.**

✓ **Fix is to spoil it on purpose: a shallow groove ground down the length of the taper.** ★★ **Too small to pass liquid or let the fume drift, wide enough to let air follow the stopper out.** ✓ **Came free with a finger after that, and still sealed.**
