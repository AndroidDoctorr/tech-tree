## ★★★ Yes — but only red ochre, and only the part that makes it red

**The red in both materials is ferric oxide: hematite, `Fe2O3`.**

> ★★★ **RED OCHRE IS EARTH COLORED BY HEMATITE. ROUGE IS HEMATITE PREPARED TO POLISH.**

☠ **Those are not the same material merely because they share the same red.** Natural red ochre carries clay, quartz, sand and whatever else the deposit grew around. Rouge has to be clean, fine and narrowly graded.

★ **Yellow ochre is the useful comparison.** Its color usually comes from goethite, `FeO(OH)` — iron oxide carrying hydroxyl. Heat it and it gives up water and becomes hematite, turning red.

> ★★ **So the family resemblance is real:** yellow ochre → roast → red hematite pigment; copperas → dehydrate and decompose → red hematite residue. **The chemistry supplies the color. Preparation decides whether the result is paint, dirt or polish.**

## ☠ Yesterday I named it one operation too early

**Raw `COLCOTHAR-1` was red and fine-looking, but it still carried soluble sulfate and particles of every size the retort happened to make.**

☠ **Put that straight onto glass and one coarse grain would write its name across the whole face.**

✓ **Washed repeatedly until a dried drop of the decant left no visible salt crust. Then levigated: stir, let the heavy grains fall briefly, pour the red suspension away, and let the finest fraction settle in a clean bowl.**

✓ **The separation came up exceptionally clean:**

- **`ROUGE-FINE-1`** — the slow-settling fraction, very fine and even
- **`IRON-OXIDE-PIGMENT-1`** — the early-settling fraction, still useful red pigment but not trusted on a finished surface

> ★★★ **COLCOTHAR IS ROUGE FEEDSTOCK. LEVIGATION MAKES IT ROUGE.**

## ✓ The glass test — it worked, and it did almost no work

**Took clean cullet, put the same `LAP-GRIT-4` haze across the whole face, then worked only half with wet `ROUGE-FINE-1` on clean linen.**

✓ **The grey haze cleared. A reflected cord went from a broad blur to a sharp line. No new scratches appeared.**

☠ **The old scratches remained. Even after more work, the rouge barely moved them.**

> ★★★ **THE FINEST ABRASIVE REMOVES THE LEAST MATERIAL.**
>
> ★★★ **ROUGE DOES NOT MAKE A BAD SURFACE GOOD. IT MAKES A GOOD SURFACE FINISHED.**

★★ **That is not a poor abrasive. It is a finishing abrasive.** The coarse grade establishes shape; each finer grade removes the marks of the one before; rouge removes the final haze. Skip a stair and the last stair cannot reach down to fetch it.

---

## ✓ The brass test — brighter, but not automatically better

**A trace of rouge with oil on linen brought the coupon bright quickly.** It also made every older file line and hammer mark easier to see, because the flat areas brightened before the low marks disappeared.

> ★★ **POLISH DOES NOT HIDE THE SURFACE. IT REVEALS IT.**

☠ **And a polished patch beside six months of warm brass would look like a repair, not an improvement.**

✓ **Norima stays untouched.** If her trim is polished, all eight pieces should be done as one refinishing pass, followed by the same protective finish. That is a choice about her appearance, not an abrasive test.

## ✓ Retort stock — but not four copies of an unproven answer

**The rouge spent most of the morning settling, which left the hands free for clay.**

★ **The obvious production choice was four copies of `RETORT-B`, because it rang best after the stoneware fire. The Assistant objected correctly:** `B`'s extra grog is proven against thermal shock, not against acid attack. More grog can also mean more exposed interfaces for acid to enter.

> ☠ ★★★ **DO NOT TURN A FIRING WINNER INTO A PRODUCTION STANDARD BEFORE IT HAS SURVIVED THE PRODUCT.**

✓ **So the stock is split, with geometry held constant:**

| Retorts | Body | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **`RETORT-E` · `F`** | ~7 mm · ~¼ grog | Production copies of `A` |
| **`RETORT-G` · `H`** | ~7 mm · ~⅓ grog | Production copies of `B` |

**All four:** shallow ~30° neck · bulb and neck thrown separately · scored slurry joint · fillet both sides.

✓ **Bodies and joints came up even.** Damp-cloth wrapped, crated out of the breeze, necks wrapped slightly heavier than bulbs to keep the drying rate together.

⧗ **Bone-dry target ~d3330.** `RETORT-B` remains the next acid run and the clean comparison; its etch against `A` decides which pair becomes the long-term standard.

## Discoveries

1. ★★★ **RED OCHRE AND ROUGE SHARE HEMATITE, `Fe2O3`, BUT NOT A JOB.** *Red ochre is earth colored by hematite; rouge is hematite cleaned and graded to polish.* ★ **Yellow ochre is usually goethite, `FeO(OH)`, and roasting drives off water and turns it into red hematite.**
