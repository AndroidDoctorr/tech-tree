> ★★★ **THE RATE DID NOT NEED GRAMS. THE CONTROL SUPPLIED THE SCALE.**

**Jar 2 breathes much more slowly than an open dish, Jar 3 more slowly again, but both continue moving.** That is enough: a sealed jar is supposed to exhaust its sealed air and stop. Continuing gain is room air crossing the closure.

☠ **The word “slow” does not rescue Jar 3.** The ark is meant to hold seed for years; a small weekly leak is a large annual exchange.

✓ **Jars 1 and 4 have one flat interval.** They need one more unchanged read at d3327 before certification, per the original rule.

⚑ **Jars 2 and 3 are rejected for charge, their closures marked for rework, and their tests restart from zero after resealing.**

## ☠ ★★★ The cave instrument is not an instantaneous hygrometer

**The cave dish gained strongly against the identical open dish on the workshop bench.** The cave is materially wetter than the workshop over the same five-day interval.

**But the gypsum never gives an instantaneous answer. It only gains, accumulating every wet hour until it is re-burned.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THIS DOES NOT MEASURE HUMIDITY. IT MEASURES HUMIDITY TIMES TIME.**
>
> ★★★ **IT IS A RAIN GAUGE FOR AIR, NOT A THERMOMETER FOR AIR.**

★ **That still makes it useful.** With the same charge, exposed area and interval, it compares shelves, seasons and ventilation changes. It cannot report relative humidity, and a five-day gain cannot say whether the cave was moderately damp throughout or very damp for one day.

⚑ **Renamed `CAVE-HYGRO-1` → `CAVE-HUMIDITY-DOSE-1`.** Re-burn and expose for fixed seven-day intervals. Compare it with a matched workshop control every time.
