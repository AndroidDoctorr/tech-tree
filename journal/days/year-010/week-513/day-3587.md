### ⚠ **First sleeve geometry failed honestly**

Mixed an electrical ceramic body from **~85% dry M26 kaolin + ~15% fine forsterite grog**. Formed the first planned sleeve at **36 mm long × 10 mm OD**, around twin **2.0 mm waxed mandrels** with a **2.0 mm centre web**.

The sleeve looked clean until the mandrels came out. One dragged before the other, sheared the thin centre web, and split the piece lengthwise.

**Nothing fired and nothing lost:** body re-wedged immediately.

> ⚠ **A long twin bore is two tools trying to leave one fragile body at once.** The failure was green shear, not bad clay and not bad drying.

## Discoveries

| ID | Notes |
|---|---|
| ☠ **COPPER–CONSTANTAN IS NOT THE STEEL-HEAT PROBE** | It is useful below roughly 400 °C and wrong for prolonged annealing heat: copper oxidizes, drifts, then melts before the melt furnace's upper range. ★ **First probe becomes iron–constantan for the muffle**, while `FURNACE-2` keeps cones until an optical method exists |
| ★★ **SEGMENTATION IS COMPLIANCE, NOT A COMPROMISE** | A one-piece ceramic tube must survive forming, firing, installation and every expansion mismatch as one brittle object. Short beads move at their joints and a failed segment costs one segment, not the probe |
| ★★★ **THE COLD JUNCTION IS PART OF THE INSTRUMENT** | Copper joined to iron and copper joined to constantan make two extra junctions. They cancel only when both sit at the same known temperature |
| ★ **THE GALVANOMETER READS ZERO, NOT TEMPERATURE** | The potentiometer opposes the thermocouple until the galvanometer reads nothing. Null removes lead and contact resistance from the answer |
