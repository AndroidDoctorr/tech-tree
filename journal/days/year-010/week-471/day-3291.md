## ✓ The fix, and it pays for itself

**Did not improvise.** ★ **I already own the design — I built it on Thursday.** **Scaled it down and put it where I can watch it.**

✓ **`ORE-BAY-1`**, west of the forge, off the yard slope:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Base** | **Puddled clay pan**, sloped to one corner. *Same floor as every leach I own* |
| **Bed** | Coarse stone over the clay, so liquor runs under the rock rather than through the floor |
| **Kerb** | Dry-laid stone, one course, to stop rain sheeting in off the yard |
| **Catch** | **Sunk lined pot at the low corner**, lidded |
| **Cover** | ✓ **Roofed — and this is the opposite of the heap.** ☠ *The heap WANTS rain. This is working stock I mean to smelt, and I do not want it rotting before it reaches the furnace* |
| **Separation** | ⚑ **Galena in its own kerbed bay, marked.** *Lead does not mix with the general ore and it does not get handled with the same hands* |

⧗ **Half a day. Clay, stone, one pot, and a lean-to off the forge wall.**

> ★★★ **AND THE CATCH POT IS NOT A WASTE FITTING. IT IS A SECOND VITRIOL SOURCE, AT HOME, WHERE I CAN LOOK AT IT.** *The Kisecik heap is a farm I visit twice a year and cannot observe.* ★★ **This is the same reaction at a tenth of the scale with my eyes on it every day** — ⚑ **so the first thing I learn about how fast that liquor builds, I learn here, ninety days before I open the sump up there.**
