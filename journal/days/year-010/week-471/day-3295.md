## ☠ ★★★ Then I went down to the ditch with the snap test in my hand

**My slate says `P-RETT-14`, day 12, snap test today.** *It has said something like that for a while. I walked down there with the bundle already half-pictured — how it would feel, whether the fibre would ring off the boon clean.*

☠ **Both troughs are empty. Rinsed, bars stacked, dry as a shelf.**

**Nothing has been in that water since `P-RETT-27` closed on d3136.** ☠ **One hundred and fifty-nine days.**

⚠ **And `P-RETT-14` is not even a stale entry for the current arc — it is from Y6.** *Loaded d2023, pulled d2038, broken and closed d2044.* ☠ **Twelve hundred and fifty-one days.** *I have been copying forward a line about a bundle that finished before the Atelier had a roof on it.*

☠ ★★ **Worse, and I may as well write it down: I ticked it on the farm pass four days ago without going down there.** *The slate said day 9, I was busy with the ore bay, and a line that has been true four hundred times in a row does not get checked the four hundred and first.*

### ★★★ The reason, and it is in the shape of the journal itself

**Every day I write closes with an Open block, and every morning I copy yesterday's Open block forward into today's.** *That is the whole mechanism. It is why nothing gets dropped.*

> ☠ ★★★ **AND IT IS ALSO A MACHINE FOR MAKING OLD THINGS LOOK CURRENT.** *A line copied four hundred times is written in this morning's hand, in this morning's ink, in this morning's list.* ★★★ **It is indistinguishable from a line written once, today, about something real.**

☠ **Third time in a month, and it is the same failure wearing a third coat:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **d3285** | **The seed test was calendared in my own hand above the sow gate** — and caught nothing, because ★ *a tick mark cannot be wrong* |
| **d3286** | **Kisecik sulfur was indexed as `M-14` with a road already built to it** — while I planned 117 km for sulfur |
| ☠ **Today** | **A rett bundle with a day count on it, in an empty ditch** |

> ☠ ★★★ **THE REGISTER LOOKS EXACTLY THE SAME WHETHER IT DESCRIBES THE WORLD OR MERELY REMEMBERS IT.** *There is no mark on the page for "this was true once."*

✓ **Fix, and it is one column wide: every open item carries the day it was OPENED, not just its text.** ★★ *`P-RETT-14 · opened d2023` would have screamed off the slate the first morning I copied it into Y7.* ★★★ **Age is the only thing that distinguishes a live line from a ghost, and I have been throwing it away every single morning.**

⚑ **Next load is the wild flax pull, 26 Jun. The troughs are correct to be empty. Only the slate was wrong.**

## ★★★ `GEN-WW-2` — the magneto becomes a dynamo

**Stood in the wheelhouse and looked properly at the field for the first time since d3261.**

☠ **`GEN-WW-1` is a magneto. Its field is ×6 hardened steel rods in an iron yoke, and a steel rod holds what a steel rod holds.** *That is a fixed asset. I bought it once, and from the day I magnetised it, the only direction it can go is down — heat takes a little, shock takes a little, years take a little.*

> ★★★ **SO WIND COILS ON THE POLES AND FEED THEM FROM THE MACHINE'S OWN OUTPUT.** ★★★ **The field stops being something the machine CARRIES and becomes something it EARNS, continuously, for as long as the water runs.**

### ★★ And it starts itself, on the ghost of the last time it ran

**Iron remembers.** *When I kill the field, the yoke does not go to nothing — it keeps a trace.*

★★★ **That trace is enough.** *Spin the armature and the remembered field makes a trickle. The trickle goes round the field coils and strengthens the field. The stronger field makes more than a trickle. It climbs up its own output and does not stop until the iron saturates.*

> ★★★ **THE MACHINE BOOTSTRAPS ITSELF OUT OF THE MEMORY OF ITS LAST RUN** — *and the ceiling is no longer what steel can hold, it is what the iron will take.*

☠ **The failure mode is the same fact inverted, and it is worse than not starting.** ⚠ **If I wire the field so it opposes the residual instead of reinforcing it, it does not merely fail — it ERASES the memory, and the next attempt starts from less than this one.** *The water can be roaring and the machine makes nothing, and every minute it spins makes it harder to ever start.*

✓ **So: scribe the polarity into the yoke with a chisel before a single turn goes on.** ★ **And keep `DANIELL-CELL-1` on the bench to flash the field back in if I kill it.**

### ★★★ The thermal constraint, which is the whole design

⚠ **The coating is wax-rosin and it softens hot.** *Known since the coil work — it is written on my own limits list.*

☠ ★★★ **And a shunt field is the worst possible place for that, because the field coil is the one winding that NEVER RESTS.** *The armature does the work and gets a rest between loads. The field does no work at all and runs every second the wheel turns.*

> ★★★ **THE COIL THAT RUNS ALL THE TIME IS NOT THE ONE DOING THE WORK.**

**Sat with it and the answer is better than I expected, because the field does not care how it gets what it wants:**

> ★★★ **A FIELD WANTS AMPERE-TURNS, AND IT IS A PRODUCT — IT DOES NOT CARE WHICH OF THE TWO FACTORS CARRIES IT.** ★★★ **Heat cares enormously.** *Few turns at high current and many turns at low current make the same field, and only one of them cooks the varnish.*

✓ ★★ **Many turns of fine wire, low current, air between the layers, not potted solid.** ☠ *And that is exactly what a shunt field is supposed to be anyway* — ★★★ **so the thermally safe answer and the electrically correct answer are the same answer, which almost never happens.**

★ **Better still: fine wire is the CHEAP answer in copper.** *Six hundred metres at 0.5 mm is about a kilogram. The same six hundred metres at 0.9 mm is nearly three.*

### ⧗ And the bottleneck is a kind I have never had

**Reckoned the wire: ~600 m, ~1 kg. I have `CU-BAR-Y10-1` at ~390 g and odds and ends that will not stretch.** ⧗ **So ~600 g to make, and `CU-CELL-1` and `CU-CELL-2` make ~50 g a day between them for two looks a day.**

☠ **Twelve days. And there is nothing whatever I can do to make it eleven.**

> ★★★ **EVERY BOTTLENECK I HAVE EVER HAD WAS "GO AND GET IT" OR "LEARN HOW."** *Both of those reward working harder, and I am good at working harder.* ☠ ★★★ **This one is a RATE. The copper arrives at fifty grams a day whether I stand over the cell or walk away from it for a fortnight.**
>
> ★★★ **Which means the correct response is to go and do something else entirely — and that is a discipline I have genuinely never needed before.**

✓ **Started the draw with what I have. Cell tended. Poles measured and the yoke scribed.**

## ★★ And the thatch is a precision problem, which I had filed as a weather problem

**Walked past the machine covers on the way back and saw them differently.**

☠ **They are thatch. Temporary, put up years ago, and [BC-2](../../../../government/regulations/building-code-2.md) retires thatch outright** — *do not build, do not spec, do not mention.* **The only thatch left on this campus is over my most tolerance-sensitive equipment and over the wagon.**

★ **I had this filed as "replace when convenient — it keeps the rain off."** ☠ **It is not a rain roof.**

> ★★★ **A ROOF OVER A MACHINE IS A TEMPERATURE ROOF.** *Sun on one side of a lathe bed and shade on the other is not a warm patch — it is a BEND, and it is a bend that comes and goes with the hour of the day.* ★★★ **A uniform temperature I can measure and correct for. A gradient just quietly makes the work wrong.**

★★ **That is the honest limit of the comparison trick.** *If the standard and the workpiece sit at the same temperature, the expansion cancels exactly and I need no clever alloy.* ☠ **But that only holds for UNIFORM temperature.** ★★★ **A machine with a hot end and a cold end is not a measurement I can correct — it is a machine that is the wrong shape while it runs**, *and no amount of comparing will fix it.*

⚑ **So the cover replacement moves off the cosmetic list and onto the precision list**, ★ **and it wants the same canvas as the wagon cover** — *which is the July hemp, and which is why the yarn has to be even before it arrives.*
