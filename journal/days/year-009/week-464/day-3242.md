## TOP-BAR-HIVE-4 · **✓ built** — *and it is the SWARM BOX*

**One build, two jobs.** **The wax fix and the swarm catcher are the same object, and only one of them has a deadline.**

### Form · **horizontal, sloped-sided**

| Choice | Why |
|--------|-----|
| **One long horizontal box** — not stacked | **Nothing heavy ever gets lifted.** I work along it a bar at a time |
| **SIDES SLOPE INWARD** | **This is the trick I nearly missed.** With vertical walls the bees **weld the comb to the sides** and it will not lift. **Slope the walls in and the comb hangs FREE of them** |
| **Volume** | **~40 L.** Not arbitrary — **scout bees judge a cavity by size**, and this is the size they like |
| **Follower board** | **A movable partition.** A swarm in a huge empty box cannot heat it. **Shrink the space to the colony and slide it back as they grow** |
| **Entrance** | **Small and low** — defensible against wasps and robbers, and it keeps mice out |
| **Roof** | **Deep overhang · **selenite skylight rejected *(condensation would soften it — under an eave only)* |

### The bars · **the whole instrument**

| Spec | |
|------|---|
| **Width** | **~33 mm, centre to centre** — natural worker-comb spacing |
| **Bee space** | **~8 mm** kept at every edge — **wider and they comb it, narrower and they glue it shut** |
| **Guide** | **A bevelled ridge down the underside of each bar, rubbed with WAX.** Without a guide they build diagonally across the bars and nothing lifts |
| **Fit** | **Shoulder to shoulder — the bar row IS the ceiling.** No gaps, or they build up through them |
| **Check** | **BARS-COMB-GUIDE-READ HIT** — ran a straightedge across the whole row, **every bar sits true and level.** **One proud bar and the comb line goes crooked from there on** |

**Cut a **spare set** while the jig was set up. **The jig is the expensive part, the bars are not.

## CONSULT · **the nickel answer** *(filed — this changes several things at once)*

### Constantan — **closer than expected, and I do not need to know the recipe**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | **Roughly half copper, half nickel. **Copper I have in quantity |
| **Nickel** | **Sulfide ore at Dörtyol** — roast to oxide, reduce with charcoal. **Harder to reduce than copper**, but the kiln reaches it |
| **The part that matters** | **I do NOT need to know the correct percentage.** Cast a **series of buttons** at stepped ratios, **measure each hot and cold on the bridge, and take the one that moves least.** **Find the minimum by experiment — reproducible beats true, again** |
| **Test aid** | **Nickel is magnetic.** A magnet sorts ore and checks a melt |
| **SAFETY** | **Roasting sulfide gives SULFUR DIOXIDE — choking, genuinely dangerous in a confined space.** **Outdoors, upwind, never in the wing** |

### **THE BIG ONE — the nickel trip is also the ACID trip**

> **Roasting sulfides is how sulfuric acid gets made.** **My acid bank is **4.3 ml** and bulk leaching wants **kilograms**. That gap has been the hard ceiling on the whole chemistry tree.**

| Route | Read |
|-------|------|
| **Vitriol distillation** | **Weather or roast sulfide ore to a SULFATE, then distil the sulfate in a strong retort → oil of vitriol.** **No lead chamber needed — just a retort that survives, and I can fuse glass now** |
| **Lead chamber** | **Blocked — Pb ~15 g.** Would need sheet lead by the kilo |

**This reframes Dörtyol entirely. It is not a nickel trip with an acid bonus — it may be the other way round.**

### Platinum — **stop looking for it**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it was used** | **Only because it **happens to expand like soda glass**. **Nothing magic — it is a MATCHING problem |
| **Two ways round it** | **1. **HOUSEKEEPER SEAL** — a feather-thin copper edge, so thin it yields instead of cracking the glass. **A skill, not a purchase** · 2. **MATCH THE GLASS TO THE METAL INSTEAD.** I make my own glass — **change its composition |
| **And the third, which is the joke** | **An iron-nickel alloy at roughly 45–50% nickel expands like soda glass.** It was invented **precisely as the platinum substitute for lamp seals.** **The same trip that gets me constantan gets me the seal alloy** |

### Thermometers — **the glass is not the hard part**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The hard part** | **BORE UNIFORMITY.** A capillary that varies in bore gives a **lying scale** |
| **The test** | **Exactly the slide-wire test again:** introduce a **short thread of mercury** and walk it along the tube, **measuring its length at each station.** If the length changes, the bore changes. **Reject the tube or mark the scale non-uniformly to suit** |
| **Fixed points** | **Melting ice** and **boiling water** — but **boiling moves with the weather**, so **a barometer and a thermometer are the same project** |
| **Ranges** | **Mercury freezes around −39** — **use ALCOHOL for the cold end.** Mercury for the hot |

And the news about Dörtyol has been rattling around my head all day, because I went in thinking of it as a nickel trip. Nickel gets me a resistance alloy that does not drift, and — this is the good part — an iron-nickel that expands like my glass, which is the whole reason anyone ever wanted platinum.
