## BC-2 AMENDMENTS · **the code has fallen behind the campus**

**Read BC-1 straight through against what I can actually do now. **Most of it holds. Three things are missing and one of them is serious.

### THE SERIOUS ONE · **BC-2-SEISMIC — confined masonry**

> **I live on a live fault, and I am not going to die.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The recalibration** | **A damaging quake here runs about **once in 125 years |
| **Which means** | **"Solid for the next few decades" is the wrong question.** In a century I am probably fine. **In a millennium I will bury this campus eight times.** **I should not build for my lifetime, because I do not have one** |
| **What BC-1 has** | **Single-wythe brick with a bond beam at the head.** That is **unreinforced masonry** — it carries weight beautifully and **it has no answer at all to sideways shaking** |
| **The fix** | **CONFINED MASONRY.** Brick panels **boxed in by reinforced concrete tie-columns and tie-beams.** The panel does the carrying; the frame **stops it coming apart**. I have **pozzolanic concrete** and **iron** |

#### The detail that decides whether it works

> **BUILD THE BRICK PANEL FIRST. CAST THE COLUMN AGAINST IT SECOND.**
>
> **Leave the brick ends **toothed** — stepped in and out course by course — and pour the concrete into those teeth. **The concrete keys into the masonry and the two become one thing.
>
> **Cast the column first and butt brick against it and you have built a post standing next to a wall.** Same materials, **none of the benefit.** This is the whole trick and it is entirely about **order of operations**.

#### The rest of the seismic slate

| Rule | |
|------|---|
| **Closed ring** | **Tie-beam at the head must be CONTINUOUS and tied into every column** — it is a **ring**, not four lintels |
| **Plinth beam** | **Tie the column bases together at the socle too**, or the feet splay |
| **Rebar** | **Modest bar is plenty. **HOOK THE ENDS** — plain round bar bonds poorly** · **twisted square bar bonds far better and I can twist bar** |
| **Cover** | **25–40 mm of concrete over every bar.** Iron in concrete rusts, **rust swells, and swelling spalls the concrete off**. **Lime-pozzolan is alkaline and passivates the iron — but only where it actually covers it** |
| **Openings** | **No window or door within about half a wall-height of a corner.** Corners are where shear failure starts · **every opening properly lintelled with generous bearing past the jamb** |
| **Panel length** | **Limit unbraced wall run — **a column or cross-wall at intervals, not one long unsupported panel |
| **Roof tie** | **Tie the roof structure DOWN to the ring beam.** An untied roof simply leaves |
| **Roof mass** | **TR tile is heavy and mass up high is exactly what you do not want.** Keep it, but **brace and tie the roof frame properly** |

#### Retrofit — **and no, do not rebuild**

> **The buildings are sound. Rebuilding is enormous and unnecessary. RETROFIT, in this order:**

| Priority | Work |
|----------|------|
| **1** | **RING BEAM.** A continuous reinforced band round each building at wall head. **Highest value single intervention on unreinforced masonry, by a long way** |
| **2** | **CORNER TIES.** External buttress-columns at the corners, **tied through the wall.** Not as good as cast-against-toothed-brick, **far better than nothing** |
| **3** | Plinth tie at the socle where accessible |
| **4** | Render + plaster *(below)* |

**New builds: full confined masonry from the first course.**

### BC-2-DAMP · **the thing that must happen BEFORE plaster**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Missing from BC-1 entirely** | **A damp-proof course** |
| **Why it matters now** | **Plaster over a wall that wicks groundwater will blister and crumble at the base, and gypsum plaster is slightly SOLUBLE — it will simply dissolve out** |
| **The fix** | **A continuous barrier course in the socle** — pitch bed, or a course of dense tile bedded in pitch. **Blocks capillary rise** |
| **Rule** | **NO INTERIOR PLASTER ON ANY WALL WITHOUT A DPC BELOW IT.** *That is a hard gate, not a recommendation* |

### BC-2-RENDER · **the grout coat, and he was right**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Never plaster straight onto brick** | **The finish follows every irregularity, the key is poor, and **suction differs between brick and mortar joint so the set comes out patchy |
| **Scratch coat** | **Rough lime-sand render, pressed hard into the joints, SCRATCHED while green** to key the next coat |
| **Fibre goes IN the mix** | **Chopped flax tow through the base coat** — that is what haired plaster is, and I have tow |
| **Scrim — half right** | **Stretching cloth over a whole solid wall is waste and can DEBOND.** **Scrim belongs over JOINTS and material changes** — and the most important one is now obvious: **over the junction between a brick panel and a concrete column, because that is exactly where the crack will be.** Also over lath and in corners |
| **Interior vs exterior** | **GYPSUM PLASTER IS INTERIOR ONLY** — it is not waterproof and it re-wets. **EXTERIOR RENDER IS LIME, always.** No exceptions |
| **Substrate** | **DAMP THE WALL FIRST** or it drinks the water out of the plaster and you get chalk |

### BC-2-PRECISION · **now that I can measure**

| Rule | |
|------|---|
| **Master straightedge · master square · plumb · water level** | **A water level is two vessels and a tube and it is dead accurate over any distance** — better than any straightedge for setting a foundation |
| **Square by the FLIP TEST** | **Scribe against the square, flip it, scribe again. If the lines diverge, the square is out by HALF the divergence.** **A square that checks itself — no external standard needed** |
| **Mortar by MEASURING BOX** | **Batch by volume in a box, not by eye.** Consistent mortar is most of consistent masonry |
