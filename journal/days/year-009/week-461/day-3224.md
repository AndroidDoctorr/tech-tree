## GYPSUM-ON-SOIL · reasoning before I spread anything

| Question | Read |
|----------|------|
| ****Calcium?**** | ****Almost certainly not the limit.** I live on limestone. **The whole valley is calcium** — the beds are cut into it, my mortar comes out of it, the cave is dissolved out of it. **Adding calcium to calcareous ground does approximately nothing** |
| ****Sulfur?**** | ****This is the real argument.** Sulfur *is* a food the plants need and it is **not** supplied by the rock here. **Pulses and alliums want it most** — and **lentil, chickpea, fava and allium are all in the ground right now** |
| ****Does it burn?**** | ****No.** It is **poorly soluble and does not shift the ground sour or sweet** — unlike lime, which swings it. **Gypsum is close to impossible to overdose**, it just sits and releases slowly. **Low risk** |
| ****The crust**** | ****This may be the biggest effect of all.** My heavy ground **seals into a crust after rain** and the seedlings have to fight through it. **Gypsum makes clay crumb instead of seal** |
| ****Against**** | ****I can turn this rock into plaster, which nothing else I own can do.** Feeding it to a field that may not need it is **spending a rare thing on a cheap job** |
| ****Resolution**** | ****SORT IT.** Clean white → **plaster**. Gritty, dirty, unsortable → **the field**. The field gets the waste stream, not the good stock |
| ****And TEST it**** | ****A strip trial, not a blanket dressing.** Half a run dressed, half left bare, **same bed, same day** — CI-1 grammar. **On limestone ground the effect may be invisible, and I will not know unless I leave myself a control** |

### The selenite is the surprise

| Read | Notes |
|------|-------|
| ****It is transparent**** | ****I can see through it.** Thin sheets peel off flat and clear, a little cloudy but **you can read a hand through them** |
| ****Which means WINDOW**** | ****A pane.** Not glass — I have no flat glass and no way to make it — but ****a translucent pane that lets daylight into a room and keeps weather out**** |
| **And a lantern** | **A **shielded lamp** that does not gutter and cannot be knocked into straw |
| **Limit** | ****Soft, and water will soften it.** Under an eave, not exposed. **It will not take a knock.** But for a **north light over a bench** it is enormous |
| ****And it proved the chemistry**** | **Held a clear sheet in the heat: ****it went cloudy white and opaque as the water left it.**** **I watched gypsum become plaster.** The same reaction, but **visible** |
