## SALT-ID-1

**Two things to test, not one.** The leach separated them for me without my meaning it: **what dissolved** and **what did not**. I went back to the discard heap and recovered **~2.1 kg of leach residue** — *the part I threw away is half the answer.*

### A · the RESIDUE *(the insoluble bulk)*

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| ****Fingernail**** | ****Scratches with a thumbnail.** Soft — softer than lime rock, softer than my nail **—** **GYPSUM-SCRATCH-CONFIRM HIT** |
| ****Vinegar**** | ****NO fizz. None.** This is decisive: **chalk and limestone boil under vinegar**, and this sits there. **It is not a carbonate** |
| **Solubility** | **Barely dissolves — **which is exactly why it stayed behind while the bitter salt washed through** |
| ****Gentle roast**** | **Powdered it, roasted **gently** on a warm hearth-stone until it stopped steaming. Went to a **dry white flour** |
| ****RE-WET**** | ****Mixed the white flour to a batter — and it STIFFENED IN MY HANDS AND SET HARD IN MINUTES.** Set **cold**, set **fast**, took the grain of the bowl perfectly **—** **PLASTER-SET-FIRST-TRY HIT** |
| ****Control**** | **Roasted a second pinch **much hotter**. ****It will not set at all.** Dead.** So there is a **window**: enough heat to drive the water off, **not enough to kill it** |

> ****This is gypsum, and roasted gypsum is PLASTER.****

### B · the SOLUBLE BITTER SALT *(~8 g)*

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| ****Lime water**** | ****Heavy white gelatinous precipitate.** Reasoning: **if this were a salt of lime, lime water could not throw anything down.** It is a salt of **some other earth** |
| ****Flame test**** | **Ran it in the ****hydrogen blowpipe flame — because that flame is nearly invisible**** |
| **Control first** | **Splint dipped in **plain brine** → ****blazing orange-yellow**** |
| **Then the sample** | ****Nothing. No colour at all** **—** **FLAME-TEST-CLEAN HIT.** **Not a soda salt** |
| ****Why this only worked now**** | ****A flame test is unreadable in a wood fire — the fire is already screaming yellow.** I needed a flame with **no colour of its own**, and I built one three weeks ago for a completely different reason |
| ****Charcoal reduction**** | **Pinch mixed with charcoal dust in a hollow on a charcoal block · **blowpipe on it hard** · let it cool · **wetted it** |
| **Result** | ****ROTTEN EGGS.** Unmistakable, immediate **—** **SULFATE-HEPAR-STINK HIT** |
| **What that means** | ****Burn a sulfate down with charcoal and you get a stinking sulfur salt.** The stink **is** the sulfur. ****Both of these are sulfates**** |

### Verdict

| | Read |
|---|---|
| ****Bulk floor**** | ****GYPSUM** — a soft, non-fizzing, poorly soluble **sulfate of lime, carrying water**. Roast it → **PLASTER** |
| ****Soluble minor**** | ****An EPSOM-class BITTER SALT** — a sulfate of a **different earth than lime**, freely soluble, strongly bitter, **no soda in it** |
| ****One floor, two sulfates**** | **One soluble, one not — **which is why the leach sorted them for me by accident**. The cool-sharp taste that fooled me into "nitre" was the **bitter salt**; the pale bulk under it was the **gypsum** |
| **Confidence** | ****High on both behaviours, and behaviour is what I can use.** I still cannot *name* the second earth — **BITTER-SALT-1 identity narrowed, not closed** |

### What plaster is actually worth

| Use | Notes |
|-----|-------|
| ****Moulds and casts**** | ****Take an impression of a thing and reproduce it.** This is the road to **casting duplicate parts** instead of filing each one by hand |
| ****True flat surfaces**** | **A poured plaster slab is **flat by gravity** — a reference plane I currently do not own |
| **Splints** | ****A broken arm in a plaster splint** is a different injury than a broken arm in sticks |
| **Interior finish** | **Smooth wall and floor render, **sets in minutes** |
| ****The fuel argument**** | ****Lime needs kiln heat (~a full burn) and then MONTHS to carbonate in air. Gypsum needs barely more than a bread oven and sets in MINUTES by taking its water back.**** Completely different material, far cheaper |
| **Limit** | ****Not waterproof.** Interior and workshop only — **rain will soften it back** |
| **Supply** | **The cave floor powder is a **sample, not a quarry**. **Prospect for the bed it came from**: pale soft rock a **nail scratches** and that **does not fizz** |

## Discoveries

| ID | Notes |
|----|-------|
| ****GYPSUM-3223**** | ****Soft enough for a thumbnail · does NOT fizz in vinegar · poorly soluble · roast gently then re-wet and IT SETS HARD IN MINUTES.** That chain identifies gypsum and produces plaster in the same afternoon |
| **PLASTER-BURN-WINDOW-3223** | ****Under-roast and the water stays · over-roast and it is DEAD and will never set.** There is a narrow window — **drive the steam off and stop** |
| **PLASTER-VS-LIME-3223** | ****Lime: kiln heat, then months of carbonating in air. Gypsum: bread-oven heat, sets in minutes by re-absorbing its water.**** Not a weaker lime — **a different material with a different economy** |
| ****INVISIBLE-FLAME-TEST-3223**** | ****A flame test needs a flame with no colour of its own.** A wood fire is uselessly yellow. **The hydrogen blowpipe is nearly invisible and is therefore a perfect test flame** — run a **known control first**, then the sample. **Blazing yellow = soda** |
| ****HEPAR-TEST-3223**** | ****Roast an unknown with charcoal, then wet it — rotten eggs means SULFATE.** The charcoal strips the sulfate down to a sulfur salt and the smell gives it away. **A whole class of minerals now testable on a charcoal block** |
| **LEACH-SORTS-3223** | ****A leach is a separation, not just an extraction.** What stays behind is **half the sample** — **never discard the residue before the assay is closed** |
