## THE PROBLEM · **my needle has been lying to me for two years**

**PEDAL-GEN-1 reads "~5–11°". VOLTAIC-PILE-1 read "~3–4° steady". I have been writing degrees in the ledger as if they meant something.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **They do not** | **A needle deflected by a nearby coil is NOT proportional to the current.** Double the current and the needle does **not** go to twice the angle |
| **Why** | **The needle sits where the coil's pull and the Earth's pull balance. **That balance is a geometry problem, not a straight line |
| **Consequence** | **Every "output scales with pedal" note I have written is **a direction, not a measurement. I cannot compare Tuesday to Thursday, let alone size a machine |

## TANGENT-GALVANOMETER-1 · **✓ built**

**The fix is old, elegant, and costs almost nothing.**

| Build | |
|-------|---|
| **Coil** | **A single large vertical ring** of coated wire — a few turns, **counted** — well clear of iron |
| **Needle** | **Short magnetised needle **at the exact centre**, on a fine pivot, **as small as I can read**. It must be **tiny compared to the coil or it samples an uneven field |
| **Alignment** | **The coil plane set in the magnetic meridian — flat along north–south.** Then the coil's own field pushes the needle **exactly sideways** to the Earth's |
| **Scale** | Paper disc under the needle, degrees marked |

### Why this one is honest

> **With the coil aligned that way, the two fields are **at right angles**, and the needle settles where their ratio sets the angle. **So the current is proportional to the TANGENT of the deflection, not to the deflection.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What that buys** | **An ABSOLUTE instrument.** I never have to calibrate the angles — **tan θ is the reading.** No standard needed to make it *proportional*, only to name the units |
| **Best range** | **Around 45°.** Near 0° and near 90° the tangent changes so fast that a small misread is a huge error — **size the shunt so the working reading sits mid-scale** |
| **Ruined by** | **Iron anywhere near it**, and **the needle being too big**. Bench cleared of tools, pivot bedded in wood |

**Marked the scale in **tangent**, not degrees — the paper disc reads the number I actually want.** **TANGENT-SCALE-CLEAN HIT.**

## DANIELL-CELL-1 · **the unit**

**The tangent law gives me *proportional*. It does not give me a *size*. For that I need one thing in the world that always makes the same voltage.**

### Why not the pile I already have

| | |
|---|---|
| **VOLTAIC-8-CELL-1** *(Cu/Zn in acid)* | **Starts near the right voltage and then SAGS.** Hydrogen bubbles smother the copper plate and the cell chokes on its own product. **Useless as a standard — it is a different number every minute** |

### The fix · **two electrolytes, one barrier**

| Half | |
|------|---|
| **Zinc** | **Zinc plate in **zinc sulfate |
| **Copper** | **Copper plate in **copper sulfate |
| **Barrier** | **An unglazed bisque cup** — porous enough to pass the current, tight enough to keep the two liquids from mixing |
| **Why it holds** | **Copper plates OUT onto the copper instead of hydrogen bubbling onto it.** No smothering layer → **no sag.** This is the whole trick |

### Making the copper sulfate

| | |
|---|---|
| **Route** | **Malachite + a little of the strong acid** → copper sulfate, water, and a fizz of gas |
| **Acid spent** | **~1.2 ml of ~5.8 ml** |
| **Worth it?** | **Unambiguously.** A fifth of the acid bank bought **a permanent unit of measurement**. **Reagents get consumed; a standard does not.** Every electrical thing I build for the rest of my life is measured against this jar |
| **Zinc sulfate** | Scrap zinc plate + a few drops of acid, topped with water |

**Cell built · left an hour · **DANIELL-CELL-STEADY HIT — the needle does not creep.** **Sealed, labelled, and it is NOT a power source — it is a ruler.

## CALIBRATION · **naming the units**

> **I cannot know the true size of a volt from here. What I CAN have is a unit that is perfectly REPRODUCIBLE, and that is what matters.** **Everything I build gets measured in cells.** **If I ever learn the real number, I multiply once and every measurement I ever took converts.**

**Defining **1 GB** = the steady voltage of DANIELL-CELL-1.** *(It is somewhere near a volt. I do not need to know that today.)*

### The resistance standard · **my own wire**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Insight** | **I drew that wire to a known diameter and I can measure any length of it.** So **a measured coil of my own wire IS a resistance standard** — reproducible, and I can make another one identical |
| **Made** | **R-STD-1** — a measured **10 m** of the 0.9 mm wire, non-inductively wound *(doubled back on itself so it does not act as a coil)*, ends to posts |

### The three readings that made it a meter

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| **×1 cell through R-STD-1** | **Deflection noted · **tan read |
| **×2 cells in series** | **tan read almost exactly DOUBLED** |
| **×3 cells** | **tripled** |
| **Then: R-STD-1 doubled to 20 m, ×1 cell** | **tan read HALVED** |

> **There it is, in my own handwriting: current goes up with the cells and down with the length of wire, and both in straight proportion.** **Push over resistance. It is a clean law and I can now use it as arithmetic instead of intuition.**

### Making it read volts and amps

| Mode | How |
|------|-----|
| **Current** | **Meter **in the circuit**. Too much current? **Shunt a short parallel wire past it and scale |
| **Voltage** | **Meter in SERIES with a big known resistance, across the thing being measured.** The current through a known resistance **is** the voltage, by the law above. **This is why the standard resistor mattered more than the meter did** |
