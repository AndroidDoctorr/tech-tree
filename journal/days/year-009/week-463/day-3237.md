### Distilling it · **rosin is the RESIDUE, not the resin**

**I had been treating raw pine resin as "rosin." It is not.**

> **Raw oleoresin = rosin DISSOLVED IN turpentine.** Heat it and **the turpentine distils off**, leaving **hard, brittle, glassy rosin** behind. **You get both, and I had been throwing the better half away as smoke.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rig** | Retort, condenser, cooled receiver |
| **SAFETY — stated before lighting anything** | **Turpentine vapour is FLAMMABLE.** **Water/sand bath, NEVER a direct flame under the retort** · **receiver kept cool and away from any fire** · **fume hood** · joints checked before heat, not during |
| **Turpentine** | **~22 ml** — clear, sharp-smelling |
| **Rosin** | **~80 g** — pale, brittle, snaps clean |

### What the turpentine is actually for

| Use | |
|-----|---|
| **Solvent** | **The first real **non-water, non-alcohol solvent I have |
| **Varnish thinner** | **This is the one.** When the seed-flax harvest gives me **linseed**, turpentine is what thins the varnish to a brushable film |
| **ROSIN-IN-TURPENTINE = a COLD varnish, available NOW** | **Dissolve rosin in turpentine and it brushes on cold and dries by evaporation — no melt pot.** Soft, and it re-dissolves in more turpentine, so **not a replacement for the dip coat** — but **exactly right for touch-ups, lead seals, and soaking the commutator hub** |

## GEN-WW-1 · **sizing, before I wind anything**

**64 m sounds like a lot until you ask what a generator actually needs.**

### How much wire

| | |
|---|---|
| **Armature plan** | **×4 coils · ~100 turns each · ~0.25 m mean turn** |
| **Wire needed** | **~100 m + leads ≈ ~110 m** |
| **Have coated** | ~64 m |
| **Short by** | **~46 m** |
| **Can I get it?** | **Yes, comfortably.** The bar has **~138 m** left in it *(785 g ÷ ~5.7 g/m at 0.9 mm)* · plus **~17.55 kg of ore** unsmelted. **Copper is genuinely not the constraint any more — drawing time and WAX are** |

### THE PROBLEM I had not thought about · **the wheel is SLOW**

> **A water wheel makes torque and no speed. A generator wants speed and barely any torque.** They are **exact opposites** and I was about to bolt one to the other.

| | |
|---|---|
| **WW-2** | **~10–15 rpm.** That is what a water wheel *is* |
| **What a generator wants** | **Hundreds of rpm** |
| **Why it matters** | **Output rises with **turns × field × SPEED**. At a tenth of the speed I need **ten times the turns for the same result |
| **And ten times the turns costs** | **~1000 m of wire · ten times the wax · ten times the winding · **and ten times the resistance, which throws most of it away as heat |

### The answer is GEARING, and gearing is cheap

> **A belt stage costs two pulleys and some leather. A tenfold winding costs a kilometre of copper.** **Buy the speed with ratio, not with wire.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target** | **~16:1 step-up** → WW-2 at ~12 rpm becomes **~190 rpm** at the armature |
| **Two stages, not one** | **~4:1 twice.** A single 16:1 needs either an enormous wheel or a tiny one — and **a tiny pulley SLIPS**, because the belt wraps too little of it to grip. Two moderate stages each grip properly |
| **Already in hand** | **WW-2 belt collars are quick-release** and the **table-saw belt branch** proves the geometry. This is a known build, not a new one |
| **Bonus** | **The wheel has torque to spare and nothing to do with it.** Gearing is exactly the trade that converts the surplus I have into the shortage I have |

### Field · **magneto, not dynamo**

**Using the permanent rods** *(×7 spare MAG-BOOT-EM in the tray)* **rather than a wound field.** **A permanent field just works from the first turn — a self-exciting dynamo has to bootstrap off residual magnetism and can simply fail to start.** **Simpler, and I have the magnets sitting idle.**

*End Day 3237 · Cal-Y10 D92 · ~24 Mar. All sixty-four metres are coated and there is no bare wire left in the shop. Went for pine resin and learned two things: tapping is something you leave running, not something you go and do, and the sticky stuff on the bark is not rosin at all — it is rosin dissolved in turpentine, and I have spent years boiling the turpentine off into the air. Twenty-two millilitres of it in a stoppered bottle now, which is the first proper solvent I have ever owned. Then I sat down and sized the generator before winding a single turn, which is just as well, because I was about to bolt an armature straight onto a wheel that turns twelve times a minute. A wheel is torque and no speed; a generator is the reverse. Making up the difference with turns would cost a kilometre of copper and throw most of the output away as heat in its own windings. Making it up with two belt stages costs four pulleys and some leather. The wheel has more torque than it knows what to do with, and gearing is precisely the trade that turns what I have too much of into what I have too little of.*
