| Part | |

| **Source** | Daniell cells |

### Why iron and not copper

**Copper is too good. **A copper slide wire would have almost no resistance, so the end-block contacts would swamp the measurement.** **Iron at three tenths of a millimetre gives a sensible resistance per metre and the contacts stop mattering.

### The uniformity check — **the thing that makes or breaks it**

> **If the wire is not uniform along its length, the length ratio LIES, and the whole instrument is a decorated guess.**

**Checked it properly instead of assuming: compared first half against second half, then quarter against quarter, balancing each against the next. **SLIDE-WIRE-UNIFORMITY HIT — even end to end. *(It should be — I drew it slowly and annealed on a schedule precisely so it would be.)*

### Using it

| | |
|---|---|
| **Balance** | **Slide the jockey until the detector reads ZERO.** Then **unknown : standard = length : length** |
| **Work near the MIDDLE** | **Same principle as working near 45° on the tangent galvanometer: error is smallest when the ratio is near one.** If the balance point sits near an end, **swap in a different standard** until it comes back to the middle |
| **Error source** | **Contact resistance at the end blocks.** Heavy copper, clamped hard, cleaned |

## FIRST RESISTIVITY TABLE · **in my own units**

**Took a measured length of each metal I own, at a measured gauge, and balanced it against R-STD-1.**

| Metal | Relative resistivity *(copper = 1)* |
|-------|-------------------------------------|
| **Copper** | **1** — the benchmark, and now I know why it is worth the trouble* |
| **Lead** | **High** — a poor conductor, which explains a few things* |
| **Iron** | Several times copper |
| **Brass** | **Worse than either parent metal** |
| **Bronze** | **Also worse than its parents** |

### The finding

> **An ALLOY conducts worse than EITHER metal that went into it.** **That is not what I would have guessed — I would have expected something in between.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it matters** | **This is **the same fact** as *alloys drift less with temperature*, seen from the other side. **A mixed lattice scatters the flow no matter what the temperature does |
| **So** | **Alloys are the right material for resistors — BOTH because they resist more per metre AND because they hold steady.** **Replace the pure-iron R-BIG-1 with brass** |
| **And** | **Resistivity is a FINGERPRINT.** Measured gauge + measured length + the bridge **identifies an unknown metal**, or catches a bad alloy batch |

## AMPLIFIERS · **the answer is a ladder, and the tube is the top rung** *(consult)*

### First: **for the metrology, I need no amplifier at all**

> **A headphone is astonishingly sensitive — that is exactly why it was the standard null detector.** Balancing a bridge to silence takes **almost no power**. **Do not build an amplifier to solve a problem that does not need one.**

### And for sound: **an amplifier is not how you get loud — an oscillator is**

| Rung | What | Read |
|------|------|------|
| **0** | **No amplifier** | **Headphone null. **Solves the whole bridge problem today |
| **1** | **A horn** | **Acoustic gain, no electricity whatsoever. **Unreasonably effective |
| **2** | **CARBON MICROPHONE** | **Genuine electrical POWER GAIN with no vacuum.** Charcoal granules squeezed between two plates: a **whisper** of sound pressure modulates a **battery's** current. This is how telephones carried across continents before tubes existed — **couple an earpiece to a carbon mic and you have a repeater with real gain.** **I have charcoal.** *(Granule packing shifts and it hisses — noisy, but it is gain)* |
| **3** | **Relay** | On-off gain. Switching, not sound |
| **4** | **Magnetic amplifier** *(saturable reactor)*** | **Real, quiet, continuous gain with no vacuum** — a DC current controls an iron-cored coil's opposition to AC. **Hungry for iron and wire, but nothing in it is beyond me |
| **5** | **VACUUM TUBE** | See below |

### What a tube actually costs

| Requirement | Where I stand |
|-------------|---------------|
| **High vacuum** | **The killer.** A piston pump gets nowhere near it. The historical answer is a **mercury pump** — falling drops of mercury dragging the gas out, running for **hours to days** · **which means MERCURY, which means finding and roasting cinnabar. An ore hunt before I even start** |
| **Sealing wires through glass** | **Glass and metal expand differently and the seal cracks.** The classic fix was **platinum**, because it happens to expand like soda glass. **I have none.** A **feather-edged copper seal** is the achievable alternative — **and it is a skill, not a purchase** |
| **A hot cathode** | **A filament that emits and survives. **Carbon works badly; the good answers need metals I do not have |
| **Getter** | A flash of reactive metal to eat the last of the gas |

> **Verdict: a tube is a multi-year arc that starts with a mercury mine.** **Not impossible. Not next.**

### The one electronic part that IS cheap

> **COPPER OXIDE RECTIFIER.** Heat copper and the oxide layer that forms **passes current one way and not the other.** **A real rectifier, no vacuum, no rare metals, and I can make one this week.** It gives no gain — but **rectification is most of what I actually want for measurement**, and it makes an AC bridge readable on a DC meter.

### And the synthesizer — **skip the amplifier entirely: build a TONEWHEEL**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | **A toothed iron wheel spun past a magnet and a pickup coil.** Each tooth passing makes a pulse; shape the teeth and **the output is very nearly a pure tone** |
| **Why it is the right answer** | **The frequency is set by TOOTH COUNT × SHAFT SPEED — two things I can measure.** So a tonewheel is **not just a voice, it is a calibrated frequency source** |
| **Which closes the loop** | **Pendulum gives the second → measured shaft speed → the wheel's pitch is KNOWN, not remembered.** I can check my inherited A against it instead of trusting it |
| **Power** | **Enough turns on the pickup drives a headphone directly.** Loud speakers can wait for rung 2 or 4 |
| **And then** | **Several wheels, a keyboard of switches, and resistors to mix them in proportion — that is additive synthesis and it is an ORGAN.** I have a water wheel, belts, a lathe, magnets and wire |
