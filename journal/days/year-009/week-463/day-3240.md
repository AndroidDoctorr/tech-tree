## The coil-gauge question, now that I can actually check it

**Since I have a meter, I stopped guessing and did the arithmetic.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The surprise** | **For a FIXED winding window, the gauge barely changes the coil's efficiency.** Go finer and you fit more turns — but each turn is thinner *and* the total wire is longer, and the two effects **very nearly cancel** |
| **What gauge DOES set** | **The trade between VOLTAGE and CURRENT.** Fine wire = more turns = higher voltage, less current. Fat wire = the reverse |
| **What actually sets efficiency** | **The size of the winding window and the field** — not the wire I choose to fill it with |
| **Conclusion** | **Stay at 0.9 mm. **It was the right call for a different reason than I thought, and now I can prove it rather than assert it |

## METROLOGY SLATE · **filed, not built** *(consult)*

### Standard resistors — **the answer is a NULL, not a better measurement**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The trap** | **Trying to make wire accurate enough to hit a value. **I cannot draw to that tolerance and never will |
| **The method** | **Make it deliberately LONG, then TRIM to match.** Every precision resistor ever made was **trimmed**, not manufactured to size |
| **The instrument** | **A BRIDGE.** Compare the unknown against the standard in a ratio and **adjust until the detector reads ZERO** |
| **Why a null is so much better** | **Detecting "nothing" is enormously easier than judging "how much."** A needle position is a guess; **a needle that does not move at all is a fact.** **This is the single biggest step in accuracy available to me and it costs nothing** |
| **Cheapest form** | **SLIDE-WIRE BRIDGE** — one uniform wire, a sliding contact, and a rule. **The resistance ratio becomes a LENGTH ratio**, and I can measure length beautifully |

### Stability — **alloys, not pure metals**

> **A general rule worth having: PURE metals change resistance a lot with temperature; ALLOYS change very little.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **So R-BIG-1 in iron is the worst case** | Pure metal, high drift |
| **Better with what I already have** | **Brass or bronze resistance wire** — an alloy, and I can make both |
| **Better still** | **Sealed box, or a **sunk oil bath, to hold one temperature |

### Standard inductors — **the easy one**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why easy** | **An air-cored coil's inductance comes from GEOMETRY AND TURN COUNT ALONE.** No material property to know, nothing to drift. **Count the turns, measure the former, and it is computable and duplicable** |
| **Rule** | **AIR CORE for a standard.** Iron makes it larger but **nonlinear and lossy** — a good working choice, a **bad** standard |
| **Build** | Single layer, rigid former, turns counted aloud and recorded |

### The RLC / perfect-pitch idea — **this is a very good idea and here is the sharp edge**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it works** | **A resonant circuit's frequency is set by L and C together.** Know the frequency and one of them, and **the other falls out.** And **frequency is the quantity I can pin down most precisely of anything I own**, because I can hear it |
| **THE EAR'S REAL SUPERPOWER** | **Not judging pitch — detecting SILENCE.** The ear is a **superb null detector** and a mediocre magnitude meter. **So: an AC bridge driven with a tone and balanced until the headphone goes quiet.** Same null principle as the resistance bridge, and it is **far** better than sweeping for a loudness peak |
| **THE SHARP EDGE** | **My perfect pitch is a MEMORY, not an artifact.** I know what A sounds like **because of instruments I heard in another life** — that reference has had nine years with nothing to check it against. **I must not build a unit system on a recollection** |
| **The fix, and it is cheap** | **1. **PENDULUM → the SECOND.** A pendulum's swing depends on its length. **Absolute time from a string and a rule** · 2. **MONOCHORD → absolute PITCH.** A string's frequency comes from its **length, its tension, and its weight per length** — all three of which I can measure. **That gives me a true frequency from mechanical quantities, with no memory involved** · 3. **Then compare my remembered A against it** — and whichever wins, **forge a TUNING FORK and make it the artifact. Forks are remarkably stable |
| **The pattern** | **Same bootstrap as the electrical units: one physical object, everything else by arithmetic.** A jar and a coil gave me volts, ohms and amps. **A fork would give me hertz — and hertz plus ohms gives me henries and farads** |

### And the potentiometer — **better than my voltmeter**

> **In the precision sense a potentiometer is **a divider that compares two voltages by nulling them against each other.** **At balance it draws ZERO current — not "nearly none," none.** That is the perfect version of the rule I wrote yesterday, and it is how a cell's true voltage gets measured. **Queued.

**And yes — **once the bridge exists, the resistivity of every metal I own becomes measurable in my own units**, which also makes resistivity a **fingerprint for identifying an unknown metal.
