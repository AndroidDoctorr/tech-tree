## GRAPE-SHOOT-THIN-Y10 · **the real work**

**Everything below came off by hand — thumb pressure at the base.**

| Take off | Why |
|----------|-----|
| **Doubles** — two shoots from one node | **Keep the stronger, snap the weaker. Two shoots in one bud's space is **twice the leaf in the same air |
| **Blind shoots** — shoots carrying **no** inflorescence | **They are pure canopy with no return this year. **Keep only the few I want as renewal wood |
| **Trunk suckers** and shoots off **old wood** | **They steal vigour and give nothing.** The vine cannot tell that they are useless — I have to |
| **Anything growing into the interior** | **Shade and still air = **mildew and bunch rot**. The cordon wants light and a breeze through it** |

### Snap them GREEN

> **A green shoot snaps clean off at the base with a thumb.** Wait two weeks and it is woody — then it needs a knife, and it leaves a **wound**. **The whole job is easy now and unpleasant later.**

### DO NOT strip it bare

**Leaves are the engine.** Over-thinned fruit in **this** climate **sunburns**. **Thinned for light and air, not for tidiness.**

---

## THE POINT · **this year's shade is next year's crop**

> **A bud only becomes fruitful if it was in SUNLIGHT when it formed.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **How it works** | **The buds that will grow in **spring Y11** are being laid down **this summer, inside this canopy |
| **So** | **A bud buried in shade this June is a BLIND SHOOT next April.** Which shades the vine further. Which blinds more buds |
| **Meaning** | **Shoot-thinning is not about this year's grapes at all. It compounds.** A crowded vine gets worse every year on its own, and a thinned one gets better |

### Crop-load rule for later *(write it down now)*

> **~12–15 healthy leaves to ripen ONE cluster.** Count the shoots I kept, count the inflorescences on them, and **that arithmetic tells me how many bunches to drop after fruit set** — not guesswork at harvest.

**Counted today so I have the number in hand at set.** Most shoots carry **2 inflorescences**; a few carry 3.

## VINE-WINTER-DAMAGE · **one cordon arm**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Found** | **A stretch of the cordon — several spurs in a row — pushed nothing.** Bark scraped: **brown underneath, not green.** Cold-killed over winter |
| **Extent** | **One arm section only.** The rest of the cordon broke strongly *(the push this year is good)* |
| **Not a loss** | **A cordon is rebuildable.** This is what renewal wood is for |
| **The fix** | **Find the **nearest live bud below the dead stretch** — take a strong shoot from it and, instead of pinching it out, **train it along the wire this season** as the replacement arm. **Lay it in as it grows, tie soft. Next winter it gets spurred back and it *is* the cordon |
| **Dead wood** | **Cut out now** — it is not feeding anything and it harbours rot |
| **Cost** | **One season of reduced crop on that arm.** That is all |

**Marked the renewal shoot with a tow tie so I do not absent-mindedly thin it off on the next pass.**
