## THE THREAD QUESTION · answered by arithmetic, before touching anything


|                                  |                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Flax by weight**               | **Not a problem at all.** Over **2 kg** of line banked — P-FLAX-LINE-Y8-1 ~1.03 kg, flax line bank ~425 g, HEMP-LINE-26 ~870 g                                                                                             |
| **But weight is the wrong unit** | **Serving is measured in LENGTH OF THREAD PER LENGTH OF WIRE**, and the ratio is brutal                                                                                                                                    |
| **The ratio**                    | **Wrapping a **0.9 mm** wire close-turn with **~0.3 mm** thread: each turn travels the **circumference** and advances only **one thread width**. That is roughly **twelve to thirteen metres of thread per metre of wire |
| **For 64 m of wire**             | **~800 m of thread**                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Thread I own**                 | **~850 m** *(THREAD-STOCK-2 ~533 m · thread ~307 m · flax thread bank ~13 m)*                                                                                                                                              |
| **Verdict**                      | **I could just barely do it — and it would consume essentially my ENTIRE thread bank on a single coil, plus an absurd number of hours at the serving board.**                                                              |
| **So: do not serve the bulk**    | **Thread goes only where it earns it** — **coil leads, crossovers, and layer edges**, where turns meet at an angle and any coating is thinnest                                                                             |
| **Paper**                        | **~14 sheets** banked and **~9 kg of paper-grade shive** behind it. **Interleaving is cheap.** Paper is not a constraint                                                                                                   |

## THE COATING · and a real dead end avoided


| Candidate                | Read                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Olive oil**            | **NO. And this matters.** I have **six litres** of it and it is **useless here** — **olive oil is a non-drying oil. It never hardens.** It will sit tacky and greasy on that wire **forever**. No amount of waiting turns it into a film |
| **Linseed oil**          | **This is the one that works — a DRYING oil.** It takes up air and **polymerises into a tough solid skin**. But I have **none pressed**, and the seed-flax strip is **not harvested until summer**. **Not available today**              |
| **Beeswax**              | **Available, and **more coming — the apiary band opens in two days**. Easy to apply. **But soft, and it creeps when warm                                                                                                               |
| **Beeswax + pine rosin** | **The answer.** Rosin blended into the wax gives a **harder film with a higher softening point** than wax alone, and still melts low enough to pull wire through                                                                         |




### Made and tested


| Step           | Notes                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Blend**      | **Wax + rosin, melted together, trialled at a few ratios · settled on the **hardest blend that still flowed thin                                            |
| **Applicator** | **A small pot over gentle heat with a felt wiper at the outlet.** Wire goes in bare off the reel, **through the melt, through the wiper, onto a second reel** |
| **Coats**      | **Two passes.** One coat is a film with holes in it; **two coats is a film**                                                                                  |
| **Film**       | **Holds — continuous, tight, no bare patches under the glass** **—** **WAX-ROSIN-FILM-HOLDS HIT**                                                             |
| **Done**       | **~22 m coated** *(a first run, deliberately short)*                                                                                                          |

## THE TEST · before committing a whole coil

> **The rule I set myself yesterday: **wind a short test length and prove it BEFORE spending the wire.


| Test                 | Result                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Wound**            | **A short tight test coil on a former, **layer-wound with paper between layers                                                                 |
| **End to end**       | **Passes current freely.** The wire is sound                                                                                                     |
| **Across the turns** | **Nothing. No path.** Turn-to-turn is **isolated** **—** **TEST-COIL-ISOLATION HIT**                                                             |
| **Squeeze test**     | **Compressed the winding hard by hand and re-tested: **still isolated.** That is the one that mattered — **a coil gets squeezed as you wind it |
| **Verdict**          | **The grammar works: coat on the run, two passes, paper between layers, thread only at leads and crossovers**                                    |




### The design consequence · *and it is a nice one*


|                                       |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Wax-rosin has a temperature limit** | **Let the coil run hot and the film **softens, creeps, and the turns find each other                                                                                                                                               |
| **But I have copper to spare**        | **~785 g of bar and ~17.5 kg of ore still untouched**                                                                                                                                                                                |
| **So: wind it FAT**                   | **Thicker wire, more copper, lower current density, and the coil simply never gets warm enough to matter.** **Abundant copper lets me get away with a weak insulator** — I buy thermal headroom with metal instead of with chemistry |
| **And later**                         | **When the flax seed comes in and I press **linseed oil**, I can **re-coat with a proper hard varnish** and run it harder. **The wax build is not a compromise, it is the version I can have now                                   |

## Discoveries


| ID                                  | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **DRYING-VS-NON-DRYING-OIL-3232**   | **Olive oil NEVER hardens — it is a non-drying oil, and no amount of time makes it a film.** **Linseed** takes up air and polymerises into a tough skin.** Having six litres of the wrong oil is having none. **Press linseed when the seed-flax comes in |
| **SERVE-BY-LENGTH-NOT-WEIGHT-3232** | **Thread wrap costs ~12–13 m of thread per METRE of wire.** Flax by weight is irrelevant; **spun thread length is the real unit**, and 64 m of wire would eat my whole bank. **Serve only leads, crossovers and layer edges**                               |
| **WAX-PLUS-ROSIN-3232**             | **Rosin in the wax raises the softening point and hardens the film** while still melting thin enough to pull wire through. **Two coats, never one — one coat is a film with holes in it**                                                                   |
| **SQUEEZE-THE-TEST-COIL-3232**      | **Test isolation while COMPRESSING the winding.** A coil is squeezed as it is wound; a film that passes loose and fails tight will fail **after** it is buried under three hundred turns                                                                    |
| **BUY-HEADROOM-WITH-METAL-3232**    | **Abundant copper lets you tolerate a weak insulator.** Wind fatter, drop the current density, and the coil never reaches the temperature that would soften the wax. **A materials limit solved with a design choice**                                      |
