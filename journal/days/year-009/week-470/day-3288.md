## ☠ ★★★ First — why nine years of sulfur never became one drop of acid

**I have had native sulfur off this hill since d1651. `M-14`. In the register. And I have 4.3 ml of vitriol.**

> ☠ ★★★ **BECAUSE SULFUR WAS NEVER THE BOTTLENECK. THE CONVERSION WAS.** *Burning sulfur is trivial and gives a choking gas that then refuses to become acid without a large chamber, a nitre feed and a process I have no way to run.* ★★ **So having the element did nothing, and I concluded I needed MORE and BETTER sulfur — which is how a supply problem that was not a supply problem turned into a 117 km expedition.**

★★★ **The whole Dörtyol arc was the wrong answer to a correctly-felt frustration.**

---

## ★★★ The acid route, and it is not the one I planned

**I have been carrying "roast the sulfide, catch the gas" in my head for a year, and treating the choking fume as the price of the whole chemistry tree.**

**Broke a fresh lump this morning and found the answer on the face of yesterday's cut instead.** ☠ **The weathered cap is rusty and the seeps coming off it are sour.** ★★★ *Pyrite plus air plus water makes iron sulfate and sulfuric acid, slowly, by itself, without any fire at all — and it has been doing it on that hillside since long before I was here.*

> ★★★ **THE PROCESS IS ALREADY RUNNING. I DO NOT HAVE TO INVENT IT — I HAVE TO CATCH IT AND SPEED IT UP.**

### ★★★ Green vitriol, and then the oil of it

| Step | |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Break the sulfide small and heap it open to air and rain.** ★ *Crushed, not powdered — it needs air THROUGH it* |
| **2** | ⧗ **Wait months.** It oxidises on its own |
| **3** | **Leach the heap and boil the liquor down to green crystals** — **green vitriol** |
| **4** | ★★★ **Dry-distil the crystals hard in a strong retort.** *They give up their water, then give up something far more aggressive, and what condenses is **oil of vitriol*** |

> ★★★ **AND THAT IS WHY IT IS CALLED OIL OF VITRIOL.** *The name is not decoration. It is a recipe — the oil you get out of vitriol — and it has been sitting in the word the entire time I have been planning a two-week journey to get it another way.*

★★★ **No roast. No sulfur burning. No chamber. No cloud of choking gas over a camp.** ⚠ **The distillation is genuinely fierce and it is where all the danger now lives** — *but it is one small vessel, indoors under a hood, on my own bench, at a time of my choosing, instead of a smoking heap at the far end of a road.*

> ★★ **I TRADED A LARGE UNCONTROLLED HAZARD FOR A SMALL CONTROLLED ONE, AND THE SMALL ONE IS THE KIND I AM GOOD AT.**

> ☠ ★★★ **AND NOTE WHAT THE ROUTE ASKS FOR: NOT SULFUR. PYRITE.** *The element I had catalogued and could not use is bypassed entirely; the rock I was walking over is the feedstock.* ★★ **I was short of a PROCESS and I went shopping for a MATERIAL.**

## ☠ ★★★ And then a lump smelled of garlic

**Went through the fresh face lump by lump, and one of them came apart under the hammer with a smell I know and did not expect here.** ☠ **Garlic. Sharp, immediate, unmistakable.**

**Looked at what I had broken.** *Silver-white with a brassy cast, harder than the pyrite around it, blockier in the fracture.* ☠ ★★★ **Arsenopyrite. And not a curiosity in the corner of one vein — it is in every cut I have opened today. It is one of the main minerals on this hill.**

★ **And it explains the crust.** *There is a pale green-grey powdery bloom on the weathered faces that I have been walking past for two days calling it a tired malachite.* ☠ **It is not copper. It is what arsenopyrite turns into when it rots** — which means the weathering that is making my acid has been mobilising arsenic the entire time, in front of me, labelled.

### ☠ Three things change, and the third one is the worst

| | |
|---|---|
| ☠ **The heap liquor is not just acid** | **Arsenic goes into solution alongside the iron.** ⚠ *The sump is not catching one product; it is catching a product and a poison in the same jar, and they will not separate by wishing* |
| ☠ ★★★ **NEVER ROAST THIS ORE** | **Roasting arsenopyrite drives off arsenic as a white fume.** *This is the thing that killed assayers for centuries.* ★★ **I had already abandoned the roast for the acid — I am now abandoning it for everything, permanently, at this hill** |
| ☠ ★★★ **The vein gold is INSIDE the arsenopyrite** | *The free gold in the gravels is free. The gold in the rock is locked in the one mineral I have just forbidden myself to roast.* ⚠ **So the placer is not the poor cousin of the vein. For now it is the only gold I can actually have** |

> ☠ ★★★ **THE WARNING AND THE DANGER ARE NOT THE SAME SUBSTANCE, AND THEY DO NOT ARRIVE TOGETHER.** *Struck fresh, this ore announces itself — the garlic is loud, instant and impossible to mistake.* ☠ ★★★ **Roasted, the thing that comes off is a white fume with no smell at all.** ★★★ **The ore warns me clearly while it is still safe, and goes completely silent at the moment it becomes lethal.**

☠ ★★ **Which is the exact inverse of every hazard instinct I have.** *I have spent nine years learning to treat a strong smell as the signal to back off and clean air as the all-clear.* ⚠ **Here that rule is not merely useless, it is precisely backwards** — and if I had met this ore first in a roasting pit rather than under a hammer, I would have read the absence of a smell as safety.

★ **So it goes in the register as a rule and not as an incident, the way the pozzolan lesson did:** ⚑ **`SMELL-IS-NOT-THE-HAZARD` — an absent warning is not an absent danger, and on some materials it is the danger.**

⚠ **Practical, today:** *gloves stay on, I stop eating with the hands I am breaking rock with, the crushing happens upwind, and the sump gets a lid and a mark rather than an open mouth.* ★ **None of that is new equipment — it is the kit I already carry, pointed at a hazard I did not know I had.**

## ☠ ★★★ Then I tried to site the heap and there is nowhere

**Walked the district for a place to build it, and every candidate failed for the same reason.**

**A pyrite heap makes acid. Rain falls on it. The acid goes downhill.** ☠ **And everything here goes downhill to the Orontes — my water, my fish, my ford, and the bank I mean to put a bridge on.**

★ **The seeps off that cap are the demonstration.** *Sour, stained orange, and nothing living in them for a good way down.* ☠ **That is a small natural heap showing me exactly what a large deliberate one would do.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE FAILURE MODE AND THE PRODUCT ARE THE SAME LIQUID.** *Acid running out of a pyrite heap into a river is the worst thing I could do to this valley. Acid running out of a pyrite heap into a jar is the only reason I am standing here.* ★★★ **There is no difference between them except whether something is underneath it.**

### ★★★ So containment is not the safety measure. It is the harvest

> ★★★ **I WAS ABOUT TO TREAT LINING THE HEAP AS A PRECAUTION — A COST, A DUTY, A THING DONE SO AS NOT TO POISON ANYTHING.** ☠ **It is not a precaution. Every drop that escapes into gravel is a drop of the product I came for.** ★★★ **A leaking heap is not merely dangerous, it is a heap that does not work.**

★★ *And that resolves the siting problem completely, because it stops being a question about where the drainage can safely go.* **The drainage does not go anywhere. It goes into a sump.**

☠ ★★ **And the arsenic doubles the argument rather than complicating it.** *An hour ago containment was worth doing because the escaping liquid was the product. Now the escaping liquid is the product **and** a poison that does not break down and does not wash away* — ★★★ **two independent reasons for the same lining, which is the most comfortable kind of decision there is.**

✓ **Built it:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Base** | **Puddled clay, sloped to one corner** — *the same floor I have built for every leach I own* |
| **Bed** | Coarse stone over the clay so the liquor can run under the heap rather than through the floor |
| **Sump** | ★★ **A sunk, lined catch at the low corner, roofed against rain diluting what it has already collected** |
| **Heap** | **Crushed sulfide, loose and open, walled and turned by hand** |
| **Cover** | ⚠ **None.** *It needs the weather — this is the one process I own that wants to be rained on* |

⚑ **And it is built HERE, not at home.** ★★ *Hauling low-grade rock sixteen kilometres to rot it would be sixteen kilometres of carrying the part I throw away. **The heap stays at the ore; only the liquor travels.***
