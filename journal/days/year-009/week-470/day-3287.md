## ☠ ★★★ Then I understood what the rust was

**Sat under the arch out of the rain and looked at the ridge, and the whole thing turned over.**

**The four iron ores I was so pleased about yesterday — limonite, hematite, the ochres, the boxwork — are not a second, separate, lucky deposit. They sit in a band, and the band is exactly where the sulfide ought to be.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE IRON CAP IS THE SULFIDE, ROTTED. Pyrite at the surface has had ten thousand years of rain and air on it, and what rain and air do to pyrite is turn it into rust and acid and carry the rest away.** ★★★ **The rusty ground is a gravestone, and it is standing exactly over the body.**

★★ **Which explains this morning perfectly.** *The fresh sulfide is not scarce. **It is under the part that has already been destroyed**, and the destroyed part is the only part I can see from up here.*

> ★★★ **VIRTUE AND DEFECT, AGAIN, AND THIS TIME IT IS A SIGNPOST: the weathering that ruined the top of the ore is the FLAG THAT MARKS IT.** ☠ *Without the rust I would never have found the sulfide. Because of the rust, I nearly concluded there was not much of it.*

✓ **Dug through the cap in two places before dark.** ★ **It changes — ochre and crumb give way to hard, heavy, brassy, unweathered rock.** ★★ **That is the real deposit and it starts perhaps a body's length down.**

## ✓ Mapping by rock, not by find

**Spent the wet afternoon doing this properly, which meant putting the sacks down.**

> ★★★ **A FIND IS A POINT. A HOST ROCK IS A TERRITORY.** *I have spent nine years collecting points — a stain here, a float boulder there, a stake and a distance in the register — and a point tells you only about itself.* ★★ **Map the rock and you can say where the NEXT one will be without having walked there.**

|---|---|---|

★★ **The map is rough and it is already predictive.** *I walked to a fourth spot on the strength of it and the rock was what the map said it would be.*

⚠ **Not a complete survey.** *The cinnabar I have seen only as float and I do not yet know where it comes from — and mercury is the one thing here I would rather locate precisely than stumble into.*
