Midday **GASHOLDER-2-BELL-A-3208 @ CRAFT-WING** *(the failure)*:

| Step | Result |
|------|--------|
| **Staved up** | **Same method, smaller diameter **—** body **PASS** |
| **Crown** | **Let into a croze like a barrel head **—** **FAILED under test**** |
| **What happened** | **Loaded the bell with air and **the crown lifted and hissed at the croze** |
| **Why** | ****A barrel is squeezed inward by its bands and its head is pushed *in* by the liquid. An inverted gas bell is the opposite — the gas pushes the crown *outward*, straight out of its groove.** A croze holds nothing against that |
| **Read** | ****This is a real structural difference, not sloppy work.** Rig 1's bells were blown glass in one piece, so the question never came up |

**BELL-CROWN-FIX-3208** *(designed, cut tomorrow)*:

| Fix | Notes |
|-----|-------|
| **Cross battens** | **×2 oak battens over the crown, **tied down to the bell's own bands**, so the crown load goes into the hoops instead of the groove |
| **Or tie rods** | **Through-rods crown to skirt ring — stronger, costs iron |
| **Call** | ****Battens first** — iron is dearer than oak and the pressure here is only the bell's own weight |
| **Also** | ****Crown seam gets bitumen from the inside**, where the pressure presses the seal into the joint instead of off it |
