|------------|--------|

| **Bell B** | ****~30 ml and stalled**** |
| **Diagnosis** | ****Lead sulfate, not lead dioxide.** White PbSO₄ is an **insulator** — it skins the plate and chokes the current off. Brown PbO₂ is the conductive one you want. **Same metal, same acid — the difference is current density** |
| **Root cause** | ****The cell is too weak.** ~3 ml of acid in 400 ml barely conducts · low current spread over a 50 × 45 mm plate means low density everywhere, and a lead anode at low density **sulfates instead of forming** |
