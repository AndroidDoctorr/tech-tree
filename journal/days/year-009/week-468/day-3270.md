## ☠ ★★★ THE CAMPUS BRIDGE — the concrete is perfect and the GROUND IS LEAVING

**Concrete abutments, upgraded more than once. There is nothing here to rot, which is precisely why I have never worried about it.**

**Probed anyway. Every timber sound, every concrete face sound, deck sound.**

**Then I put the plumb cord in the water on the downstream side of the south footing, the way the kit was packed for, and it went down and kept going.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE BED HAS BEEN CUT OUT FROM UNDER THE DOWNSTREAM FOOTING. A hole, deeper than the footing is set, hard against it.** *The bridge is in excellent condition. **It is standing on ground that is leaving.***

★★ **And that is the lesson, and it is sharper than the one I set out with:**

> ★★★ **I UPGRADED THIS BRIDGE TO CONCRETE TO MAKE IT PERMANENT, AND CONCRETE IS COMPLETELY INDIFFERENT TO BEING UNDERMINED.** ★★ **Every upgrade I made addressed the ROT clock. None of them touched the SCOUR clock, and the scour clock runs on WATER, not on years** — *and this is a real tributary against my own campus, which is the most water of any crossing I own.*
>
> ☠ ★★★ **ROT AND SCOUR ARE DIFFERENT CLOCKS, AND MAKING A BRIDGE BETTER AGAINST ONE DOES NOTHING AT ALL AGAINST THE OTHER. Worse — it buys CONFIDENCE, and the confidence is what stopped me looking.**
>
> ★ **Scour does not announce itself the way rot does. Rot you can smell and probe. A scour hole is UNDER WATER and everything above it looks perfect right up until it does not.**

### The fix

**Riprap apron — graded stone packed into the hole and out downstream, big stone on the outside so the current cannot sort it away.** ★★ **The principle is that water cuts where it ACCELERATES, and it accelerates where the flow is squeezed past the footing.** *You do not fight the current; you give it a bed it cannot pick up.*

⚠ **Started it with what I could carry. It needs a proper stone lap — filed as a hero, not a touch.** **BQ dropped to ~1.5 until the apron is in.** ★ *And it is the crossing on my own doorstep, so the stone lap is cheap — no cart trip, just work.*

★★ **And a standing read added: PLUMB EVERY FOOTING, EVERY SPRING, AFTER THE HIGH WATER.** *It costs two minutes per crossing and it is the only way the clock I was not tracking gets read at all.*

## Bitumen — the seeps are good, and they are not oil

**Asi margin bench, ~6 km. Three seeps as remembered, one of them properly generous: a black weep out of a shaley bench, soft in the sun, tarry and stringy, pooling in a hollow the size of a hearth.**

**~40 kg lifted cold into the crocks, sand-dusted so the lumps do not weld.**

### ⚠ But I should be clear with myself about what I have got

> ⚠ ★★★ **A SEEP GIVES THE HEAVY END ONLY. Whatever light fraction was ever in this has spent a very long time evaporating off at the surface** — **what is left at a seep is, by definition, what did not leave.** ★★ **"Prospecting for oil" and "collecting bitumen" are not the same activity, and I have been saying them as if they were.**

★★ **Which is fine, because the job I actually need it for wants the heavy end:**

| Use | |
|---|---|
| ★★★ **DAMP-PROOF COURSE** | **The gate on the August plaster. This trip was the dependency** |
| **Caulking · roof seal · hafting mastic** | Immediate |
| **Road surfacing** | *Already proved — ASPHALT-FIELD-1, PINARUS trial strip d3027* |
| ⚠ **Lamp fuel / light fractions** | **NOT from a seep. Would need DESTRUCTIVE DISTILLATION** — ⚠ *hotter and filthier than the turpentine still, heavy smoke, and the residue is coke rather than a clean bottom* |

★ **Filed: the still can wait. The DPC cannot, and the DPC is what I came for.**

### ⚠ ★★ Could I dig down to the liquid? — asked, and answered with a free test instead of a shovel

> ☠ ★★★ **A SEEP IS THE TOP OF A CONDUIT, NOT THE LID ON A TANK.** *The oil came up a crack from somewhere that may be far below and far sideways.* ★★ **Digging under a tar mound usually finds the same tar, and then rock. There is no pool down there waiting.**

★★ **But not always — an oil-soaked sand at shallow depth is a real thing, and there is one cheap way to tell the difference:**

> ★★★ **SCOOP THE POOL EMPTY AND COME BACK IN A WEEK.** **If it refills, something is FEEDING it and there is flow behind the face. If it does not, it is the dead top of an exhausted crack and a pit buys me nothing.** ★ *Costs an afternoon of nothing, answers the question honestly, and it is a STATE trigger rather than a guess.*

☠ ★★★ **AND IF IT DOES REFILL, THE ANSWER IS STILL NOT A SHAFT — BECAUSE I WORK ALONE.**

> ☠ ★★★ **Hydrocarbon vapour and sour gas are HEAVIER THAN AIR and pool in the bottom of a hole.** *Sour gas deadens the sense of smell at exactly the concentration that kills, so the warning stops before the danger does.* **A man goes down a ladder and does not come back up, and no flame can be carried down to see by.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **THIS IS THE FIRST HAZARD MY PREPAREDNESS CANNOT BUY DOWN, BECAUSE PREPAREDNESS FOR A CONFINED SPACE *IS A SECOND PERSON.* A solo deep shaft has no rescue, and everything else — rope, lamp, forced air — assumes somebody is on the other end of it.** ⚠ *And respawn is not an answer: I would wake up at home with nothing on me and the hole would still be there.*

★★ **What is allowed: a WIDE, BENCHED, OPEN TRENCH — never a shaft.** *Gas does not pool in something open to the sky and wide enough to walk out of.* **Never deeper than I can climb out of unaided, and a bellows and pipe if it ever gets close.**

★★★ **Verdict: run the refill test, then decide. TRENCH, NOT SHAFT, and landscaping first — the flowers have a deadline and the tar does not.**
