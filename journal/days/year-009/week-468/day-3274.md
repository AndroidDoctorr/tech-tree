## ☠ ★★★ SKEP-2 RENDER — ~190 g

**SKEP-1 gave ~850 g on d3265. Expected SKEP-2 to give something close. Lifted the basket and the comb was grey, webbed, and full of grit.**

> ☠ ★★★ **WAX MOTH.** *Silk galleries tunnelling through every dark comb, frass like coarse sand in the bottom of the skep, and the old brood comb reduced to a matted felt.* **The white and pale comb was untouched.**

### ★★★ And the reason they ignored the clean comb is the whole lesson

> ☠ ★★★ **WAX MOTH LARVAE DO NOT WANT THE WAX. THEY WANT THE COCOONS.** *They eat the shed larval skins, the silk and the pollen worked into old brood comb — the protein.* ★★★ **So they destroy exactly the comb that is mostly cocoon, and walk past clean honey comb, which is mostly wax and has nothing in it they can digest.**
>
> ★★ **Which is the SAME FACT as d3265, read from the other end.** *Old brood comb is barely made of wax — I learned that from a poor render. **The moth learned it first, and it eats accordingly.*** ★ **Two independent measurements of the same thing, and they agree.**

### ☠ ★★ Nine days, and the nine days crossed a threshold

| | |
|---|---|
| **SKEP-1** | **Driven, sat, rendered d3265** · *cool* · ★ **~850 g** |
| **SKEP-2** | **Driven d3253, sat, rendered d3274** · ☠ **NINE DAYS LONGER, AND WARMER** · ☠ **~190 g** |

> ☠ ★★★ **AN EMPTY COMB IS NOT STORED. IT IS EXPOSED.** *A comb inside a live colony is defended every hour of the day — bees patrol it, chew out the eggs, throw the larvae out.* ★★★ **The moment I drove the colony out, the comb stopped being guarded and started being FOOD.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **AND I WROTE THE RENDER DATE IN DAYS WHEN THE CLOCK IS ACTUALLY TEMPERATURE.** *"~d3274" was a fine number in a cold April. The moth's development rate is set by warmth, and the last week was warm.* ★★ **Same shape as the rett and the crust: a calendar trigger standing in for a state trigger, and the state moved.**

### What it changes

| | |
|---|---|
| ★★★ **RENDER IMMEDIATELY** | **A driven skep goes to the render bench when the last brood hatches, not on a date.** *Waiting buys nothing — the wax is already made* |
| ★★★ **STORED COMB WANTS LIGHT AND DRAFT** | ☠ **The moth wants dark, still and warm — which is exactly what "put it safely in a box" provides.** **Open rack, airy, lit. The storage that feels careless is the correct one** |
| ★★ **Store only PALE comb** | **Dark comb is moth bait and yields almost nothing anyway.** *Render dark comb the day it comes out; bank pale comb* |
| ★ **Cold kills them** | **A winter-rendered comb is safe. A late-spring one is on a timer** |

★ **Recovery: the larvae went to the fish traps as bait, and the matted slumgum burns.** ★★ **And the constraint is still CLOSED — 850 + 190 = ~1.04 kg, against ~110 g for all of Y10 before this.** *This was a loss of upside, not a return to short.*

## ★★★ BACK-DRAINS — and the first cut taught me the terrace is not what I said it was

**Started T-2, the worst of the three. Dug the trench at the foot of the cut face in the rain, on purpose, so I could see it working.**

> ☠ ★★★ **I CALLED A TERRACE "A TROUGH WITH A WALL AT THE UPHILL END." THAT IS WRONG, OR AT BEST HALF.** *The trench filled from the FACE, not from the sky — a steady weep all along the cut, on a day of light showers, with more water coming out of the wall than falling on the bench.*
>
> ★★★ **A HILLSIDE CARRIES WATER INSIDE IT, DOWNHILL, THROUGH THE SOIL. WHEN I CUT A BENCH I PUNCHED A HOLE IN THAT, AND THE WATER THE SLOPE USED TO CARRY PAST NOW COMES OUT OF MY WALL.** ★★ **The bench is not collecting rain. It is standing in the slope's plumbing.**

★★ **Which is why the back-fall mattered so much more than a finger of slope should** — *it was not catching the occasional shower, it was catching a continuous supply.* ★ **And it explains the thing I never explained: why those benches were wet days after rain stopped.**

### ☠ ★★ So a surface gutter would have failed

> ★★★ **THE WATER ARRIVES BELOW GRADE, SO THE DRAIN MUST GO BELOW GRADE.** *A channel scratched along the foot of the cut would have caught the runoff and let the seepage walk straight under it into the bench.* **A FILTER DRAIN — a stone-filled trench cut down past the seepage — or nothing.**

| | |
|---|---|
| ★★ **Cut BELOW the weeping line, not to it** | **Find the line by digging in the wet. It is visible for about a day a fortnight and invisible the rest of the time** |
| ☠ ★★★ **SILT KILLS A FILTER DRAIN, SILENTLY** | **Fines wash in and cement the voids, and it fails from the inside with no sign on the surface** → ★★ **GRADE THE FILL: coarse stone in the core, progressively finer outward, so the soil never touches the voids** |
| ★★ **A rodding eye at each end** | *Per d3272 — **a buried thing with no way to inspect it is a thing I will find out about by flood*** |
| ★ **Fall it to somewhere CHOSEN** | **Straight into the terrace outfall, not "downhill"** |

**Done: T-2 trench cut and graded-filled, ~11 m. T-1 and T-3 set out and started.**
