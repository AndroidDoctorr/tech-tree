## ★★★ CAMPUS-BRIDGE — the scour is not something the stream is doing to me

**Went down with the barrow to start setting the apron. Could not set a stone — the water is up and the colour of tea, and I cannot see the bed.** ★ **So I stood and watched it instead, which turned out to be the better half of the day.**

### ☠ ★★★ The footing makes the hole

> ★★★ **THE WATER DOES NOT SIMPLY RUN PAST THE FOOTING. It piles against the upstream face, DIVES, and rolls back on itself in a collar that wraps right round the base — and that roll is what is digging.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **SO THE HOLE IS NOT WHERE THE CHANNEL RUNS FASTEST. IT IS AT THE OBSTRUCTION, BECAUSE THE OBSTRUCTION IS WHAT MAKES IT.** *I put a solid thing in a moving stream and the stream is excavating around it. The bridge is not a victim of the scour. The bridge is the cause of it.*

★★ **Which settles a question I had been carrying wrong.** I had been thinking of the scour as damage — something that happened, that I repair, that is then repaired. ☠ **It is not an event. It is a permanent condition of having built there,** and it will run again every single time the water comes up.

### ☠ ★★ And it means a flat apron of stone is the wrong answer

**I had been picturing this as filling a hole. Tip in enough rock, hole gone.** ★★★ **Two ways that fails, and I would have found both of them the hard way.**

| | |
|---|---|
| ☠ ★★★ **The armour sinks through the bed** | **Big stone straight onto fine bed does not sit on it — the current pumps the fines UP THROUGH THE GAPS between the big stones and carries them off, and the armour settles quietly into the hole it was laid to fill.** ★★ **It wants a graded bed under it: fine, then medium, then the big stone on top** |
| ☠ ★★ **The edge unzips** | **Water gets under the outer lip, lifts the first stone, and then has a new outer lip to work on.** ★ **Key the toe in BELOW the scour line or the apron comes apart from the outside in** |

> ★★ **I built exactly that graded filter yesterday, in the T-3 drain, for completely unrelated reasons — and did not recognise it today until I had talked myself into the problem from scratch.** *Fine under coarse so the fine cannot escape through the coarse. One principle, two jobs, twenty-four hours apart, and I nearly missed it the second time.*

### ★★★ So: build it to fail gracefully

**If the scour is permanent, an apron that seals the hole is a lie I would have to keep re-telling.**

> ★★★ **LAY A DELIBERATE SURPLUS IN A THICK COLLAR AT THE OUTER EDGE, SO THAT WHEN THE BED CUTS DEEPER THE STONE ROLLS DOWN AND ARMOURS THE NEW FACE ON ITS OWN.** ★★ **An apron that repairs itself, out of stone I place once.**
>
> ★ *I cannot beat the vortex. I can make sure that every time it digs, it buries itself a little further.*

★ **Angular stone only — rounded cobble rolls, angular keys together.** ⚠ **And the big stone goes outermost, where the water is fastest, not thrown in the middle where it is easy to reach.**
