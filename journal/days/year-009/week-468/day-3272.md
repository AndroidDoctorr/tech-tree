## ⚠ First, a correction to my own framing yesterday

**I wrote as though drainage were a thing I had not thought about. That is not true and I should not let the note stand.** *The terracing WAS a drainage project. The impluvium has been upgraded three times. The ditches exist and were deliberately cut bigger than the water I had.*

> ★★ **What is actually new is narrower and it is still worth having: "BIGGER THAN I NEED" IS A GOOD INSTINCT AND IT IS NOT A SPECIFICATION.** *Bigger than the water I had then. **I have never once computed bigger than WHAT.***

### ★★★ And the worry about eventual rebuilds has a cheaper answer than rebuilding

> ★★★ **A DRAIN NETWORK GAINS CAPACITY BY DIVISION, NOT BY ENLARGEMENT.** ☠ *Enlarging a buried main means digging up everything built over it.* ★★ **But I can always add a SECOND OUTFALL, split a catchment, or hold water back — and every one of those is surface work on ground I have not committed yet.**

| Instead of a bigger main | |
|---|---|
| ★★ **Split the catchment** | **Two or three independent outfalls, each serving a quadrant.** *No single pipe has to carry the campus, and one blockage is not one flood* |
| ★★ **Store, then release** | ★★★ **The impluvium is already this — it is not a cistern that happens to catch rain, it is a BUFFER, and a buffer turns a peak into a trickle.** **More small storage beats one large pipe** |
| ★★ **Keep ground permeable** | *Water that never enters the network needs no network* |

★★★ **So the eventual "major rebuild" is probably avoidable entirely, and the thing to protect is not pipe diameter — it is KEEPING ROOM FOR A SECOND OUTFALL.** ⚠ **That is a LAYOUT decision, which means it has to be made now, on the plan, before anything is built over the line it would take.**
