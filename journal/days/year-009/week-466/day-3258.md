## ★★ FIRST DISCOVERY, BEFORE ANY WIRE WENT ANYWHERE: a cell is not a resistor

I have been testing GEN-WW-1 into R-BIG-1 for ten days and quietly assuming a load is a load. **It is not.**

> ★★ **A RESISTOR CONDUCTS FROM ZERO.** Any voltage at all pushes some current through it, in proportion. **AN ELECTROLYTIC CELL HAS A THRESHOLD — a DECOMPOSITION VOLTAGE — and below it essentially NOTHING HAPPENS.** Push half of what it needs and you do not get half the copper. **You get no copper.** Above it, current climbs steeply.
>
> **A cell is a BACK-PUSH plus a resistance, not a resistance.** It pushes back against me with a voltage of its own, and only what I have *over and above* that does any work.*

**And the size of the back-push is something I CHOOSE, by choosing the anode.** That turned out to be the whole design decision of the day.

| Anode | What happens | Voltage cost |
|---|---|---|
| **SOLUBLE — a copper anode** | **Copper dissolves off one plate and lands on the other. **A TRANSFER. Nothing is decomposed, no gas is made** | **Almost nothing |
| **INERT — a carbon rod** | **Copper lands on the cathode and **OXYGEN comes off the anode** — and prising oxygen out of water is expensive | **A volt and a half of back-push, and then some |

> ★★ **These are two different trades, not two ways of doing one thing.** A soluble anode **REFINES** copper I already have — cheap, but it cannot make me any more of it. **An inert anode WINS copper out of ore liquor, which is the only one of the two that is actually a source.**

**Inert it is. Carbon rod, hard-baked.** And I paid the volt and a half knowingly rather than discovering it as a failure.*

## ★★ THE THING THAT MAKES THE ACID CEILING SURVIVABLE

Writing out what happens at each electrode, the accounting came out better than I expected.

- **Acid + azurite → copper in solution**
- **At the cathode: the copper comes out as metal**
- **At the anode: oxygen goes off — AND THE ACID IS PUT BACK TOGETHER**

> ★★ **THE ACID GOES ROUND. It is a BUCKET, NOT A FUEL.** It carries copper from the ore to the cathode, drops it, and returns to the ore for more. **The only losses are what stays wet in the spent residue and what I spill.**
>
> **This is why ~5 ml of vitriol can eventually work through 1.4 kg of azurite — not all at once, but CYCLE AFTER CYCLE.** The ceiling on copper was never the acid. **It was always the CURRENT, and the current is now a water wheel.**

⚠ **This relief is for COPPER ONLY.** Anything that actually *consumes* acid — and most of the chemistry tree does — is still standing on 4.3 ml. **Dörtyol does not get cancelled by today.**

## ★★ THE SECOND DISCOVERY, AND I THINK IT IS THE BIGGER ONE: the cell WEIGHS CHARGE

The copper landing on the cathode is not vaguely related to the current. **It is fixed to it.**

> ★★ **EVERY UNIT OF CHARGE THAT CROSSES BRINGS A FIXED WEIGHT OF COPPER WITH IT. Mass deposited = current × time, exactly.**
>
> **SO THE CELL IS AN INSTRUMENT. It is a COULOMETER — it weighs electricity.**

**And that is not a curiosity, it is a CHECK.** My unit of current currently rests on one artifact — the tangent galvanometer — and a thing measured only one way is a thing measured on faith.

> ★★ **I now have TWO INDEPENDENT ROADS TO THE UNIT OF CURRENT: the galvanometer's needle, and a BALANCE.** One goes through magnetism and the earth's field; the other through nothing but **weight and a clock.** **They share no assumption whatever, so if they agree, they are both right.**
>
> **And my balance is better than my needle. **There is a real chance the cell is the more accurate instrument and the galvanometer is the one that needs correcting.

**Weighed the cathode dry before it went in. Marked the hour. That is the whole experiment.**

★ **And the Daniell cell turns out to do a second job too: it is a **POLARITY** standard as well as a voltage one. ⚠ **Get the leads backwards and the cathode DISSOLVES** — I would have run for six hours and come back to less copper than I started with.* **Checked against DANIELL-CELL-1, then painted the generator terminals.

## The run

**Iron sweetening first**, per standing practice — excess ore stirred into the liquor until the fizzing stopped. **Never skip this on a production run.** *Iron left in solution does not plate out; it gets reduced at the cathode, re-oxidised at the anode, and goes round and round **eating current and depositing nothing.** A parasite that bills me for work it does not do.*

**Big flat cathode. Carbon anode. Liquor stirred. Leads on. Collar dropped onto WW-2.**

### ★★ And then the trade I did not see coming — the generator and the cell want OPPOSITE things

| | |
|---|---|
| **The generator wants a LOW-resistance load** | That is how it delivers power |
| **The cell wants LOW CURRENT DENSITY** | **Or the copper comes down as black sponge instead of sheet** |

> ★★ **CURRENT DENSITY — current per unit of cathode AREA — DECIDES THE QUALITY.** Copper can only arrive as fast as fresh copper can reach the plate. **Drive it harder than the liquor can resupply and the layer next to the cathode runs empty, the cell starts tearing up water instead, and you get powder.**
>
> **CU-LEACH-1's "spongy" cathode was never a mystery. Sponge IS the signature of too much current in too little area.**

**The two demands are reconciled by AREA, and only by area.** A big cathode takes the generator's full current *and* plates gently per square inch. **Stirring is the other half** — it is what keeps the liquor at the plate from running out. **Not optional, and I had been treating it as tidiness.**
