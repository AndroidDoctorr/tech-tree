## ★★ HIVE-6 — eggs, and the egg told me its own date

Went down expecting to carry a comb of eggs over from HIVE-3. **Did not need it.**

**Pollen going in at the door before I even opened her.** Inside: **a neat central patch of eggs, one to a cell, on the base not the wall.** *A queen, not a laying worker.*

### And then the thing I did not expect to get for free

> ★★ **An egg stands UPRIGHT on day one, LEANS on day two, and LIES FLAT on day three.** These were **leaning.** ★★ **So she began laying two days ago — the egg is a clock and it runs backwards.**

**Two to four days pass between mating and the first egg.** Which puts her mating flight **on or about d3257.**

> ★★ **d3257 IS THE DAY I STOOD IN FRONT OF THAT HIVE AND WROTE THAT THE DRONES WERE UP AND THE AFTERNOON HAD GONE WARM AND STILL — "exactly her weather."** I made that call **from the doorstep with the lid on**, and five days later **the geometry of an egg confirmed the date independently.**
>
> **That is the first time a prediction I made here has been checked by a completely separate observation and come back right.** *And the doorstep reading — the free one, the one that costs the colony nothing — is what made it.*

⚠ **She is unproven yet.** *A patch of eggs is not a laying pattern.* **Read her again in ~a week: solid, even, one per cell = a well-mated queen. Scattered and patchy = poorly mated, and she will need replacing before the summer flow.**

I might have had a live, late queen and read the non-result as a failure.*

> ★★ **THE TEST COMB IS A DIAGNOSTIC BEFORE IT IS A REMEDY. Give them a comb of eggs and WATCH WHAT THEY DO WITH IT:**

## ★★ THE ASSAY, RERUN PROPERLY — and it cost me to find out

Three measurements this time, each removing one variable, exactly as I should have done on d3261.

| Comparison | What it removes | New copper better by |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn wire vs R-STD-1, as they were *(d3261)* | **Nothing. Three variables** | **~4%** ⚠ *uninterpretable* |
| **Both annealed identically** | **The cold work** | ★ **~7%** |
| **Raw plate, four-terminal, vs annealed standard** | **The cold work AND the melt** | ★★ **~11%** |

### What that decomposes to

- **The metal itself: ~11%.** *Electrowon copper genuinely conducts far better than anything I have smelted.*
- **The drawing hid ~3 points.** *My sample was fresh off the die and hard; cold work raises resistance.*
- ☠ **The melt cost ~4 points. I burned a third of my own advantage in the crucible, and then measured what was left.**

> ☠ ★★ **THIS IS THE LESSON AND IT IS NOT THE ONE I EXPECTED. I assumed loose controls would make an answer NOISY. They did not — THEY SYSTEMATICALLY HID MOST OF IT.** The confounds all pushed the same way, so the sloppy experiment did not give me 11% ± something. **It gave me a confident, quiet, wrong 4%.**
>
> ★★ **A bad control does not widen the error bar. It moves the answer and tells you nothing is wrong.**

★ **Annealing copper is nothing like steel** — heat to a dull red and **it does not matter how it cools.** *Quench it, air it, walk away; copper does not care. Doing both samples on the same afternoon in the same fire is what made them comparable.*

### ★★ FOUR-TERMINAL-JIG-1 works, and it changed what is measurable here

Heavy clamps outboard to push current in, **two knife-edge voltage taps at a fixed spacing further in.** The taps read a voltage across a known length of the sample and **carry almost no current, so their own contact resistance drops almost nothing.**

> ★★ **I can now measure a resistance far below my own connections.** *Shunts, strips, plates, contact quality, a single joint.* **The clamp is out of the argument for good.**
