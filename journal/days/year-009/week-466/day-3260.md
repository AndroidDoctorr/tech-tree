## ★★ ASSAY ONE — density, and why I did NOT use a measuring jar

The plate weighs **7.1 g**. That is about **0.8 cm³ of copper**, and my first instinct — drop it in water and read the rise — is hopeless. **I would be trying to read eight-tenths of a millilitre off a scratch on glass.**

> ★★ **Weigh it in air. Then weigh it again hanging in water. The DIFFERENCE is the weight of the water it pushed aside, and water's weight and volume are the same number.** **Density out of two weighings and no volume measurement at all** — and my balance is far better than any mark I can scratch on a jar.

⚠ **One thing nearly spoiled it:** the plate came out of the water reading too light, twice running. **Bubbles.** An electrodeposit is faintly rough and surface tension pins air to it, and **every trapped bubble lifts the sample and reads as LESS DENSE — which is to say, falsely IMPURE.** Wetted it thoroughly and worked the surface with a brush under water before the reading.

### The number

| | |
|---|---|
| In air | **7.1 g** |
| Apparent loss in water | **~0.80 g** |
| **Density** | ★ **~8.88** |
| Copper should be | **8.96** |

**About 99% of what copper ought to weigh for its size.**

> ⚠ **And I must not oversell that. DENSITY CATCHES POROSITY, NOT CHEMISTRY.** A deposit riddled with tiny voids and a deposit carrying an impurity **read exactly the same way.** ★ **All this number really says is: nothing heavy and nothing light is in there in bulk, and the plate is close to solid.** *A screen, not a verdict.*

## ★★ ASSAY TWO — resistivity, which defeated me, usefully

This was supposed to be the good one. Conductivity collapses with impurity far faster than density moves, so the bridge should be the sensitive instrument. **It could not read the sample at all, and the reason is worth more than the reading would have been.**

> **A short fat plate has almost no resistance.** Resistance wants length and hates cross-section, and this thing is **all cross-section and no length.** Its resistance is far below anything SLIDE-WIRE-BRIDGE-1 can see — **and worse, it is far below the resistance of my own clip contacts**, so what I would actually be measuring is how hard I squeezed the clamp.

### ★★ Which is exactly the problem four terminals were invented for

> ★★ **USE FOUR LEADS, NOT TWO. Two heavy leads push the current in at the ends. Two separate fine leads tap the voltage further in.**
>
> **The contact resistance of the current leads sits in the current path, where it steals voltage from the source and nobody cares. The voltage taps carry almost NO current — so however filthy their contacts are, they drop almost NO voltage across themselves.** ★★ **The clamp stops being part of the measurement.**

**That is the technique that makes small resistances measurable at all, and I will want it for every shunt, every wire QC run and every strip of metal I ever test.** *Filed. Build the jig.*

### But I am still not assaying THIS plate

Even four-terminal, 7 g in a slab is marginal. **The honest fix is to make the sample the right shape: melt it and draw it into fine wire.** 7 g goes to about **11 m of 0.3 mm**, which the bridge reads comfortably.

⚠ **And I am not doing that to this plate.** It is the first copper the river ever made and it is reserved. **Run the cell four more days, take a plate I can afford to destroy, and assay that one.** *The cell makes more; the first one is only first once.*

★ **And the comparison is better than an absolute anyway: R-STD-1 is ten metres of my BEST PREVIOUS copper.** Bridge the new wire against it, same gauge, same length. ★★ **I do not need to know either resistivity. I need the RATIO, and the ratio is the improvement.** *Null again. Compare, do not measure.*

> ⚠ **Which raises something I had not thought through: if the new copper is purer, then R-STD-1 is not "1 R of copper" — it is 1 R of *whatever I had in Y9.*** **And that is fine.** A standard does not have to be true, it has to be **stable and reproducible** — the same lesson the Daniell cell taught. ★ **But it means the STANDARD IS THE ARTIFACT, NOT THE RECIPE. If R-STD-1 ever dies I cannot rebuild it from better wire and call it the same ohm.**

## ★★ ASSAY THREE — the one that cost nothing, and turned out to be the sharpest

I already had the two numbers. **The ammeter said what current flowed and the clock said for how long — so I knew the charge. And the balance said how much copper came of it.**

> ★★ **Divide one by the other and you have CURRENT EFFICIENCY: how much of the electricity actually became metal, and how much went somewhere else.** *No new apparatus. No new work. Two numbers I had already written down.*

**The charge should have laid down about 7.7 g. I got 7.1.** ★ **About 92%.**

**And the missing 8% is not lost, it is ACCUSED.** *Some went to the iron still cycling in the liquor — reduced at the cathode, re-oxidised at the anode, billing me for work it does not do. Some went to hydrogen coming off instead of copper.* ⚠ **92% says my iron sweetening is good but not complete.**

### ★★ And then the trap: I cannot have both of those numbers from one experiment

> ★★ **The coulometer and the efficiency are THE SAME TWO MEASUREMENTS READ IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS.** If I trust the ammeter, the weighing tells me the efficiency. If I trust the efficiency, the weighing tells me the ammeter is right. **I cannot learn both at once. One equation, two unknowns — and yesterday I was quietly planning to claim both.**

### Breaking it — and the answer is the same trick twice

> ★★ **Put a cell of KNOWN efficiency in series with the unknown one.** Same charge through both, exactly.
>
> **A cell that REFINES — clean bath, soluble copper anode, no iron — has almost nothing else it can possibly do with the current. Its efficiency is essentially total.** So it reports the true charge by weight alone, **with no ammeter in the argument at all.** ★★ **Then the dirty winning cell's weight gives its efficiency honestly, and the ammeter gets graded against both.**

> ★★ **Series is the answer to nearly everything in this cell.** *Same charge through both* is what gives equivalent weights; *same charge through both* is what calibrates efficiency. **It is the bridge's idea in another costume: do not measure a thing, compare it with a thing you trust.**

**That is tomorrow's build, and it is small: a second jar, a copper anode, clean liquor, one wire between them.**

## Discoveries

1. ★★ **DENSITY FROM TWO WEIGHINGS, NOT A VOLUME.** In air, then hanging in water — **the loss IS the displaced water, and weight and volume are the same number for water.** Far better than trying to read 0.8 ml off a jar.
2. ⚠ **BUBBLES READ AS IMPURITY.** Surface tension pins air to a rough deposit and **every bubble lifts the sample, making it read less dense.** Wet and brush under water before reading.
3. **~8.88 against copper's 8.96 — about 99%.** ⚠ **But DENSITY CATCHES POROSITY, NOT CHEMISTRY** — voids and impurity read identically. **A screen, not a verdict.**
4. ★★ **RESISTANCE WANTS LENGTH AND HATES CROSS-SECTION** — a plate is all cross-section, so its resistance is below both the bridge's floor **and my own clip contacts.** I would have been measuring how hard I squeezed.
5. ★★ **FOUR-TERMINAL MEASUREMENT: heavy leads push current in at the ends, separate fine leads tap voltage further in.** The voltage taps carry almost no current, **so however bad their contacts are they drop almost no voltage — the clamp stops being part of the measurement.** *The technique that makes small resistances readable at all.*
6. **Make the sample the right shape: melt and draw to ~11 m of 0.3 mm.** ⚠ **Not this plate — it is reserved. Run the cell four days and destroy the next one.**
7. ★ **Bridge the new wire against R-STD-1 and I need no absolute value — THE RATIO IS THE IMPROVEMENT.** *Compare, do not measure.*
8. ⚠ ★ **THE STANDARD IS THE ARTIFACT, NOT THE RECIPE.** R-STD-1 is 1 R of *the copper I had in Y9*. **If it dies I cannot rebuild it from better wire and call it the same ohm.** *Reproducible beats true — but only the object is reproducible.*
9. ★★ **CURRENT EFFICIENCY COSTS NOTHING — charge in, metal out, divide.** **~92%**, and the missing 8% is not lost but **accused**: iron cycling, and a little hydrogen. **Sweetening is good, not complete.**
10. ★★ **THE COULOMETER AND THE EFFICIENCY ARE THE SAME TWO MEASUREMENTS READ BACKWARDS. ONE EQUATION, TWO UNKNOWNS — I cannot learn both from one experiment**, and I was about to claim both.
11. ★★ **BREAK IT WITH A KNOWN CELL IN SERIES.** A **refining** cell *(clean bath, soluble copper anode, no iron)* has nothing else to do with the current, so **it reports the true charge by weight alone, with no ammeter in the argument.**
12. ★★ **SERIES IS THE BRIDGE'S IDEA IN ANOTHER COSTUME.** *Same charge through both* gives equivalent weights **and** calibrates efficiency. **Do not measure a thing — compare it with a thing you trust.**
