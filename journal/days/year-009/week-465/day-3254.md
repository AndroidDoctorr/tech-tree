## ⚠ RICOTTA-Y10-2 · **THIN** · *and the reason is that I hoarded the whey*

**7.35 L re-cooked. **~110 g out. *For that volume it should have been better.*

### ★ **The diagnosis: **whey is a STREAM, not a STOCK

> **I have been **banking whey, the way I bank everything — a crock on the cool step, topped up batch after batch, saved for a day I had time.
>
> **That is the wrong verb for this material.**
>
> **Most of that 7.35 L was **weeks old**. **Whey keeps souring after it leaves the curd** — it gets steadily more acid as it sits, and **ricotta is a re-cook that wants whey close to how it came off. The oldest litres in that crock had nothing left to give.
>
> **And it was ACID whey to start with**, which I already knew yields poorly — I just compounded it by letting it age on top.

### **Correction**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Run ricotta the SAME DAY as the cheese.** | **Every time. It is not a separate job, it is the back half of the cheese job** |
| **Old whey is not wasted — it just has different uses** | **PLASTER RETARDER** *(where sour is fine)* · **goat feed** · soil* |
| **And the June rennet changes this properly** | **Sweet whey gives far more ricotta than acid whey.** Once the thistle comes in, the back half of every cheese day is worth real food |

## ★ BARK TAN · **DEER-HIDE-Y10-1 → BELT LEATHER**

### **Why this hide is for belting, specifically**

> **The generator's belt train is the newest load-bearing thing on the campus and it is running on **whatever leather I had.** I now know exactly what a belt needs, because **the belt taught me last week.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Use the BUTT — the back and rump** | **Densest fibre, least stretch.** The belly is stretchy and useless for belting |
| **Cut belting ALONG the backbone** | The fibre runs that way — least stretch in the direction of pull |
| ★ **STRETCH IS THE ENEMY OF A BELT** | **A belt that stretches goes slack, and slack costs WRAP, and wrap is grip.** *That is last week's lesson arriving from the leather side* · **PRE-STRETCH under load before final lacing** |
| **Grain side to the pulley** | Smoother and denser — better grip, better wear |
| **Stuff it with tallow** | **Supple, water-resistant · **and I rendered deer tallow off this same animal three days ago |

### Method — **oak bark liquor, weak to strong**

- Hide fleshed, hair off, rinsed
- **Into the WEAKEST liquor first** — then progressively stronger over weeks

### ★★ **And here is the thing that stopped me: **LEATHER CASE-HARDENS TOO

> **If I put a raw hide straight into a strong tan liquor, the tannin strikes the surface, seals it, and the middle never tans at all.** Firm outside, raw and rotting inside.
>
> **That is the JERKY FAILURE. Exactly it. Same shape, different craft, four days apart.**
>
> ### **THE GENERAL RULE — worth more than either instance:**
>
> **ANY PROCESS THAT WORKS FROM THE OUTSIDE IN CAN BE RUINED BY HURRYING IT, BECAUSE HURRYING SEALS THE SURFACE AND STRANDS THE MIDDLE.**
>
> **Drying meat. Tanning hide. **And now I think about it — curing lime and concrete, and carburising steel.** **All of them: START WEAK, GO SLOW, LET THE FRONT MOVE INWARD AT ITS OWN PACE.
>
> **And in every case the finished-looking outside is the LIE.**

**Hide in the weak pit. Weeks, not days. Logged the ladder of liquors so I do not have to trust memory.**

*End Day 3254 · Cal-Y10 D109 · ~10 Apr. Grey and showery, a dairy-and-pits day. The ricotta was thin and it is my fault in a way I found interesting: I have been banking the whey. A crock on the cool step, topped up batch after batch, saved for a day I had time — which is how I treat every other material I own, and it is the wrong verb for this one. Whey goes on souring after it leaves the curd, and most of those seven litres were weeks old and had nothing left to give. Ricotta is the back half of the cheese job, not a separate job, and from now on it runs the same day. The old whey is not wasted, it just belongs in the retarder jar and the goats, where sour does not matter. Then the deer hide into the tan, and I picked it out for belting specifically, because the generator's belt train taught me last week exactly what a belt wants — the butt of the hide where the fibre is densest, cut along the backbone where it stretches least, grain side to the pulley, stuffed with tallow off the same animal, and pre-stretched under load before it is laced, because a belt that stretches goes slack and slack costs wrap and wrap is the whole of grip. And then I stopped with the hide in my hands, because I was about to put it into a strong liquor to save time, and a raw hide in strong liquor does exactly what my first jerky did: the tannin strikes the surface, seals it, and the middle never tans at all. Firm outside, raw and rotting within. The same failure, four days apart, in two crafts with nothing else in common. So it is not a fact about meat or about leather. Any process that works from the outside inward is ruined by hurrying it, because hurrying seals the surface and strands the middle — drying, tanning, and now that I think about it curing lime and carburising steel as well. Start weak, go slow, let the front travel in at its own pace. And in every one of those cases, the outside looking finished is precisely the lie.*
