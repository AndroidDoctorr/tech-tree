*End Day 3253 · Cal-Y10 D108 · ~9 Apr. Built the seventh hive before breakfast, which is the third time with that jig and it took a morning, and cut two spare bar sets while it was set up. Then the thing I had been waiting to do properly. Hive 5 spent a fortnight not laying because a driven colony arrives carrying nothing — it has to build its whole house out of its own body at eight of honey for one of wax, and I had simply let it. I do not have to let it any more. So before a single stroke on the basket, Hive 7 was furnished: a drawn comb from Hive 3 so the queen has somewhere to lay tonight with no wax bill at all, a comb of sealed brood so there is a workforce arriving in days, and thin honey-water in from the first hour rather than from whenever I noticed they were slow. Hive 5 was an experiment I ran on a colony; Hive 7 gets the answer to it. The drive itself took about twenty-five minutes rather than fifteen, because Hive 2 is the heavy one and there are simply more of them, and then the roar changed note the way it does and they began to walk up, and I saw her cross at the joint. Skep 2 is standing beside its replacement with the entrance open and the brood hatching out; it renders around d3274, three weeks after Skep 1. Which means every colony on this campus is now on movable comb — the aim I wrote down on the twenty-second of March, finished on the ninth of April — and two full baskets of comb are queued to render. Wax has been throttling the electrical work all year, and in five weeks it will simply stop being a constraint. Then the rack. Thirteen kilos of deer in, four and a bit out, weighed wet and dried until the weight had fallen by two-thirds and then stayed there, which is the part that matters: no plateau, no shell, and three cut at random were dry the whole way through. It is the first food I have ever made to a number instead of to a judgement, and the only reason I can tell the difference is that the failed batch looked exactly as convincing.*
