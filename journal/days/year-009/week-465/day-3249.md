## ★ **DOCTRINE · ONE CHANGE, THEN MEASURE** *(formalised d3249)*

> **I have always built in **replaceable parts, and I used to tell myself it was about repair. It is, but that is the smaller half.
>
> **A machine made of swappable pieces is a machine you can run an EXPERIMENT on.** Change one part, re-measure against the same baseline, and **the difference belongs to that part and nothing else.** **Replaceable parts are how you isolate a variable in a thing that has to keep working.**
>
> **And this only became possible **yesterday**, because **you cannot improve what you have not baselined. Before d3248, *"better"* was a feeling. Now it is a subtraction.
>
> **RULE: one change per measurement. If I do two things and it improves, I have learned that two things improve it, which is almost nothing.**

## CHANGE 1 · **CLOSE THE AIR GAP**

### First problem: **you cannot close a gap you have not earned**

> **The clearance is generous because I did not trust my own concentricity. **The gap is not set by how close I WANT the armature — it is set by how much the armature WOBBLES. Shim it closer than the wobble and it strikes the pole face.
>
> **So: measure the wobble first.**

### **RUNOUT GAUGE — a pointer is a lever, and a lever is an amplifier**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Build** | **A light pointer on a pivot, **short arm riding the armature, long arm swinging free over a scale |
| **Why** | **The long arm multiplies the motion.** A wobble too small to see becomes a visible swing. **This is the same trick as the balance beam and the slide wire: do not try to SEE a small thing, ARRANGE for it to move something big** |
| **Result** | **Spun by hand, the pointer swung — **and the wobble was NOT all in the core. Part of it was the shaft, part was the bearing |

### ★ **TRUE IT IN ITS OWN BEARINGS — the principle worth keeping**

> My instinct was to pull the armature, chuck it up, and dress it round.
>
> **That is the wrong instinct.** A perfectly round armature running in a slightly worn bearing still wobbles. **Do not make the PART round — make the ASSEMBLY run TRUE.**
>
> **So I dressed it **in situ, spinning in the very bearings it will live in**, cutting only where the pointer said high. **What comes out is not a round armature. It is an armature that runs true where it actually runs — and the bearing's own error is dressed out along with everything else.

**RUNOUT HIT — came in well.** Wobble cut to a fraction of what it was.

### Then, and only then, close the gap

- **Pole faces **re-dressed CONCAVE to match the armature's curve — a flat pole face over a round armature carries a big *average* gap even where it is nearly touching
- **Remember the gap is in the circuit TWICE** — once on each side
- Shimmed in to a hair over the measured runout

## ★★ **RE-MEASURE — and the disappointment is the discovery again**

| | **d3248 baseline** | **d3249 after gap** |
|---|---|---|
| **Open circuit** | **~1.5 GB | **~1.85 GB |
| **Loaded** | **~1.15 GB | **~1.40 GB |
| **Current** | **~0.45 I | **~0.55 I |
| **Power** | **~0.14 GB·I | **~0.21 GB·I |

> **+~25% on voltage, ~+50% on power, for one afternoon and no materials. Good.**
>
> **But I roughly HALVED the gap and I did NOT get anything like double the field. Why?**

### ★ **The answer, and it is the useful part of the whole day**

> **The magnet is not an infinite source of magnetism. It is part of the circuit, and it has a resistance of its own.**
>
> **While the gap was huge, **the gap was the whole story** and closing it paid nearly in proportion. **Now that the gap is small, the magnet's own internal reluctance is a comparable share of the loop — and squeezing the gap further is squeezing the part that is no longer the bottleneck.
>
> **So the measurement did not just tell me the gain. It told me WHERE THE NEXT WALL IS.** **The next lever is not a tighter gap. It is MORE AND BETTER MAGNET** — more rods, a shorter yoke path, or eventually **an electromagnet field**, which I can now afford to excite because I have a machine that makes current.
>
> ***I could not have known that without measuring before and after. One change, one measurement.***

## ⚠ **CAUGHT: THE FLYWHEEL IS THE WRONG FIX, AND I HAD IT ON THE LIST**

**I went to size a flywheel against the speed droop. **Stopped, because it does not work.

> **A flywheel stores energy across a FLUCTUATION. It cannot add average power.**
>
> **The wheel does not dip to 8 rpm and recover — **it SETTLES there.** That is the speed where the water's torque and the generator's drag balance. **A flywheel would arrive at 8 rpm just the same, only heavier, because there is no surplus anywhere in the cycle for it to store.
>
> **Nothing redistributes its way out of a steady droop. The only things that ADD power are more water and more head.**

**But it is not useless — it is filed against **the wrong problem**:**

| Problem | Flywheel? |
|---|---|
| **Steady speed droop under load** | **NO. Only more water / more head** |
| **Bucket-by-bucket torque pulsation** | **YES — and that ripple shows up in the output as a wobble in the voltage** |

**Re-filed: FLYWHEEL-WW2-1 → under "voltage ripple", not "droop".** *And if I build one: **put it on a FAST shaft, not the slow one — stored energy goes as the SQUARE of speed, so a small mass turning fast beats a large mass turning slow.**
