## ⚠ JERKY-TRIAL-1 · **CASE-HARDENED** · *and the trial did exactly its job*

**~0.35 kg of fresh goat** — deliberately a **small proving batch**, because I have never made this and I was not about to learn it on a hunt's worth of meat.

### **First: jerky is NOT the same thing as my smoked meat, and I nearly treated it as one**

| | **Smoked haunch *(what I already do)*** | **JERKY** |
|---|---|---|
| **Keeps** | **Months, **on a cool shelf** | **Months, in a BAG, in summer, being carried |
| **Water left in** | **A fair amount | **Almost none — that is the entire trick |
| **For** | **The larder | **THE PACK |

> **The expedition problem is not "will it keep", it is "will it keep with no cool shelf, at body heat, in a sack."** That is what drives the water out further than I have ever driven it.

### Method settled

1. **LEAN ONLY. Trim every scrap of fat.** *Fat does not dry — it goes rancid. **Fat is what spoils jerky, not bacteria.*** Goat is obliging here
2. **Slice WITH the grain** for a strip that holds together in a bag *(across the grain is tenderer and falls apart)*
3. **Thin and EVEN** — uneven strips means the thin ones are bricks before the thick ones are safe
4. **Salt first.** Salt pulls water out on its own before any heat, and **what is left is water the spoilage cannot get at** — the salt is doing half the preserving before drying starts
5. **Cold smoke over the rack — **smoke is not just flavour, it keeps flies off and slows the fat going off
6. **Screen it.** Flies lay on drying meat. A smoke haze and a screen

### ★★ **THE DONENESS TEST IS THE SCALE, and this is why the batch was worth making**

> **I weighed the strips wet and hung them **to a target weight, not to a look.** **Dry until it has lost about two-thirds of what it weighed.
>
> ***A measured process, not a guessed one. Same week, same idea.***

### **And the scale is what caught the failure**

> **Partway through, **the strips stopped losing weight — and they were nowhere near the target.
>
> **They looked done. Dark, firm, dry to the thumb. They were not.**
>
> **CASE HARDENING: I hung them too close to the heat, the outside dried into a shell, and the shell SEALED THE WATER INSIDE.** Cut one across and the middle was still soft and wet. **In a bag, that middle is where it rots — from the inside, invisibly, three hundred miles out.**
>
> **I would have packed these. By eye and by thumb they passed. THE WEIGHT IS WHAT FAILED THEM.**

### Fix

| | |
|---|---|
| **Low heat, lots of air** | **Airflow does the drying; heat only helps it along.** **I had that backwards** |
| **Thinner, more even** | A thick strip case-hardens first |
| **Read the weight CURVE, not just the weight** | **If weight stops falling before target, it is SEALED, not DONE.** Score it, drop the heat, more air |

**Batch: **~0.14 kg salvaged** *(re-scored, re-hung low and airy, came down to target)* · **~0.21 kg cut up and eaten this week — no loss worth counting, which is what a trial batch is for.

**JERKY-DOCTRINE-1 filed. Hunt next, with a method that works.**
