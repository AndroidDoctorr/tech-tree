## ★★ SKEP-1 RENDERED — the thing that has throttled the year

**Three and a half weeks since the drive. Brood all hatched out, basket empty of bees, combs mine.**

**Cut out, sorted, and rendered ON WATER — never dry.** *The water caps the temperature below scorching and the dross sinks.* **Strained through linen, cakes lifted cold.**

### ⚠ And the yield taught me what a skep comb actually IS

**About 6 kg of comb went in. Under a kilogram of wax came out** — and for a moment I thought I had done something wrong.

> ★★ **I had not. OLD BROOD COMB IS BARELY MADE OF WAX ANY MORE.** Every larva raised in a cell **spins a cocoon and leaves the skin of it behind**, and the bees varnish over it with propolis and raise the next one on top. ★★ **A comb that has raised twenty generations is a stack of shed larval skins glued together, with a thin wax scaffold running through it.**
>
> | Comb | Wax by weight |
> |---|---|
> | **Fresh white comb** | ★ **Nearly all of it** |
> | **Comb that has held honey only** | High |
> | **Old dark brood comb** | ⚠ **A small fraction — the rest is cocoon, propolis and dirt** |
>
> ★★ **This is the real reason the wax bank has been in GRAMS for three years, and it is a sharper reason than I had.** *It was never only that a skep cannot be opened. **It is that the comb I could scavenge was the oldest and worst comb in the hive** — the cappings and the black brood comb — and I was rendering the fraction with the least wax in it.*

★ **The slumgum residue is not waste** — fire starter, and it is most of what went in.

### ★★ THE NUMBER

**~850 g of clean wax.** *The whole year to date has been ~110 g.*

> ★★ **AN EIGHT-FOLD JUMP IN ONE AFTERNOON, AND SKEP-2 FOLLOWS IN NINE DAYS.** **Wax has been the binding constraint on the electrical work all of Y10 — every coat run, every insulation batch, every stalled winding. It is over.**
>
> ★★ **And going forward it is structurally different, not just bigger. On top bars I harvest HONEY comb and let the brood combs stay put** — ★ **so from now on I am rendering the RICH fraction and leaving the poor one in service, which is the opposite of what a skep forced on me.**

## ★★ HIVE-6 — the scale's blind spot, one day after I started trusting it

**Hefted it. Still light. No better than two days ago, and they have taken every drop of the syrup.**

**My first read was that something is wrong. So I opened her, which — as it turns out — was the only way to find out that nothing is.**

**The brood nest has grown. Noticeably. Eggs, young larvae, a widening patch, and comb drawn out past where it was.**

> ★★ **SHE IS NOT STARVING. SHE IS SPENDING. A small colony with a new laying queen converts food into BEES as fast as I can supply it — and brood weighs almost nothing while it is being made.** **The syrup went in and came straight back out as larvae and fresh comb.**
>
> ☠ ★★ **WHICH MEANS A COLONY THAT IS INVESTING AND A COLONY THAT IS STARVING LOOK IDENTICAL ON THE SCALE.** Both read flat. **The difference is not in the weight — it is in the BROOD, and the scale cannot see brood.**

★★ **This does not spoil yesterday's idea, it finishes it:**

> ★★ **THE SCALE TELLS ME WHEN TO LOOK. IT DOES NOT REPLACE LOOKING.** *A cheap measurement earns its place by being taken every day — and the price of that is that it is thin. **A flat line is a QUESTION, not an answer.***
>
> **Weight + one brood look = the whole picture. Neither alone is anything.**

⚠ ★ **And I very nearly acted on the thin reading.** *Two more days of that and I would have decided she was failing, and a colony pulling this hard is exactly the one I would have wrecked by interfering.*

**Kept feeding. Entrance stays reduced. Next read is the LAYING PATTERN at ~d3269, and that is a brood question, not a weight question.**
