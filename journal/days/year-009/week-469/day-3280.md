## ★★★ The block yard opens

**Built the mould and tamped the first blocks. `BC-2-BLOCK` from a standing start.**

### ★★★ Dry-tamped, and the reason is the whole method

> ★★★ **A WET MIX CANNOT LEAVE ITS MOULD UNTIL IT HAS SET, SO A WET-CAST YARD NEEDS ONE MOULD PER BLOCK.** ☠ ★★★ **AN EARTH-MOIST MIX, TAMPED HARD, HOLDS ITS OWN SHAPE THE INSTANT THE BOX LIFTS OFF — SO ONE MOULD MAKES EVERY BLOCK I WILL EVER LAY.**
>
> ★★ **That is not a refinement of casting. It is the reason the technique exists.** *The constraint designed the method.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Block** | ~40 × 20 × 20 cm, **two tapered cores** — conduit, rebar, less weight, and it cures from the inside as well as the outside |
| **Mould** | Strapped timber box, **draft on every face**, core formers tapered so they draw · lift the box UP, never tip it |
| **Tamp** | **Three lifts, each rammed before the next.** One deep fill tamps solid on top and stays loose at the bottom |
| ★★ **Handling** | **The block never moves until it is cured — you carry the BOARD.** *Green block handling is where a yard loses its week* |

### ★★ And the size is set by the five-hundredth one

> ★★ **I sized it to what I can lift comfortably ALL DAY, not to what I can lift.** *A block I can just manage is a block I will stop making.*

### ☠ ★★★ The cure is the opposite of everything I have done this week

> ☠ ★★★ **LIME-POZZOLAN DOES NOT DRY. IT REACTS WITH WATER, AND IF THE WATER LEAVES BEFORE THE REACTION FINISHES, THE REACTION SIMPLY STOPS AND NEVER RESTARTS.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **I HAVE SPENT THE WHOLE WEEK GETTING WATER OUT OF THINGS — the terraces, the seed, the jerky, the jar — AND THIS IS THE ONE MATERIAL WHERE I MUST KEEP IT IN.** ★★ **I have a drying habit and it will quietly kill a hundred blocks.** ⚠ **Damp sacking, shade, and no sun on a green block. A block left out to "dry" is a block that never cured at all.**

★ **Three blocks marked for the 7-, 28- and 90-day breaks.** ★★ *The rope-break rig does this job unchanged — a known load applied until something gives. Same rig, different material.*

## ☠ ★★★ Then the yard stopped at seven blocks

**Weighed out for the eighth and there was no pozzolan left.**

> ☠ ★★★ **`POZZ-TUFF-1` IS ~2.1 kg. THE ENTIRE BLOCK PROGRAMME IS RATE-LIMITED BY TWO KILOGRAMS OF CRUSHED TUFF, AND I HAVE BEEN TREATING LIME AS THE SCARCE INPUT BECAUSE LIME IS THE ONE I HAVE TO BURN.**
>
> ★★ **Lime is work. Pozzolan is a place I have to walk to, and I had never once counted it.**

### ★★★ And the answer was in the reject pile

> ★★★ **CRUSHED FIRED CLAY IS A POZZOLAN. Ground brick, kiln seconds, broken pot — all of it.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **AND IT MUST BE THE UNDERBURNT STUFF. Fire clay hard enough and it vitrifies into glass that will not react; the pozzolan is the brick that did NOT get hot enough — which is precisely the brick I have been stacking as failure for nine years.**

★★★ **Which is the d3278 rule for the second time in three days: a material's virtue and its defect are the same property read in two directions.** ★ *I did not have to work it out this time. I recognised it while I was standing there with the empty measure in my hand — which is the entire reason for writing a thing down as a rule instead of as an incident.*

⚠ **`CACO3-P7` already carries "plus underburnt returns" at ~4.3 kg, and there is a reject stack at every kiln on this campus.** **Grinding queued.**
