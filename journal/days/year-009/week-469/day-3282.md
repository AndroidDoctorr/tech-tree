## ★★★ What a hood is actually for

**I had it filed as the thing that takes the smoke away. It is not.**

> ★★★ **THE FLUE TAKES THE SMOKE AWAY. THE HOOD'S ONLY JOB IS TO HOLD THE SMOKE STILL LONG ENOUGH FOR THE FLUE TO GET TO IT.**
>
> ★★★ **A HOOD IS A BUCKET HELD UPSIDE DOWN.** *It works by being a container, not by pulling.*

☠ **And a flat canopy is a bucket with no sides.** *The plume rises, spreads out underneath, reaches the rim and rolls off it — and an island canopy has rim on all four faces, which is why an island is the hardest hood there is.* ★ **A wall hood gets three sides of containment for free from the building. This one gets none.**

### ★★ Why the lip, and why it costs nothing

> ★★ **THE LIP TURNS THE RIM INTO A WALL AND THE CANOPY INTO A RESERVOIR. Smoke pools under it instead of spilling, and the flue empties the pool at its own pace.**

☠ ★★ **The trade on any hood is brutal: hang it LOW and it captures well and you crack your head on it; hang it HIGH and it is comfortable and it does not work** *(the plume spreads as it climbs, so a high hood must also be a wide one)*.

> ★★★ **THE LIP IS THE WAY OUT OF THAT TRADE — it hangs at the EDGES, where nobody stands.** ★★ **It converts headroom I was not using into capture I need.**

★ **And there is a lip at the hearth apron already, put there to keep the fire in.** *A lip at the bottom to hold the fire, a lip at the top to hold its smoke. I built the first one three years ago without seeing it was half a pair.*

## ☠ ★★★ Then the two halves of the job turned out to be enemies

**Set out the louvers and stopped with the string in my hand.**

> ☠ ★★★ **THE LIP EXISTS TO STOP A CROSS-DRAFT STEALING THE PLUME. THE LOUVERS EXIST TO MAKE A CROSS-DRAFT.** *One line on a list, two items, in direct opposition — and I would have cut the openings at the height that felt natural, which is the height a person stands.*

☠ **Louvers at head height blow the plume out from under the canopy. That is not a ventilated workshop, that is a smoke machine with a chimney attached.**

### ★★★ Cross-draft LOW, dead air HIGH

> ★★★ **THE AIR HAS TO MOVE WHERE I AM AND STAND STILL WHERE THE SMOKE IS — and those are different heights, so both are possible at once.** **Inlets down at the skirting, nothing above bench height anywhere near the island.**

★★ **Which is the ordinary stack arrangement, arrived at backwards: low in, high out, and the fire drives it.** ★★★ **The hearth is the fan** — no moving parts, it runs precisely when there is something to clear, and it stops when there is not.

---

## ☠ ★★★ And then the real find, which was nothing to do with comfort

**The hub has TWO vertical paths on purpose — the ridge slot at the tower top for warm air, and the stack for smoke, kept separate since d2225 because a vent is not a flue cap.**

> ☠ ★★★ **TWO WAYS OUT OF A TIGHT BUILDING ARE TWO WAYS OUT COMPETING FOR ONE WAY IN — AND IF THE FLUE LOSES THAT COMPETITION, IT REVERSES AND THE HEARTH SMOKES INTO THE ROOM.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **THE RIDGE VENT CAN STRANGLE THE STACK.** *I built the two paths separate so they would not interfere, and they interfere anyway — not at the top, where I was looking, but at the bottom, where I never put anything.*

★★★ **So the louvers are not a comfort feature. They are the make-up air, and they are what stops the ridge vent and the flue fighting each other.** ★★ **I have been carrying them on the small-closes list for two hundred days as a nicety.**

### ☠ ★★★ I have built nothing but exits

> ☠ ★★★ **RIDGE SLOT, FLUE, FUME HOOD, KILN STACK, CELLAR HATCH — EVERY VENT ON THIS CAMPUS IS A WAY OUT. I HAVE NEVER ONCE SIZED A WAY IN.**
>
> ★★★ **AND THE REASON IS THE SAME REASON I NEVER COUNTED THE POZZOLAN TWO DAYS AGO: I COUNT WHAT I HAVE TO BUILD AND NEVER WHAT ONLY HAS TO BE THERE.** *Air is free and a hole is nothing, so neither ever appeared on a list.* ☠ **Free is not the same as present.**

⚠ ★★ **Every stack on this campus wants auditing for its inlet, not its outlet.** *The kiln and the forge first — they are the two that pull hardest.*

## ✓ Built, and tested with threads

**Low louvers cut on the two faces that do not look at the island.**

> ★ **Tested with damp straw for smoke and light threads pinned at the lip rim, at the louvers and at the flue throat.** ★★ **You cannot see air, so you have to give it something to move** — *and a thread that hangs dead is a reading too.*
