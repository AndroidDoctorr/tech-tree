## ✓ ★★★ Pozzolan — and the supply is now renewable

**Walked every kiln on the campus and pulled the reject stacks: underburnt brick, seconds, cracked tile, the broken-pot heap behind the pottery.** ★ **More than I expected, because I have been adding to those piles for nine years and never taking from them.**

> ★★★ **AND IT REFILLS ITSELF. Every firing throws some underburnt stock, so the block yard's limiting input is now tied to the KILN rather than to a walk out to a tuff bank.** ★★ **Two processes that were unrelated yesterday now feed each other, and the waste stream is the feed.**

⚠ **The cost is the grinding.** *It has to go to a flour, not a grit — a coarse powder barely reacts, because the reaction happens on the surface and a coarse grain has almost none.*

### ★★★ So put it on the wheel

> ★★★ **GRINDING IS EXACTLY WHAT WATER POWER IS FOR, AND I HAVE A BELT TREE STANDING IDLE HALF THE DAY.**

**Rigged a stone runner off the free collar at `WW-2` and fed it the crushed rejects.** ✓ **It grinds while I do something else, which is the whole argument for the wheel and only the second job I have ever given it.**

★★ **This changes the block yard from a labour-limited job to a materials-limited one** — *and materials I now make as a by-product of firing anything at all.*

## ✓ The mould, running

**Tamped through the afternoon on fresh pozzolan.** ★ *The mould is behaving — draft on the faces and tapered cores mean the box lifts clean every time, and the rhythm is quick once the mix is right.*

⚠ **Mix by the measuring box, never by eye** — *a block that is short of binder looks identical to one that is not, and it will keep looking identical until the day it is carrying a roof.*

## ☠ ★★★ Then: who waters the blocks while I am at Dörtyol?

**Stood looking at the damp sacking and realised I am about to leave for two weeks.**

> ☠ ★★★ **LIME-POZZOLAN CURES BY REACTING WITH WATER. DAMP SACKING NEEDS A MAN WITH A BUCKET EVERY DAY, AND FOR THE NEXT FORTNIGHT THERE IS NO SUCH MAN.** *Every green block on this campus would have stopped curing about four days after I walked out of the gate, and I would have come home to a stack that looked perfect.*

### ★★★ So sink them

> ★★★ **PUT THEM UNDER WATER. A submerged block cannot dry, needs nobody, and cures harder than a damp-sacked one ever will** — *the water is not a precaution against drying, it is the other half of the reaction, and more of it is better.*

**Cut a curing pit at the pad edge, puddled and fed off the `SLUICE-2` tail, and racked the blocks in on spacers so the water gets all round every face.**

> ★★ **THE TRIP FORCED A BETTER METHOD THAN THE ONE I WOULD HAVE CHOSEN.** ★ *I was going to water them by hand for ninety days because that is what was in front of me. A constraint I could not argue with produced the answer in an afternoon.*

⚠ **Test blocks racked separately and marked** — **d3287 · d3308 · d3370.** ☠ *The 7-day break falls while I am away and is simply lost; the 28 and the 90 are the ones that decide anything.*
