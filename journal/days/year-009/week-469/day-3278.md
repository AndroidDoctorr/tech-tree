## ⚠ ★★ CRUST-READ 2 — the effect is real and it is SMALLER

**`GYPSUM-STRIP-TRIAL-1`, second read, after a wholly different rain.** ✓ **The alternating pattern is still there — treated blocks crumble, control blocks pan.** ⚠ **But the gap has narrowed. Clearly.**

> ★★ **THIS IS THE READING I INSISTED ON HAVING, AND IT WALKED BACK THE FIRST ONE. That is not a disappointment, that is the method working.** *A single reading of anything is a claim; the second reading is the first honest number.*

### ☠ ★★★ And the reason is the mechanism

> ★★★ **GYPSUM WORKS BY DISSOLVING — it has to go into solution to deliver its calcium to the clay.** ☠ ★★★ **AND A THING THAT DISSOLVES GETS WASHED DOWNWARD BY EVERY RAIN THAT FALLS ON IT.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **SO THE PROPERTY THAT MAKES IT WORK IS THE PROPERTY THAT MAKES IT TEMPORARY.** *It is not being consumed by the soil. It is leaving.*

★★ **Which converts the trial from "does it work" to a question with a much better shape: HOW OFTEN.** ⚠ **This is a dose on a schedule, not an amendment I apply once** — and I would have found that out in Y12 by wondering why the beds went back to panning.

★ **Remaining reads stand: flowering, and weigh by block at harvest.**

## ☠ ★★★ The ark: I have been protecting the seed with the wrong thing

**Sat down to build `CAVE-3-DOOR-1` and got about as far as picking the timber before the whole plan came apart in my hands.**

> ☠ ★★★ **A WOODEN DOOR DOES NOT STOP A MOUSE. IT IS FOOD WITH A DELAY.** *And a mouse is the entire threat to a seed store — not weather, not damp, not collapse. Mice.*

★★ **So if the door were the thing protecting the seed, the ark would be failing on the day I finished it.** **It is not the thing protecting the seed. I had simply never asked which layer was doing which job.**

### ★★★ Three layers, three different jobs

| Layer | The job it ACTUALLY does | |
|---|---|---|
| **The cave** | ★★ **Temperature, and nothing else** | **Cool and steady all year.** *The one thing no container can provide, and the only real reason to walk out there at all* |
| **The jar** | ☠ ★★★ **Dry · airtight · rodent-proof** | ★★★ **This is the ark. All of it** |
| **The door** | **Weather, and animals bigger than the crawl** | ★ **A porch.** *Worth having. Not worth waiting for* |

> ☠ ★★★ **THE JARS AND THE DOOR HAVE BEEN SITTING ON THE SAME "CHEAP, ANY WET DAY" LIST AS IF THEY WERE TWO COMPARABLE CHORES — AND ONE OF THEM IS THE ENTIRE THING AND THE OTHER IS A CONVENIENCE.**
>
> ★★★ **AND THAT REVERSES THE ORDER OF WORK: THE SEED CAN GO OUT THERE BEFORE THE DOOR EXISTS.** *I have been holding the whole ark hostage to its least important layer for fifty-seven days.*

⚠ ★★ **The jar body has to be STONEWARE, fired to vitrification — not earthenware.** ☠ *A porous wall is not a seal no matter how good the lid is.* **Ground lip, pitch over wax.** ★ *Same thinking again: name the layer that does the job, then check that it can actually do it.*

## ☠ ★★★ Sealing is the LAST step, not the first

**Then I nearly made the real mistake, which was to seal a jar.**

> ☠ ★★★ **YOU CANNOT SEAL MOISTURE OUT OF A JAR. YOU SEAL IT IN.** *Seed packed damp and closed perfectly is damp seed, perfectly preserved, dying exactly on schedule — and a leaky jar in a dry cave would at least have gone on drying.*
>
> ★★★ **A SEAL DOES NOT CREATE A STATE. IT LOCKS IN WHATEVER STATE YOU SEALED.** ★★ *Which is the hurry-seals-the-surface rule from d3254 wearing completely different clothes: the outside is finished and the inside never was.*

### ★★ And I cannot air-dry below the air

**No amount of hanging it up gets seed drier than the room. It needs a desiccant shut in with it** — a small open dish, **not touching the seed**.

> ★★★ **AND THE DESICCANT IS CALCINED GYPSUM, WHICH I ALREADY HAVE AND ALREADY WROTE OFF.** ☠ ★★★ **"It stales in the store" and "it is a ferocious desiccant" are THE SAME SENTENCE READ FROM TWO DIRECTIONS.** *The fault is the function.*

### ☠ But the stock is spent, and that is the proof

⚠ **Weighed the ~2.1 kg of calcined gypsum. It has already taken up most of what it can hold.**

> ☠ ★★★ **IT HAS BEEN WORKING AS A DESICCANT THIS WHOLE TIME — ON THE OPEN AIR OF A STOREROOM, WHICH IS AN UNLIMITED SUPPLY, FOR NO BENEFIT WHATEVER.** *It did not go bad. It did its job, in the wrong place, at nothing.*
>
> ★★★ **SO: RE-BURN IT. Calcining IS the recharge — roasting drives the water straight back off.** ★★ **A desiccant is not consumed, it is FILLED, and it can be emptied again.**
>
> ☠ ★★★ **AND IT MUST GO FROM THE FIRE TO A SEALED JAR WITH AS LITTLE OPEN AIR IN BETWEEN AS I CAN MANAGE. Every minute it sits out is capacity spent on the room.**

★★ **Which is why "store the STONE, burn on demand" was already the right rule — and I had it filed as a plaster rule without ever knowing the reason.**

### ★★★ And the dish is also the instrument

> ★★★ **WEIGH THE DESICCANT IN, WEIGH IT OUT. WHAT IT GAINED IS WHAT THE SEED LOST.** ★★ **A desiccant is a moisture METER that happens to also do the drying** — *and when it stops gaining, the seed is at equilibrium and it is time to swap in a fresh charge and seal for good.*

★ *The jerky scale, the rope's turns per metre, the marked comb. Find the thing that is already countable and count it.*
