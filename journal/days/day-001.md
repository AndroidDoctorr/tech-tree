# Day 1

## Meta

- **Day:** 1
- **Season (approx.):** Late spring, early Holocene
- **Location:** Limestone terrace above the Orontes, oak scrub on the slope; Amuq plain and river visible below, gorge cutting east toward the interior
- **Weather:** Clear morning, hazy west (Mediterranean not far); cold night, heavy dew

## Status

- **Body:** Fine. Clothes are wrong for bush work — something modern, no boots. Feet already hot spots on gravel.
- **Hunger:** Low nagging. You could ignore it for days, but your mouth wants something chewy.
- **Sleep:** One long unconscious stretch before "today" — you don't know if that was respawn logic or ordinary sleep.
- **Injuries:** None
- **Inventory:** Handheld assistant (working), clothes on back, no tools, no bag, no food

## Events

You come awake on gravel that isn't road gravel. It's limestone chips, river-rounded pebbles, and snail shells. The air smells like wet mud somewhere below, crushed oak leaves, and a faint salt edge on the breeze — not the sea, but close enough to feel it.

The first minute is orientation, not heroics. You stand slowly, turn a full circle, and count what matters:

1. **Sun** — low east, morning. Below you, a green valley opens toward haze in the west. You hear water — not a trickle, something that runs.
2. **Noise** — finches, frogs from the direction of the sound, something rat-sized in the brush. No human voices anywhere. The silence is the loudest thing.
3. **Ground** — dry terrace above a wooded slope. Oak and pistachio on the hills; reed beds and darker green patches down where the land flattens into the plain.

You tell the assistant aloud where you think you are and when. It agrees: roughly 10,000 BC, Orontes valley, northwest Levant — where Antioch will sit one day, on the river's left bank where it leaves the gorge. Post–Younger Dryas warming; the Amuq plain is wetter than the high desert eastward. Early Neolithic people will settle this valley within a few centuries, but you are alone in this scenario — no smoke on the horizon, no tells, no flint scatter from camps.

You walk downhill toward the sound, not rushing. Twice you freeze at movement in the scrub (rock partridge, then a fox). The Orontes appears through the trees: brown-green, actually moving, shin-deep in the shallows near the bank. You kneel and drink from a side eddy. It tastes of mud and minerals and life. Better than a seep. You mark the place in memory and on a flat stone with three scratches — your first map.

Afternoon is spent in a tight radius of that bank. You are not building a kiln today. You are not smelting. You are doing the boring ancestor work that makes every later day possible:

- You choose a **sleep site**: dry terrace above flood line, limestone shelf with a shallow overhang, view of the approach and the river bend below. No dead oak poised to fall. You drag one rotten branch away and clear stones that would dig into your back. *This is your respawn point.* You take it seriously — the plain floods; you checked the high-water stain on trunks and stayed above it.
- You gather **material intelligence**: chert cobbles in the gravel bar (grey-brown, glassy when struck); thick reeds in the muddy margin; willow switches; oak branches dead-dry enough to snap; inner bark on a fallen limb that might twist into cordage if you soak and beat it.
- You try to knock a **flake** off a chert cobble with another rock. Three hits produce powder; the fourth spalls a sharp edge the size of your thumb. You cut your finger admiring it. You now have a cutting edge and respect for the edge.
- You do **not** get fire. You know bow drill in theory, but you lack cordage that won't snap, a hearth board pre-drilled, and a spindle — and your hands are soft. You note dry hearth wood candidates on the terrace and save fire for when you have string and patience.

Before the light goes, you eat nothing processed — a handful of grass seeds you can't really mill yet, sour berries from a bush you're only half sure about. You watch a fish shadow in the shallows and have no way to take it. Hunger eases but doesn't vanish. You pee downstream like you're supposed to. You lie on the ledge and watch stars you recognize from the wrong era, with the river muttering below.

## Assistant

**You:** What's actually first — fire, tools, or food?

**Assistant:** In your rules, **sleep site first** (respawn), **water second**, **information third** (what grows, what's sharp, what's dangerous). Fire and formal tools are day-two problems once you have cordage and a safe place to fail. Food can wait longer for you than for a historical human, but your hands still need calories for fine motor work — don't skip eating entirely out of laziness. You found a river — that's a strong start; fish and reeds will matter soon.

**You:** Am I crazy to think charcoal → kiln → smelt → electromagnets?

**Assistant:** Directionally sound. Charcoal and kiln are Phase 1–2 enablers. Smelting needs ore *and* sustained heat *and* crucibles — you'll feel the gap between "I know how" and "I can make it repeat." Electromagnets are surprisingly reachable once you have copper wire and iron; a battery is harder than the magnet. The real cliffs are precision industry (wire gauge, insulation) and anything semiconductor. This valley has copper deposits in the broader region, but not at your feet — you'll travel eventually.
