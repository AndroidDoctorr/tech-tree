## One paragraph

The nineteenth hundred days **close the shop window, then spend the summer giving the valley a second mill and a hut at the tin gate**: you **poured and hung Atelier south glass (×20 · BC-1 complete)**, **moved the loom and easel in**, **swapped both oil lanterns to iron**, **blast-capped the home road**, and **stood KOZAN-HUT** (**wythe · bond beam · TR roof · door · tin deferred**). A **14-day Kozan lap** **smelted in the field again (~191 g · Sn bank ~668 g)** and **left the ore pile live**. Then the century turns east: **Koruhöyük pozzolan**, a **slab that strips**, **Kiln A lime**, **SLUICE-2 finished**, **WW-POWER Phase 1–3** (**gate · ~16 L/s · wheel · belt · grind takeoff · trip-hammer belt · crank drill**). Day 1900 is **not a ribbon**. It is a **full flax day** — **wild pull lap 3**, **FLAX-PATCH-1 ghost-staked**, **TRIB-FT-01 footings curing**. You are **not** on a finished farm-fork bridge. You **are** on **daylight in the Atelier**, a **hut at Kozan**, **pozzolan you can pour**, and a **second wheel that clangs** — the first century where **power left the first mill and the road grew a room**.

## On playing one day at a time

Centadial 19 is the century of **not smelting the day you hang a door**: Kozan got a room, then tin waited for a 14-day lap. Day 1832 (**hut close**) and Day 1894 (**Phase 3 — the second mill actually takes off**) are the two character beats — **a bed at the gate**, then **power that is not WW-1**. Day 1900 is quieter and more like a Tuesday: **thin wild flax**, **a 10 m² ghost**, **footings you must not touch**. The fun at Day 1900 is that **nineteen hundred landed on fiber while concrete cured in the ditch**. **Day 1901 is another thin lap, or hands off the pour — both legal.**
