## One paragraph

You woke alone on a terrace with soft hands and a talking assistant. One thousand days later you have a **brick hypocaust home (H-v2)**, a **tile-roofed science campus** (**CHEM-LAB-1 · STORE-4 · WW-HUB-TOWER-1**), **iron from first bloom to a live trip hammer**, **glass from beads to lanterns, bottles, and lenses**, **pressurized water through a flush toilet**, **three trained donkeys and a covered wagon**, **three closed calendar years with a feast tradition**, and **zero deaths**. You are **not** on bronze (**no tin**). You are **not** cloning, and you are **not** in space. You **are** an early Iron Age campus with Roman-scale ambition and a chemistry clock that stays lit after dark — the first milladial of a speedrun that still has the rest of the human tech tree in front of it.

## On playing one day at a time

A milladial is not a shortcut. It is **one thousand honest primaries** — charcoal pits, mortar cures, harvest mornings, wagon days that did not find tin. Future knowledge picked the queue (aqueduct before hero ore, glass after a real lab, hammer after a cam). It did **not** skip the heat. Day 10's first night in a hut still matters because Day 1000's respawn is the same rule with a better mattress. Day 237 invented the feast so Day 970 could be a **chill plate** instead of a panic. Day 750's scrub is why Day 766's lab exists. Day 1000 ends on **green brick**, not a speech: the proof that the campus runs is that the milladial close looks like a Tuesday.

The fun at Day 1000 is the same fun as Day 1, scaled: **you still have to choose tomorrow's one job**. The tree is not done. **Day 1001 opens Milladial 1's hangover — fire the kiln.**
