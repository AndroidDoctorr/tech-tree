# Building Code 2 *(BC-2)*

**Filed:** Day 3275 · **Cal-Y10 D130** · supersedes [BC-1](../archive/building-code-1.md) for all new work
**Scope:** every enclosed building at HOME campus and future stakes
**Procedure:** [construction.md](../procedures/construction.md) — BC-2 says *what*, construction says *in what order*. They change together.

---

## What changed, and why

BC-1 was written at **d1759**, when the materials were brick, lime mortar, oak and tile. Since then: **pozzolanic concrete** is proven and routine, **iron bar** is available, **gypsum** is an unconstrained bed at `M-21`, and **`GEN-WW-1`** puts current on the campus for the first time.

A code written before concrete cannot tell you where to put a column, and a code written before electricity cannot tell you to leave a hole in the wall.

> ★★ **BC-1 governed a building as a shelter. BC-2 governs it as a shelter with systems running through it.**

**Retired outright — do not build, do not spec, do not mention:** wattle-and-daub · thatch · reed-and-pitch roofing on anything habitable · unreinforced single-wythe walls · bare earth floors · unglazed window openings as an acceptable end state.

---

## ★★★ The governing principle — install the SPACE, not the system

**Almost nothing in this code asks for a pipe, a wire or a duct to be installed. It asks for the VOID they will one day occupy.**

A chase costs a morning while the wall is open and is effectively unbuyable once the wall is closed, plastered and painted. This is the same rule already written into [BC-3 water](#water) about drain mains, and it generalises:

> ☠ ★★★ **CAPACITY AND ROUTE CANNOT BE RETROFITTED. Every service in this code is cheap at the moment the wall is open and enormous at every moment after.**

| | |
|---|---|
| ★★ **Leave the route** | Chase, core, sleeve, sleeper void — **required** |
| ★ **Leave the pull** | ⚠ **A flax draw-cord in every empty conduit.** The single highest-value item in this document per unit of effort. An empty buried chase with no cord in it is very nearly a solid wall |
| ◌ **Leave the system** | **Only where the run would be impossible later.** Copper is scarce and the generator is intermittent — *do not spend wire on a load that does not exist yet* |

★ **This is the deliberate pull-back on electricity and air.** Plan generously, install almost nothing.

## BC-2-BLOCK — cast block *(new standard)*

**Prerequisites, all live:** quicklime ✓ · pozzolan ✓ · sand and grit ✓ · iron bar ✓ · timber and strap for a mould ✓.

★★ **Block is castable stone, and that resolves a standing conflict in this code.** [BC-3](#ground-level-elements) requires stone at grade and forbids fired brick there, because brick wicks — but dressed stone is slow. A cast block is a stone-class unit produced at brick-class speed.

### Where block is standard

| Use | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Footings · stem walls · everything at or below grade** | ✓ **Required** — supersedes dressed stone as the default |
| **Long load-bearing runs · retaining · large spans** | ✓ **Preferred** |
| **Finished, visible, small or detailed wings** | **Brick.** Block is not prettier and the press pad is already there |
| **Existing buildings** | ✗ **Nothing.** See [retrofit](#retrofit-policy) |

### The unit

**Two-core hollow, nominal 400 × 200 × 200, actual 390 × 190 × 190 with a 10 mm joint.** Half block at 190. A hollow block this size lands near **~15 kg** — a two-hand lift, and honestly the ceiling for working alone all day. ⚠ **Solid at this size is over 30 kg. Do not cast solid units.**

> ⚠ **Measure the brick before cutting the mould.** Pick a block length that is a whole multiple of *brick + joint*, so block and brick bond together where they meet instead of fighting for the rest of the building's life. **The mould defines the module, and the mould is cut once.**

### ★★ The four jobs a hollow core does

This is why hollow, and not simply a smaller solid unit:

1. **Halves the weight** — the difference between a liftable unit and a two-man unit
2. ★★ **The cores stack into a continuous vertical chase.** *This is the conduit, and it is free* — see [BC-2-SERVICES](#bc-2-services--chases-sleeves-and-ducts)
3. ★★ **Selected cores take rebar and grout and become the tie-column, cast inside the wall** — see [seismic](#bc-2-seismic--lateral-resistance)
4. **Dead air** — a modest but real insulation gain

> ☠ ★★ **NEVER run a service through a core you intend to grout, and never grout a core you have run a service through.** Choose each core's job at setting-out, mark it on the plan, and mark it on the wall. *A wire cast into a structural column is lost, and a column cast around a wire is not a column.*

### Making them

**Dry-tamped, not wet-cast.** Mix to a damp-earth consistency — squeezes into a ball that holds its shape without glistening — ram it into a tapered stripping mould in three lifts, and **demould immediately**.

★ **This is the whole trick, and it is a scheduling decision rather than a quality one.** A wet-cast block sits in its mould overnight, so a hundred blocks needs a hundred moulds. A dry-tamped block leaves the mould in seconds, so **one good iron-strapped mould makes blocks all day.** Taper every face and every core box or it will not strip.

### ☠ Curing — the constraint that decides the schedule

> ☠ ★★★ **CEMENT CURES, IT DOES NOT DRY.** Already written into [BC-2-RENDER](#bc-2-render--render-plaster-scrim) as *SET ≠ DRY*; it matters ten times more here. **A block left in the sun to "dry" is a block that never gained strength.** Keep them shaded and damp — sprinkled, covered, out of the wind.

⚠ **Lime-pozzolan is slow.** It is not the modern binder and it does not behave like one.

| Age | Status |
|---|---|
| **~7 days** | Handle gently. Stack, do not drop |
| **~28 days** | Non-structural use |
| ★ **~90 days** | **Load-bearing. Do not go early** |

★★ **Block is a crop, not a material — you cast it a season before you need it.** Exactly the charcoal bank, the seasoning timber and the precision plates: *the queue is the plan.* A build that starts with block-making has already lost its first season, so **the mould runs in the background between heroes and the yard is never empty.**

⚠ **Green block shrinks.** Laying it early puts the shrinkage into the wall as cracks.

### Quality control

★ **Cast three spare blocks from every batch and break one at 7, 28 and 90 days.** Costs three blocks; tells you whether the mix, the pozzolan and the ramming are right *before* a wall is standing on them. Log the break in the day file.

**Mix, as a starting point to be trialled and then fixed:** roughly **1 slaked lime : 2 pozzolan : 5 sand-and-grit**. ⚠ Batch by the measuring box, never by eye — [BC-2-PRECISION](#bc-2-precision--setting-out).

### ⚠ Aggregate — the "cinder" question

Real cinder block used coal ash, which is not what is on this campus.

| Aggregate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Crushed stone · grit · sharp sand** | ✓ **The structural standard.** Use this |
| **Bloomery slag, crushed** | **Acceptable as partial aggregate** in non-load-bearing units. Historically ordinary |
| ☠ **Copper slag** | ✗ **No.** It is 3.8 kg of re-charge stock that still holds metal — it is feedstock, not gravel |
| ⚠ **Char fines · unburnt ash** | ✗ **No.** Unburnt carbon makes a weak, punky block |

### Laying

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ☠ ★★ **Lay block DRY** | **The opposite of brick.** *Wet a brick or it drinks the mortar; wet a block and it swells, then shrinks in the wall and cracks the joint.* **Same goal, opposite rule — this one is worth a note on the mould itself** |
| **Bed** | **Face-shell bedding** — mortar the shells, not the webs, except at a grouted core |
| **Bond** | **Running bond.** ⚠ Stack bond has no interlock and is weak |
| **Grouted cells** | Poured continuously in lifts, rodded to consolidate, with the rebar lapped and wired |

## Structure

### Foundation

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Strip footing** | Reinforced concrete, width ~2–3× the wall it carries, bearing on firm subgrade — **not topsoil, not fill** |
| **Stem wall / socle** | Cast block to [BC-2-BLOCK](#bc-2-block--cast-block-new-standard) · carries the DPC |
| **Subgrade** | **Dampen before the pour.** Dry ground steals the mix water |
| **Forms** | Timber, **wetted or tallow-rubbed** before the pour or they stick and tear the face |
| **Underfloor** | Gravel and lime bed, tied into the site drain — ⚠ **never a blind basin** |

### ☠ ★★ Cold joints — the one-man constraint

> ☠ ★★★ **YOU CANNOT POUR MORE THAN YOU CAN MIX AND PLACE IN ONE SESSION, AND THE FOUNDATION DOES NOT CARE THAT YOU WERE TIRED.** Stop mid-pour and come back tomorrow and you have a **cold joint** — a plane of weakness straight through the footing, invisible and permanent.

**Therefore: design every pour in segments you can honestly finish, and make the stopping points deliberate.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Shear key** | A greased timber laid in the top of the pour, lifted when green — leaves a groove the next pour keys into |
| **Dowels** | Rebar left standing proud across every planned joint |
| ★ **Plan the joint** | **A planned construction joint is sound. An accidental cold joint is a crack you built on purpose** |

### Rebar

Carrying forward from BC-1 and extended.

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Ends** | ★ **Hook them.** Plain round bar bonds poorly · twisted square bar bonds far better |
| **Cover** | **25–40 mm of concrete over every bar.** Lime-pozzolan is alkaline and passivates iron — but only where it actually covers it. Rust swells, and swelling spalls the cover off |
| ⚠ **Chairs** | **Hold the bar up off the ground on stones or fired chairs.** *Bar laid on the dirt has zero cover on its underside and will rust out from below* — the most common way a good pour fails |
| **Laps** | Where two bars join, overlap generously — on the order of **40× the bar diameter** — and wire the lap |
| **Columns to walls** | ✓ **Rebar ties the tie-columns into the wall** — dowels bedded into the bed joints as the wall goes up |

## BC-2-SEISMIC — lateral resistance

> ☠ **A damaging quake here runs about once in 125 years. I do not die.** *"Sound for a few decades" is the wrong frame — in a millennium this campus goes down eight times.* Mass carries weight beautifully and has no answer at all to sideways shaking.

**Two systems are permitted. They are equivalent in intent and must not be mixed carelessly within one wall.**

### Brick — confined masonry

★★ **Build the brick panel first, leave the brick ends toothed, cast the column against it second** so the concrete keys into the teeth. ☠ *Cast the column first and butt brick to it and you have a post standing next to a wall — same materials, none of the benefit.*

### Block — reinforced hollow-unit

★★ **The column is cast inside the wall.** Lay the block, stand the rebar in the chosen cores, grout them solid. There is no toothing because there is no interface — **the wall and the column are the same object.** This is the cleaner system and it is the reason block is now the standard for long structural runs.

### Both systems

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Columns** | Every corner, both sides of every opening, and at intervals along long walls |
| **Ring beam** | Continuous reinforced band at the head, tied into every column — ★ **a closed RING, not four lintels** |
| **Plinth beam** | Tie the column bases together at socle level, or the feet splay |
| **Openings** | ⚠ No window or door within about **half a wall-height of a corner** — corners are where shear failure starts. Generous lintel bearing past each jamb |
| **Panel length** | Limit unbraced run — column or cross-wall at intervals |
| **Roof** | ☠ **Tie the roof frame down to the ring beam.** An untied roof leaves. Tile is heavy and mass up high is what you least want — keep it, but brace and tie it properly |

## Envelope

### BC-2-DAMP — damp-proof course

> ☠ **HARD GATE: no interior plaster on any wall without a DPC below it.** Gypsum over a wall that wicks groundwater will blister at the base and — because gypsum is slightly soluble — **dissolve out of the wall.**

**Continuous barrier course in the socle:** a pitch bed, or a course of dense tile bedded in pitch. Blocks capillary rise. Required before any render on any wall.

### Walls

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Structural** | Cast block, or brick with confining columns — [BC-2-SEISMIC](#bc-2-seismic--lateral-resistance) |
| **At grade** | ☠ **Block or stone, never fired brick.** Brick wicks; anything standing permanently in wet soil spalls |
| **Chases** | ⚠ **Cut, cast or cored before the wall is closed** — [BC-2-SERVICES](#bc-2-services--chases-sleeves-and-ducts) |

### BC-2-RENDER — render, plaster, scrim

★ **Plastered walls are now the standard finish, not an upgrade.** Limewash alone is no longer sufficient for a habitable interior.

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ⚠ **Never plaster straight onto masonry** | The finish follows every irregularity, keys poorly, and suction differs between unit and joint, which sets it patchy |
| **Three coats** | **Scratch** — rough lime-sand pressed hard into the joints and scratched while green · **float** — the coat that makes the wall flat, ruled off against grounds · **finish** — thin, fine, gypsum inside |
| **Grounds** | ★ Set timber screeds to thickness and rule the float coat against them. *This is how a wall gets flat — not by eye* |
| **Fibre in the mix** | Chopped flax tow through the base coat — that is what haired plaster is |
| **Scrim on joints, not walls** | ⚠ Cloth over a whole solid wall is waste and can debond. Scrim belongs **where two materials meet** — above all over the panel-to-column junction, **which is exactly where the crack will be** — plus over lath, in corners, and ★ **over every chase you just filled** |
| **Gypsum is interior only** | ☠ Not waterproof, and it re-wets. **Exterior render is lime. No exceptions** |
| **Substrate** | **Damp the wall first** or it drinks the plaster's water and you get chalk · **set is not dry** · never re-temper |
| ⚠ **Sequence** | **Plaster comes after the roof is on, after every chase is cut, and after every sleeve is in.** Plastering early means breaking it open again |

## BC-2-SERVICES — chases, sleeves and ducts

★ **The new section, and the reason for the revision.** Nothing here requires a working system. All of it requires a decision made before the wall closes.

### General

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ☠ ★★ **Separate the trades** | **Water, wire and air get their own routes.** Where a crossing is unavoidable, **water passes BELOW wire** — a leak should not find a conductor |
| ★★ **Draw-cord** | **A flax cord left in every empty conduit, tied off and logged.** *Trivial now, and without it the chase may as well be solid* |
| **Access** | A niche or box at every end and every change of direction |
| **Record it** | ⚠ **Mark every buried route on the building's map entry before it is covered.** A chase nobody can find is a chase nobody has |

### Electrical — route only

**Live capability:** `GEN-WW-1` on the `WW-2` collar, driving `CU-CELL-1` since d3258. Low-voltage, direct, and **intermittent with the wheel**. There is no building load today.

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ◌ **Wire** | **Do not install.** Copper is the scarcest useful metal and every metre in a wall is a metre not in a cell |
| ✓ **Conduit** | **Required.** Block cores where they align; fired clay tube through masonry |
| ⚠ **Not bare iron** | Iron conduit in damp masonry corrodes. **Clay tube** |
| ☠ **Never cast wire into concrete** | Wire lives in conduit so it can be drawn out and replaced. **A wire in a pour is a wire you own once** |
| ★★ **Sweeps, not corners** | **No sharp right angles, and no more than about two of them between access points.** *You are not routing current, you are routing the act of pulling a cord through a hole you cannot see* |
| **Boxes** | Set a niche where the load will plausibly be: over each bench, beside each door, centre of the ceiling |
| **Earth** | A buried rod and a tail left accessible at the panel niche. Cheap now |

### Plumbing — sleeves now, pipe later

> ★★ **THE DELIVERABLE IS THE SLEEVE, NOT THE PIPE.** A short length of oversized fired clay pipe cast through the wall or footing at every plausible penetration. **Any future pipe threads through it with no demolition at all** — which is precisely the "minimal remodelling" test.

⚠ **On copper pipe — pulling back.** It is makeable: sheet rolled on a mandrel with a brazed lap seam, which is how it was actually done long before seamless tube. But **seamless is gated on the boring mill and the lathe upgrade, which `now.md` gates on the seasoning plates at Y11**, and more importantly **copper is the conductor.** Kilograms of it in a drain line is kilograms not in wire.

| Service | Material |
|---|---|
| ★ **Drain · waste · vent** | **Glazed stoneware, socket-and-spigot.** Clay and kilns are unconstrained — this is the right answer and the historical one |
| **Gravity supply** | Stoneware |
| **Pressure · hot · tight bends** | **Copper, sparingly**, and only where nothing else serves |
| ☠ **Lead** | ✗ **Never on potable.** Obvious, but it is the easy historical mistake |

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ⚠ **Fall** | **About 1:40 to 1:80** — consistent with the existing 1–2% site drain rule. ☠ **Too steep is as bad as too flat: the water outruns the solids and the pipe blocks** |
| **Trap** | A water seal at every fixture, or the drain vents sewer gas into the room |
| ★ **Vent** | ⚠ **Every trap needs a vent upstream or a discharge siphons it dry and the seal is gone.** *This is the entire reason a vent stack exists, and it is the part everyone omits* |
| **Rodding** | An access eye at every bend. Something will block |

### Ductwork and air

**Achievable now, not merely conceivable:** a **wetted mat with a belt-driven fan behind it** is evaporative cooling, and in a dry Syrian summer that is a real and substantial drop. `LAB-VENT-FAN-1` and the evaporation trough at `COOL-CELLAR-1` are most of the parts already.

**Also passive and available:** a duct drawing air from the cool cellar or a buried run delivers it at soil temperature, with no power at all.

☠ **Actual refrigeration is long-horizon** — it needs a compressor and a working fluid. Not a BC-2 item.

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ☠ ★★★ **SIZE THE DUCT FOR THE BUILDING YOU WILL HAVE** | **Identical to the drain-main rule, and violated far more often because air is invisible.** ⚠ **A duct needs vastly more cross-section than intuition suggests — measure a duct in hand-spans, not finger-widths.** A buried duct cannot be enlarged |
| **Route** | A chase from the cellar or north cheek to the occupied space, with the fall and a drain for condensate |
| **Intake** | Shaded, north, low, and away from the forge and every fume source |
| **Hypocaust** | The existing heating duct precedent. ★ **Where a wing may want both, run one generous chase and decide later** |

## WOODCRAFT-FINISH-1 — exposed wood

★ **Extended at d3275 from floors and furniture to all exposed wood.** If it can be touched or seen, it is sanded and treated.

| Scope | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Any exposed wood** | ✓ **Planed, sanded smooth, and treated.** Floors, furniture, frames, rails, sills, doors, shelves, handles, posts |
| **Exempt** | Hidden joists and rough structural timbers · char · trail stakes · green field splits |

**Order:** cut → plane → sand → assemble → treat.

| Treatment | Where |
|---|---|
| **Tallow · beeswax** | Interior, in hand now |
| ★ **Linseed oil** | **The proper finish — harder than tallow and it penetrates.** ⚠ Gated on the oil press, which is the **Oil** goal in [short-term-goals.md](../../plans/short-term-goals.md). *Raw flax oil never really dries; it must be heat-treated* |
| **Rosin-in-turpentine varnish** | Cold brushable, for fine work |
| **Pitch** | Exterior, renewed |
| ★ **Char the buried end** | **Any post going into the ground.** Free, and it is the actual answer to ground contact |

## BC-3 — site and layout

**BC-1 and BC-2 govern the structure. BC-3 governs the ground it stands on and the arrangement of the whole campus — and it buys things a structural code cannot.**

### Water

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ★★★ **A "flat" surface must never actually be flat** | **Every bench, slab, yard and apron gets a deliberate cross-fall — about a finger in five paces — toward somewhere CHOSEN.** *Level is for instruments* |
| ☠ ★★★ **Size drain mains for the final paved area, not the current one** | **Hard surface is always added at the top; the water arrives at the bottom, where the drain is already built, buried and the wrong size.** ★★ **Capacity cannot be retrofitted into a network** |
| ☠ ★★ **A terrace is a trough with a wall at the uphill end** | **Every cut face gets a back-drain at its foot, and the bench must fall to it** *(three of mine did not, for years — d3271)* |
| ★★ **Keep part of the site permeable on purpose** | **Every square metre unpaved is one the drains never carry.** ★ *Beds and gravel are drainage infrastructure* |
| **Spring test** | Mandatory pour test each spring — two buckets at the lowest cheek and the entry pad · clear in under about two minutes · no sill pond |

### Layout for the earthquake

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ★★★ **Redundancy of access** | **After a shake the question is not what fell, it is what can still be reached.** Two routes to water, stores and gate · ⚠ never the only path running between two tall things |
| ☠ ★★★ **Never concentrate the irreplaceable** | **Seed ark, museum, records and master tools in separate buildings.** *Free at planning time; unbuyable afterwards* |
| ★★ **Nothing heavy uphill of anything essential** | *A wall falls onto whatever is below it* |
| ★★ **Keep an open muster space** | Nothing tall around it — for working and rebuilding |
| ★ **Rebuild routes, not only structures** | The cart has to reach the rubble |

### Ground-level elements

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| ☠ **Stone or block at grade, never fired brick** | **Brick wicks; anything standing permanently in wet soil spalls.** ★ **Brick lives above a damp course** |
| ★★★ **Non-structural elements should be allowed to move** | **Dry-lay bed borders, edgings and low garden walls.** *A mortared one cracks in frost or a shake and is then broken; a dry-laid one shuffles and is still a border* · ★★ **the opposite of the answer for a wall — same reasoning, different job** |
| ★ **Lay a single course level and to a line anyway** | So it can become the first course of something taller |

> ★★★ **COMMITTING: STAKE IT, LIVE IN IT A SEASON, THEN LAY STONE.** *A layout teaches by being WALKED what it cannot teach by being DRAWN — desire lines, standing water, where the shade falls at which hour.*

### Wet work and counters

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Counter tops** | Refractory tile or dressed stone · smooth, flat, neat lime-sand grout |
| **Sinks and slops** | Ceramic or metal — ✗ not wood |
| **Splash** | Tiled and grouted behind every sink, tied to the counter pitch and drain |
| **Wood in wet zones** | Frame, legs and brackets only — [furniture-code-1.md](furniture-code-1.md) |

## BC-2-PRECISION — setting out

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Water level** | ★ **Two vessels and a tube — dead accurate over any distance**, and better than any straightedge for setting a foundation |
| **Master straightedge · square · plumb** | Kept, checked, **not borrowed** |
| ★ **Square by the flip test** | **Scribe against it, flip it, scribe again. If the lines diverge the square is out by half the divergence.** *A square that checks itself — no external standard needed* |
| **Mortar and concrete by measuring box** | ⚠ **Batch by volume in a box, never by eye.** Consistent mortar is most of consistent masonry |

## Retrofit policy

> ★ **The existing buildings are sound, recent and not due for rebuilding. BC-2 is a code for new work.** *Nothing below is urgent and nothing below justifies opening a good wall.*

| Priority | Work | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Ring beam** — continuous reinforced band at wall head | ★ **The highest-value single intervention on unreinforced masonry, by a long way** |
| **2** | **Corner ties** — external buttress-columns, tied through the wall | Not as good as cast-against-toothed-brick; far better than nothing |
| **3** | Plinth tie at socle where accessible | |
| **4** | Render and plaster to BC-2-RENDER | ⚠ DPC first, always |
| ★ **Opportunistic** | **Chase and sleeve whenever a wall is open anyway** | ★★ **Never open a wall for services. Always add services to an open wall** |

**New builds: full BC-2 from the first course.**
