## ★ Why top bars at all

> **A skep is FIXED COMB. It cannot be harvested without destroying the comb — traditionally the colony with it.** Three years of scavenging cappings is why the wax bank has always been tiny.
>
> **TOP BARS: one comb per loose bar. Lift it, cut it off, put the bar back.**

### Build rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Bee space ~8 mm** | Wider and they build comb in it; narrower and they glue it with propolis |
| **Bars **~33 mm** centre to centre** | One comb per bar |
| **Bevelled, waxed guide underneath** | Without it they build diagonally |
| **SLOPE THE WALLS INWARD** | **Or the comb welds to them and the whole principle is lost** |
| **~40 L volume** | What scouts actually choose |
| **Follower board** | **A swarm cannot heat a big empty box.** Shrink it to them, open it as they grow |
| **Keep the jig** | Cut a spare bar set every time it is set up |

## ★★ DRIVING — how you empty a skep without killing it

> **The **only non-destructive way out of a basket.

1. Invert the skep in a tub
2. **Fit an **empty box mouth to mouth, cloth-tie the joint
3. **Drum steadily on the LOWER basket's sides with both hands, 15–20 minutes**
4. **They MARCH UP. The queen goes with them.** *The roar changes note when they start*
5. Watch the joint for her

**After:** leave the emptied skep beside the new hive, entrance open, **until the brood has hatched out — ~3–4 weeks — THEN render the comb.**

**One skep at a time. **Prove it on the weaker colony first. *A heavy colony takes a longer drum — ~25 min vs 15–20. There are simply more of them.*

### ★★ FURNISH THE BOX BEFORE THEY ARRIVE *(learned the hard way, d3243 → applied d3253)*

> **A driven colony carries NO WAX BUDGET.** It must build its entire house out of its own body at **8 honey : 1 wax** — which is why HIVE-5 took a **fortnight** to lay.
>
> **Never make a colony do that again. Into the box BEFORE the first drum stroke:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **×1 DRAWN comb** | **Somewhere she can lay tonight. No wax bill at all** |
| **×1 comb of sealed / emerging brood** | Workforce in days |
| **Thin honey-water, from the FIRST HOUR** | **Not "if they look slow"** |

## ★★ CUT-OUT — how you convert a fixed-comb BOX

> **A DRIVE ABANDONS THE BROOD. A CUT-OUT KEEPS IT.** That is the whole difference, and it is why a driven colony spends a fortnight with no workforce and no wax budget. **More invasive on the day, far cheaper over the fortnight.**

1. **Warm, still day** — cold comb is brittle and cracks
2. **Cut each comb out at the top, **whole
3. ★ **Lay it FLAT on a board. Slide a board under as it comes free. NEVER hold a comb by its edge, and never let any part hang unsupported — at ANY temperature** *(**warm comb SAGS, cold comb CRACKS** — two different failures, and I prepared for the wrong one)*
4. Trim to fit under the bar
5. **Tie in with **soft linen cradle loops under and over the bar
6. ★ **RIGHT WAY UP AND RIGHT WAY ROUND** — *cells are built with a slight upward tilt; invert a comb and the cells are wrong and the honey runs out*
7. **Restore the nest order: BROOD in the middle, HONEY at the edges**
8. **Cut the ties out in ~a week** or the bees propolise the linen in

> ⚠ **THE RISK IS THE QUEEN, NOT THE COMB.** Rolling or crushing her against a comb you are cutting is what wrecks the operation. **Read every face before the knife goes in; set her comb aside first.**

## ★★ BROOD TRANSFER — rescue and boost

> **This is what movable comb is really for. It is INSURANCE, not just harvest.** A queenless colony is rescued by giving it **a comb of EGGS** from a strong one — they raise their own queen from it. **You cannot lift a comb out of a skep or a fixed box, so a colony with neither is ONE QUEEN DEEP with no way out.**

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **To RESCUE: move EGGS** | **They need something to raise a queen **from |
| **To BOOST: move SEALED / EMERGING brood** | **Bees emerging in days are accepted by ANYBODY** and are workforce immediately |
| **Shake most adhering adults off** | **Strange ADULTS get fought; brood does not** |
| **Set it right against the cluster** | |
| ⚠ **Never give a small colony more comb than it can keep warm** | **ONE comb, not three. A chilled brood comb is worse than none** |

### ★★ THE TEST COMB — a diagnostic before it is a remedy *(d3262)*

> ☠ **Giving eggs to a colony that STILL HOLDS a virgin queen is not neutral. They will not raise a rival while they have a queen, and she may destroy any cell they start.** A date-based trigger *("no eggs by day X → give eggs")* **is too blunt — it cannot tell a LATE queen from a LOST one.**

**So do not guess. Give the comb and watch what they do with it:**

| What they do with it | What it means |
|---|---|
| ★ **Build queen cells on it** | **QUEENLESS — and the cure is already in the hive** |
| **Ignore it, raise them as workers** | **A QUEEN IS PRESENT. Late, not lost. Leave her alone** |

**One move that asks the question, answers it, and fixes it.**

## ★★ Reading a colony WITHOUT opening it

> **Opening a hive costs it a day of heat and temper. **A quarter hour at the doorstep costs nothing.

| Sign at the entrance | Means |
|---|---|
| ★★ **POLLEN GOING IN** | **Pollen is the PROTEIN fed to LARVAE — not adult food.** So loads coming in mean **brood to feed**, which means **A QUEEN IS LAYING.** A queenright confirmation from ten feet away with the lid on |
| **Nectar only, no pollen** | **No brood yet. **Not alarming in a new colony — she has nowhere to lay |
| **Arcing hovers facing the door** | Orientation flights — young bees emerging |
| ⚠ **Fighting · wobbly darting fliers · bees checking cracks** | **ROBBING. Reduce the entrance NOW** |
| **Undertakers carrying out dead** | **Normal in spring; **excessive is a problem |
| **Tails up, wings fanning at the door** | Scenting — calling fliers home |
| **Drones appearing** | **Colony feels prosperous — **swarm season is on |

### ★★ HEFT — and better, a SCALE *(d3263)*

**Lift the back edge of the box. You do not open a hive to ask whether it has food.**

> ★★ **Better still: PUT A HIVE ON A BALANCE.** Weight is **a number, available every day, and it costs the colony nothing** — no smoke, no lid, no lost heat. **And it is a CURVE, not a number:**

| Weight | Means |
|---|---|
| ★ **Climbing** | **The flow is ON — and the start date is a fact about the VALLEY, not the hive** |

### ★★ QUEEN-PATTERN-TEST — one comb, two times

**A scattered brood pattern has three causes that look identical on the day:**

| Cause | Why it scatters |
|---|---|
| ★ **A NEW queen** | Three weeks in she has not settled. Early combs are ragged and tighten |
| ★ **Comb not READY** | She will not lay in a cell the workers have not polished, and **a small colony cannot keep ahead of her** — the gaps are their backlog, not her failure |
| ☠ **DIPLOID DRONE** | The queen is fine and the eggs are fine and they still die |

> ★★ **They cannot be told apart by looking harder. They separate perfectly over nine days, because they differ in WHAT HAPPENS TO THE EGGS rather than in where they are.**
>
> ★★ **MARK ONE COMB. MAP IT. RE-MAP THE SAME COMB AT SEALING.** ★ **The signal is the DIFFERENCE between what was LAID and what SURVIVED.**

| Same comb by sealing | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Gaps fill in, slab comes up solid** | ★ **Workers' backlog. Fine** |
| **Later combs tighten** | ★ **She was just new. Fine** |
| ☠ **Laid solid, seals with an even scatter of holes** | ☠ **DIPLOID DRONE. The mating was too close** |

★ **A scatter at the EGG stage argues AGAINST the bad case** — *diploid drone brood is laid solid and disappears later.*

## ★★ SWARM MANAGEMENT *(d3257)* — steering it, because it cannot be stopped

> ★★ **SWARMING IS REPRODUCTION AT THE LEVEL OF THE COLONY.** The colony is the organism; the swarm is its **offspring.** It is not a disease and not a failure. **The goal is not to prevent it — it is to make sure it happens into a box I own instead of a tree I will never find.**

### The two triggers *(both are density; both are answered with SPACE)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **CONGESTION** | **Nest **backfilled with nectar, no cells left to lay in |
| **QUEEN-SUBSTANCE DILUTION** | **Her scent passes bee to bee; in a crowd the edges stop receiving enough — **that shortfall IS the signal of "too much colony per queen" |

### Reading the cells — ☠ COUNT AND POSITION BEFORE THE KNIFE

| Type | Where | How many | Means | If destroyed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SWARM** | **BOTTOM edges** | **Many** | **Dividing | **Delayed, badly |
| **SUPERSEDURE** | **FACE of the comb** | **One or two** | **Replacing a failing queen | ☠ **COLONY KILLED — its only plan removed |
| **EMERGENCY** | **Mid-face, off a converted worker cell | **Several | **She is **already gone** | ☠ **Fatal |

- **An EMPTY cup is not a decision** — colonies keep them standing all season.
- **An EGG or a larva in jelly IS. Clock: ~8–9 days.**

### The ladder — worst to best

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | **Cut the cells | **Buys time, changes nothing.** Miss one and they go; get them all after commitment and risk QUEENLESS** |
| **3** | **Give space / open the nest | **Works — only if EARLY |
| **2** | **Pull a brood comb to a weak hive | **Relieves congestion and boosts the weak one |
| **1** | ★★ **ARTIFICIAL SWARM — split them myself** | **The reliable answer.** Give them the division on my schedule, into my box |

### ★ Giving space — space in the wrong place is not space

- **Empty bars go AT THE EDGE OF THE BROOD NEST, not the far end.*** Bees will not cross a wall of sealed honey to find a building site.* **A half-empty box can still swarm from congestion.**
- ★★ **One empty bar pushed INTO the nest reads as UNFINISHED WORK** and competes with the urge to leave — ⚠ **strong colonies, warm weather only**, or the cluster is split and brood chills*
- ★★ **Space BEFORE they want it prevents swarming. Space AFTER the first charged cup does almost nothing.** Same act, opposite value, ten days apart

### ★★ The artificial swarm — the trick is the SITE

1. **NEW box on the OLD STAND.** Parent hive shifted aside
2. Queen + a comb of stores + empty bars into the new box
3. ★★ **Flying bees navigate to a PLACE, not a box** — the whole foraging force delivers itself to the queen. **That is exactly what a swarm is: old queen + fliers**
4. **Parent keeps brood, nurses and cells → raises a new queen. **Reduce to ONE good cell or it throws casts
5. Stripped of fliers, the parent has neither numbers nor appetite to swarm again

> **None of this is possible from a basket.** A skep colony that decides to swarm simply swarms and I watch half of it leave. **Every rung of this ladder exists because the combs come out.**

### ★★ Drones are a CALENDAR — and it reads two ways at once

| | |
|---|---|
| **At a strong hive — WARNING** | **Drones only get raised when a colony feels prosperous. **Drones flying = swarm season LIVE |
| **At a virgin's hive — HOPE** | **A virgin cannot mate without drones aloft.** No drones = no mating = the colony dies childless |

### ☠ ★★★ WAX MOTH *(d3274 — cost ~500 g)*

- ☠ ★★★ **THE LARVAE DO NOT WANT WAX. THEY WANT COCOONS** — *the shed larval skins, the silk and the pollen worked into old brood comb: the PROTEIN.*
- ★★★ **So they destroy exactly the comb that is mostly cocoon, and walk past clean pale comb, which is mostly wax and holds nothing they can digest.** ★★ *The same fact as "old brood comb is barely made of wax," read from the other end — two independent measurements agreeing.*
- ☠ ★★★ **AN EMPTY COMB IS NOT STORED. IT IS EXPOSED.** *Comb inside a live colony is patrolled every hour — the bees chew out the eggs and throw the larvae out.* **Drive the colony and the comb stops being defended and starts being food.**
- ☠ ★★★ **THE CLOCK IS TEMPERATURE, NOT DAYS.** *SKEP-1 rendered cool at d3265 = 850 g; SKEP-2 nine warmer days later = 190 g. A calendar trigger standing in for a state trigger.*

| Rule | |
|---|---|
| ★★★ **RENDER A DRIVEN SKEP IMMEDIATELY** | **At last brood hatch, not on a date. Waiting buys nothing — the wax is already made** |
| ★★★ **STORE COMB IN LIGHT AND DRAFT** | ☠ **The moth wants dark, still and warm — which is exactly what "put it safely in a box" provides.** *The storage that feels careless is the correct one* |
| ★★ **Bank PALE comb only** | **Dark comb is moth bait and renders poorly anyway — render it the day it comes out** |
| ★ **Cold kills them** | **A winter render is safe; a late-spring one is on a timer** |
| ★ **Recovery** | **Larvae → fish bait · matted slumgum → fire starter**
- ☠ ★★ **OLD BROOD COMB IS BARELY MADE OF WAX.** *Every larva spins a cocoon and leaves the skin behind; the bees varnish it with propolis and raise the next on top.* **A twenty-generation comb is a stack of shed skins with a thin wax scaffold in it** — fresh white comb is nearly all wax, honey comb is high, **old dark brood comb is a small fraction.**
- ★★ **So the grams-per-year was never only the skep being unopenable — it was that the comb I could scavenge was the OLDEST AND WORST in the hive.** ★ **On top bars I take HONEY comb and leave brood comb in service: I now render the RICH fraction, which is the exact opposite of what a skep forced on me.**
- **Slumgum = fire starter, and it is most of what goes in.**
