## `SEED-RAG-TEST` — two days before the gate

Count a fixed number of seeds onto a damp rag, keep it warm, count what sprouts.

**The same harvest held in two stores can differ by half.** A bank is not a number of grams, it is a number of grams times a germination rate that nobody has measured. Testing turns a guess into a sowing rate, and it is the only way to find a dead bank while there is still time to do something about it.

Test **every** bank going in, separately. Do not test one and infer the rest.

## `SOIL-READ` — gate day

Read the soil **at seed depth, morning and afternoon**. Not air temperature, and not the calendar.

The calendar gives the band; the soil decides the day inside it. A cold wet seedbed rots seed that would have been fine a week later, and the loss is invisible until nothing comes up.

Also read moisture by hand. Soil that smears is too wet to work — sowing into it compacts the bed and it stays compacted all season.

## `SEED-RESERVE` — before anything is drilled

**Hold a reserve from every elite bank. Never sow a selected line out.**

A line represents years of selection and one bad season ends it. The reserve is not a contingency, it is the line itself; what goes in the ground is the copy.

Check **bed area against the seed bank** at the same time. Area and seed are one plan — clearing ground does nothing if no seed is waiting for it, and a full bank does nothing without the beds.

## `SOW-DRILL`

- **Depth is about two to three times the seed's own diameter.** Deeper than that and a small seed exhausts itself before it reaches light; shallower and it dries out or gets taken.
- **Fava is dibbed individually and deep** — big seed, placed, never scattered.
- **Flax: dense for fibre, thin for seed.** A crowded stand grows tall and unbranched, which is exactly what fibre wants; a thin stand branches and sets seed. Where both are wanted, split the bed rather than compromise on one rate.
- Herbs go in last and are the item most likely to get dropped when the window tightens. Sow them first if the band looks crowded.

## `SOW-ROLL` — and the walk afterwards

Firm the bed. Seed needs contact with soil to draw water; seed sitting in an air pocket does nothing.

**Then walk the bed and look for seed sitting proud.** Hard and stony ground defeats a roller — it bridges over the hollows and leaves seed on the surface in exactly the places that needed pressing. Re-rake and hand-press those patches. This is a ten-minute job that recovers whole square metres.

## `BIRD-WATCH` — the first three days

**This is when birds decide whether the field is worth visiting.** A field that pays in the first three days gets visited for three weeks; one that does not is largely left alone. The effort is front-loaded for that reason.

- Rattle line over the beds from day one, not after damage appears.
- Brush soil over fine seed — flax especially. Covered seed is not a target.

## `BIRD-DEVICE-RESET` — until the crop roots, ~3 weeks

**Birds learn any single device in about four days.** That is the real interval and it is why "weekly" fails.

- Move the scarecrow, change its tunic, layer a second device alongside.
- They are fooled by **uncertainty**, not by shapes. A convincing scarecrow that never moves is furniture within the week; a crude one that keeps changing works all month.
- **The seed is the target, not the crop.** Once it has rooted, the threat is over and the devices come down — leaving them up is how the next sowing's devices get pre-learned.

Held as an interval check in [periodic.md](../../checklists/periodic.md), live only during the establishment window.

## After the sow

File one `CI-1` observation row per crop — drill tags verified, elite draw weights logged, rogue plan noted. A sow without a record is a sow that cannot be compared to last year's.
