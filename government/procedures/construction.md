# Building construction phases

**Companion to [BC-2](../regulations/building-code-2.md).** The code says *what is required*; this says *in what order, and what cannot be undone once passed.* They change together.

> ★★★ **THE SEQUENCE RULE: deepest and most permanent first. Every later trade must be able to do its work without breaking an earlier one's.**
>
> ★★ **The test to apply at every step: "what would I have to break to do this later?"** If the answer is a wall, a floor or a pour, then it is not a later item — **it is a now item.**

**⧗ marks a hold point** — a wait that cannot be compressed. These are schedule, not delay.

---

## Phase 0 — Stock and lead time

★★ **Starts a season before Phase I.** A build that begins by making its materials has already lost a year.

1. **Cast block and stack it to cure** — ⧗ **90 days to load-bearing.** Run the mould in the background between heroes; the yard should never be empty
2. Break the test blocks at 7, 28 and 90 days and log the results
3. Fell and season timber · burn lime · bank char · lay in sand, grit and pozzolan
4. **Forge rebar stock** — smelt, draw, **twist square bar** to [BC-2-REBAR](../regulations/building-code-2.md#rebar--wrought-iron-bc-2-rebar) · cut hooks · stage laps and chairs before the pour day
5. Cut or check the mould, the measuring box, the straightedge, the square and the water level

---

## Phase I — Site selection and surveying

1. Select the site. Size, stability, drainage, wind and fumes, access — and **BC-3 layout**: two routes in, nothing heavy uphill, nothing irreplaceable concentrated
2. ★★ **Stake it and live in it a season before laying stone.** Desire lines, standing water, where the shade falls at which hour
3. Survey. Layout, floorplan, drainage plan
4. ★★ **Decide the service routes now** — where water enters and leaves, where the drain runs and at what fall, where a duct would come from, where the loads will sit. **Every sleeve and chase in later phases is placed from this drawing, and anything not on it will not exist**
5. Measure and stake, to the water level

---

## Phase II — Earthwork and deep services

⚠ **Everything that goes under or beside the foundation happens before the foundation.**

1. Level and grade. ★ Set the deliberate cross-fall — nothing is truly flat
2. Cut the back-drain at the foot of any cut face
3. Dig the footprint and the footing trenches
4. **Lay the site drains and the main runs**, to fall, sized for the *final* paved area
5. **Set the deep sleeves** — every pipe and duct crossing the footing line, as oversized clay tube
6. Set the earth rod and leave its tail accessible
7. ⚠ **Record every buried run on the building's map entry before it is covered**

---

## Phase III — Foundation

1. **Dampen the subgrade.** Dry ground steals the mix water
2. Place forms, **wetted or tallow-rubbed**
3. Set rebar on chairs — ⚠ **cover under the bar, not just over it**
4. **Pour in segments you can honestly finish in one session**, with keyed and dowelled joints at every planned stop. ☠ A cold joint is a crack you built on purpose
5. ⧗ **Cure damp and shaded before loading**
6. Gravel fill
7. Lay the stem wall in block, carrying the vertical cores true
8. **Lay the damp-proof course** — ☠ **hard gate, nothing plasters above a wall without one**

---

## Phase IV — Masonry

1. **Lay the walls** — block dry, brick wetted — leaving door and window openings clear of corners
2. ★★ **As the wall rises, keep the service cores clear and the structural cores clean.** Mark each core's job on the wall. A wire in a column cell is lost; a column around a wire is not a column
3. Stand the rebar and grout the structural cells in lifts, rodding to consolidate. For brick, tooth the panel ends and cast the columns against them
4. Build in the chases, the sleeves and the wall penetrations
5. Cast the ring beam — ★ **a closed ring, tied into every column**
6. Build in hypocausts, hearths, chimneys and fixed stonework
7. Form and pour the surrounding pads, steps and portico, all pitched to drain

---

## Phase V — Roof

1. Lay beams, rafters and joists — **brace to triangles** (collar ties, wind braces) — ☠ **tied down to the ring beam at every heel**
2. Set portico posts and frames
3. Fix battens and flashing
4. **Lay continuous sheathing** — the seismic diaphragm
5. Lay finish — ★ **light first** per [BC-2-ROOF](../regulations/building-code-2.md#bc-2-roof--seismic); heavy tile only where the ring and ties are proven on this building
6. Fit vent covers and the ridge hood

> ★ **The building is now dry, and that is what the next phase has been waiting for.**

---

## Phase VI — Rough-in

★★ **The phase the draft was missing, and the one with no second chance.** The shell is up, the roof is on, nothing is closed. **This is the only cheap moment for the next hundred years.**

1. **Run conduit** — sweeps only, no sharp corners, no more than about two bends between access points
2. ☠ ★★ **Leave a flax draw-cord in every empty conduit, tied off at both ends and logged.** *The single highest-value act in the whole build*
3. Set the boxes and niches — over each bench, beside each door, centre of the ceiling — and the panel niche with the earth tail
4. **Set the duct chase.** ⚠ Generous. Hand-spans, not finger-widths, and it can never be enlarged
5. Set the branch drain sleeves, the trap positions and the vent route
6. ⚠ **Check the separations:** water, wire and air on their own routes, and where they must cross, **water below wire**
7. ★★ **Walk the drawing against the wall and sign it off before anything closes.** Every later phase makes this more expensive, and Phase IX makes it permanent

---

## Phase VII — Openings

1. Build doors and window frames
2. Install
3. Seal — felt and wool in the groove, pitch bead at sill and head
4. Glaze

---

## Phase VIII — Plaster and interior finish

⚠ **Corrected from the draft: plaster comes BEFORE the finish floor**, not after. Plaster is wet, heavy and drops everywhere, and a finished oak floor laid underneath it is a floor you will refinish.

1. Point and grout the exterior while the scaffold is still standing
2. Exterior render — **lime, no exceptions**
3. Damp the wall. **Scratch coat**, pressed hard into the joints and scratched while green
4. ⧗ Let it set — **set is not dry**, and never re-temper
5. **Scrim the junctions** — panel to column above all, plus corners, lath, and **every chase just filled**
6. **Float coat**, ruled off against grounds. ★ This is the coat that makes the wall flat
7. **Finish coat** — gypsum inside only
8. Limewash or paint

---

## Phase IX — Floors

1. Lay and grout tile
2. Lay plank over the prepared bed
3. ★ Sand and treat — planed and sanded before install, treated after
4. Tile the counters, splashbacks and wet surfaces

---

## Phase X — Fixtures and built-ins

1. Build cabinets, shelves and built-ins
2. Moulding, baseboards, panelling
3. **Fit any service that is actually going in now** — a sink, a trap, a run of pipe through its waiting sleeve
4. ★ Sand and treat every exposed face

---

## Phase XI — Linens

1. Weave rugs, curtains and awnings
2. Build awning frames and curtain rods
3. Install awnings, hang curtains, place rugs

---

## Season and weather

| | |
|---|---|
| ☠ **Never pour or render in frost** | It freezes before it cures and never recovers |
| ⚠ **Never cure in blazing sun or wind** | Shade it, damp it, cover it. **It cures, it does not dry** |
| ★ **Best bands** | **Spring and autumn for wet trades.** Reserve deep summer for roof and dry work |
| ★ **The roof is the pivot** | **Get to Phase V before the wet season** and everything after it can proceed indoors through winter |
